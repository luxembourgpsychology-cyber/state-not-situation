# 04. Reply guidelines: answering comments

Item: 04, reply guidelines
Channel: LinkedIn and Instagram comments and direct messages
To whom: the author, for her own use when replying
Suggested send date: none; in force from the first post
What the recipient is asked to do: read once before the first post goes out
Attachments or links: https://statenotsituation.com/en/research
Approval status: awaiting Ivana

## The principle

The book's own lines do the work. Replies quote them or point to them. They add nothing the book does not say.

## Comments about the book, the cover, the launch

Thank the person in one line and answer the question if there is one. If they ask where to buy, give the site: the stores and prices vary by country, so the site is the stable answer. If they ask about French or German, the site says "French and German editions to follow"; no edition exists yet, and no date is given. If they ask about events, none are scheduled; the events page says "Public dates will be announced here." If they ask about the app, say only what the site says: it is being developed, and "A release date and access details have not yet been announced."

## Comments describing a person's own symptoms, state or situation

These will come, because the posts invite people to check their jaw and their breath. The site and the book have three lines for exactly this, and they are the whole of the answer: the research page says two of them, and page 238 says the third.

The research page: "A person’s state can influence an interpretation while a real concern remains. This framework offers questions, not a diagnosis or a substitute for professional support."

Page 238: "What belongs to the situation, and what might state be adding?"

So a reply to someone describing what they feel does three things and no more. It does not interpret what they wrote. It offers the book's question, not an answer to their case. And where the person describes distress, risk, or something that has gone on for a while, it says plainly that this is a matter for a professional who can see them, their doctor or a psychologist where they live, and that a comment thread is not that. Write this in your own plain words each time; do not keep a stock phrase, and do not use any of the phrases that circulate online for sounding caring. One honest sentence is enough.

Do not offer to see the person in your practice from a public comment, and do not diagnose from one. If someone asks for advice about another person, the book's line is the same: "Is this the situation? Or is this their state?", and the answer is still a question, not a verdict.

## Requests for review copies, interviews, talks

Give the address: ivana@luxembourgpsychology.com, and the press page or /en/invite. Do not agree terms in a comment.

## What not to do

No claims the book does not make: not "evidence-based", not "proven", not a tool for anxiety or a mental health guide. No count of references. No reviews quoted, because there are none. No argument with a critical comment; one factual correction if a fact is wrong, otherwise let it stand. No dashes, no exclamation marks, no emojis, even when the other person uses them.

Notes for Ivana: the hardest replies will be the ones where someone is plainly unwell. The guideline above asks you to send them to a professional in one honest sentence. If you want a form of words you can reuse, write it yourself once, so it is yours; the agency should not supply it.
