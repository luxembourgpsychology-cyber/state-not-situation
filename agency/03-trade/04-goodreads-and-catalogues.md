```
The first misreading

You may not be reacting to the world. You may be reacting to your state.

A short message feels cold. A meeting feels dangerous. A craving feels like a decision. A quiet room feels like judgement.

We often mistake a state for a fact. A person seems hostile. A task feels impossible. A relationship appears wrong. A whole day looks lost.

But what feels true is not produced by the situation alone. Sleep, hunger, stress, timing, attention, memory and threat detection affect what we notice and how we interpret it. Together, they shape the explanation that feels most convincing.

State. Not Situation. examines the gap between what happened and what it felt like it meant.

Check the signal. Before you believe the story.

Ivana Budišin trained first to look, then to measure. With a background in design and later in clinical psychology, she is interested in what happens between an event and the certainty we build around it.
```
