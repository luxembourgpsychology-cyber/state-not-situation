# Launch plan, 16 October to 27 November 2026

Written by the Agency Director on 16 October 2026, the day after publication. It ranks the work by what can actually reach readers, given two facts measured today: the MailerLite list has two subscribers, and the site has had 99 visitors since 1 October, seven of them on publication day and the day after. The audience does not exist yet. It has to be borrowed from people and institutions that already have one.

## The order of priority, and why

First, the things that are promised or already late. The publication email to the two subscribers, because the site promised it. The Amazon description, Author Central biography and Goodreads record, because a journalist or a bookseller who checks will find the listing before anything else. The KDP distribution questions, because no bookshop letter can be answered without them.

Second, Luxembourg. A Luxembourg clinical psychologist's first book, with legal deposit at the national library, is a local story in a country with a dense press and few English language books by residents. The Luxembourg press pitches, the four bookshops, the two libraries and the Walfer Bicherdeeg letter go in the first week. The French and German versions of the release and the pitches follow within days, because the Wort, Tageblatt, L'essentiel and Le Quotidien newsrooms work in those languages.

Third, the author's own network, which is the only audience that exists today. The LinkedIn posts and the personal emails. These cost nothing, and they are where the first readers and the first honest reviews come from.

Fourth, the talks. Four approved offers exist and nobody has been told. The AIHE follow up, the Institut Pierre Werner and neimënster, POG and the two chambers of commerce, then the university. A booked talk produces an event for /en/events, a reason for a second press round, and sales at the door.

Fifth, the wider English language world: podcasts, review outlets, extract licensing, awards. These are slower and lower probability; they go out in weeks two to four and are judged over months.

## The calendar

Week 1, 16 to 23 October. Confirm the price. Approve the publication email and send it. Fill the Amazon and Goodreads records. Answer the twelve KDP questions. Approve and send the press release and the ten Luxembourg pitches, the four bookshop letters, the two library letters, the Walfer Bicherdeeg letter, the AIHE letter. Post LinkedIn 1 and 2. Send the two personal emails.

Week 2, 26 to 30 October. French and German releases and pitches to the newsrooms not yet answered. Radio and television pitches. Venue letters (IPW, neimënster, Cercle Cité, Rotondes). HR and chamber letters. The international extract pitches. First podcast pitches. Follow up on week 1 pitches after seven days.

Week 3, 2 to 6 November. University and SLP letters. Remaining venue letters. Review outlet pitches. School and community group letters. Continue LinkedIn and Instagram on the calendar.

Week 4 to 6, 9 to 27 November. Follow ups. Awards decisions by 30 November. Any booked event announced on /en/events and pushed to the press that covered the launch. Walfer Bicherdeeg, 21 and 22 November, attended if a place was found.

## How to judge it

By 6 November: at least one Luxembourg outlet has replied, at least one bookshop has agreed to stock or host, and the Amazon paperback is Amazon's own offer for a Luxembourg address. If no outlet has replied by then, the angle changes before more pitches go, not the volume. The candidates for a new angle are the workplace material, which Paperjam and the HR networks can use, and a booked event, which gives a newsroom a date.

## The strongest counterargument to this plan

That it spends the first month on Luxembourg, a market of 650,000 people, for an English language book that could travel. The answer is that the book has no reviews, no endorsements and no audience, and a first wave of local coverage and a few events are the cheapest way to get the first of each. The test is the 6 November check above. If Luxembourg produces nothing, the second month goes to the English language podcasts and review outlets with whatever the first month did produce.

## What is not in this plan

Paid advertising. A publicist's fee. A launch party. Any claim about the book that the book does not make. Each is a decision for Ivana and none is needed for the first six weeks.
