# State. Not Situation. — final report on the first campaign

16 to 30 October 2026. Written for Ivana at her request on 30 October, to close this phase before the campaign is rebooted.

## 1. Where things stand

The book is out, stocked in a Luxembourg bookshop, deposited at the National Library, and has a launch evening with a host and its first reservation. Sales are still small, and the audience is still small.

| Measure | Figure |
|---|---|
| Copies sold through Amazon (Ivana's count, 26 October) | 3 |
| Copies placed with Librairie Alinéa | 10 reordered on 27 October, delivered the same day |
| Visitors to statenotsituation.com, 15 to 29 October | 71 (107 page views) |
| Best single day since launch week | 28 October, 18 visitors, the day the launch evening went live |
| Newsletter subscribers | 2 (one real reader, one unconfirmed); the publication email was never sent |
| Reservations for 3 December | 2 places (Laura De Robertis, English teacher) |

## 2. What worked

- **Alinéa.** It said no to hosting the launch but stocks the book and reordered within days. That is the first sign of real demand in a shop.
- **Warm people, not cold lists.** The replies all came from people who already knew Ivana: Laura Bannier at Virgule, Lucrezia Reale at the Luxembourg Times, Geoff Thompson at Chronicle.lu, Tamara Sondag at the Esch library. Copies went to Laura and Lucrezia. The sixteen people who never replied to her September emails were not chased.
- **One concrete event.** The launch evening on Thursday 3 December, 18:00, at Cereal Lovers, in dialogue with the journalist Jessika Maria Rauch, gave everything else a reason to exist. It went live on the front page with a one-click email reservation, and the first reservation arrived the next day.
- **The National Library.** The paperback and the ebook are deposited, which puts the book in the national catalogue. The Esch library asked for the publisher to consider it for its collection.
- **The site.** It now shows Alinéa as a place to buy, the launch evening, and Jessika's name, with nothing on it the book does not say about itself.

## 3. What did not work

- **The first plan was too much.** It read as a jumble and too sharp for Luxembourg. It was withdrawn on 17 October and rebuilt as a few crafted pieces.
- **The approval box in Notion did not flow.** Cards sat for a week. Several asked Ivana to decide things the team could have decided. She said so on 27 October, and from then on the team decided routine things itself.
- **Social posting did not reach new people.** Videos and posts were seen by people who already follow Ivana. Nothing connected a viewer to a purchase or to the newsletter.
- **The newsletter was never sent,** so every visitor who might have joined had nothing to join.

## 4. The friction, recorded as Ivana said it

> "I was very annoyed with this never flowing… I had to click 35 ALLOW ONCE to get one simple email."

This is fair, and it is the team's failure, not hers. The cause was mechanical. In this kind of session Claude Code asks permission before each use of a tool it has not been told it may use: each Notion update, each Gmail draft, each calendar entry. A task like "write to Alinéa" touched four or five tools, several times each, and every touch was a separate "Allow once".

What was done about it: on 27 and 28 October, allow rules were added for Notion, Calendar, Gmail reading and drafts, the newsletter and traffic checks, and file edits. They are saved in this session and in the repository's `.claude/settings.json` on the agency branch. Sending an email still asks, on purpose.

What will make it flow next time:
1. **Start each session in Auto mode**, from the mode menu next to the prompt box. It removes most remaining prompts.
2. **Merge `.claude/settings.json` to main**, so every future session reads the allow rules, not only sessions on the agency branch. The file affects only Claude Code, not the website.
3. **Fewer surfaces.** The next campaign should use Gmail drafts and one Notion page, not a database of cards. Every card was another permission and another click.

## 5. New ideas for promotion and outreach

Ranked by how likely each is to sell copies per hour of Ivana's time. Each has its strongest objection and a cheap test.

**1. Teachers, for professional development.** Laura De Robertis asked for a certificate of attendance on her first email. Teachers need training hours, and the book is about attention and stress, which is every teacher's day. Offer a 90-minute session for teachers, with a certificate, to the international and European schools and to teachers' associations in Luxembourg. Each session can carry a bulk order of books.
- Objection: official recognition of training hours in Luxembourg runs through the national training institute (IFEN), and getting a session on its list takes time. Citation needed: check IFEN's process for external trainers.
- Test: ask Laura which body recognises her hours, and whether two colleagues would come to a session in January.

**2. Christmas gift guides.** Luxembourg outlets publish book and gift lists in late November. A psychology book by a local author, sold at Alinéa, fits a "Luxembourg authors" or "books for the people you love" list.
- Objection: guides are planned early, so this must go out in the first half of November.
- Test: one short email each to Laura Bannier (Virgule) and Lucrezia Reale, who already have the book, asking whether their December lists take local authors.

**3. Book clubs in English.** Luxembourg has many English-language reading groups through the expat clubs. Offer a club kit: five discussion questions, and Ivana joining one session in person or by video when the group reads the book.
- Objection: clubs choose months ahead, and many prefer fiction.
- Test: offer it to the American Women's Club, where Martina Schweiher already wants a talk, and see whether one group picks it for spring.

**4. Workplaces.** Firms in Luxembourg run lunchtime talks on stress and wellbeing. The site's "Invite Ivana" page already exists. One talk can sell twenty copies as a company order, and it is paid work.
- Objection: corporate buyers move slowly and want references.
- Test: ask two people Ivana knows in HR or people teams whether their firm runs such talks and who books them.

**5. Reviews before ads.** The Amazon page has no reviews, so ads would buy clicks that do not buy books. At the launch evening, put a small card in each copy sold, asking for one honest sentence on Amazon. Ask the three first buyers personally.
- Objection: asking for reviews can feel pushy in Luxembourg.
- Test: ask the three first buyers by a personal message, and count reviews after two weeks.

**6. The café shelf.** Cereal Lovers is Ivana's own café. Keep a few signed copies by the till, with a small card and a code to read the first pages free.
- Objection: a café is not a bookshop, and a book by the till can look like clutter.
- Test: one month, one small stand, and count copies sold.

**7. Send the newsletter, then use it.** The list is tiny because it was never used. Send the launch invitation in the week of 9 November. After that, write one short note a month in Ivana's own voice, never a sales email.
- Objection: two subscribers make it feel pointless.
- Test: put the sign-up link in every story and post until 3 December, and count subscribers before and after.

**What to stop:** cold emails to people who did not answer, three posts a week, and paid ads before there are reviews.
