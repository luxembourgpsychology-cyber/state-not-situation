## Ten likely questions and the material behind each

### 1. What is the book about, in a sentence?

Back cover headline: "You may not be reacting to the world. You may be reacting to your state."
Back cover, last paragraph: "State. Not Situation. examines the gap between what happened and what it felt like it meant."
Your own line, on the site: "It is less interested in teaching you to trust your instincts than in showing you what, exactly, you are trusting."
Front cover line: "Your body speaks first, your mind explains second."

### 2. Why open with a plane crash?

Pages 1 to 3, the flight of John F. Kennedy Jr., 16 July 1999. The passage on the inner ear ("if the turn holds steady for fifteen or twenty seconds, the fluid in the inner ear settles"), the instruments ("The data was right there, on the panel, six inches from his eyes, the whole time, but he did not read it. He read his body instead."), and the FAA's one sentence instruction. Full text in content/en.ts under excerpt.paragraphs and at https://statenotsituation.com/en/read.
Page 3, the turn to the reader: "You operate a body that produces signals and your mind often treats those signals as truth." "But the body's confident weather report about the world is only a draft."
Page 20, the scale change: "This book is about the same error at kitchen scale. The version that happens every Tuesday."

### 3. What are Time, Attention and Safety?

Page 7: "The chapters that follow are organised around three systems that shape how your body's signals get weighted before your mind builds a story." And: "They are not brain regions or neural pathways. They are a sorting tool, a way to ask three questions when everything feels wrong at once."
Page 9, the three questions, word for word. Time: "What came before this moment? Sleep, meals, caffeine, recovery, the hour." Attention: "What keeps pulling me back? A message, a thought, a cue, an unfinished task." Safety: "What seems at stake? What have I observed, what am I assuming, and does anything need action?"

### 4. Can you give an everyday example?

Page 4, "The same day, five readings": 06:38 "A heaviness arrives before the day does." 09:12 "A two-line email reads like a judgement." 14:23 "Five neutral words tighten a jaw." 17:45 "An unanswered message starts charging cognitive rent." 22:47 "Two letters and a full stop feel hostile." Then: "The body speaks first. The mind explains second."
Back cover, four lines: "A short message feels cold. A meeting feels dangerous. A craving feels like a decision. A quiet room feels like judgement."
Page 24, Misattribution: "Giving a feeling the wrong cause. The body produces the feeling. The mind looks for a reason and takes the nearest one. The feeling is real. The reason is a guess."

### 5. What should a reader actually do?

Page 7, the first exercise: "Try something right now. Whatever you are feeling as you read this sentence. Check your jaw. Check your breath. Check your shoulders. What you found is a reading."
Page 24, Try it: "Before acting on a confident judgement, name one fact that could change your mind."
Page 238: "Is this the situation? Or is this their state?" and "What belongs to the situation, and what might state be adding?"
Back cover sign off: "Check the signal. Before you believe the story."

### 6. How solid is the science?

Page 7: evidence labels High, Medium, Low, and "These markers exist because the book's own risk is the same risk it describes."
Your descriptions of the markers: High, "Replicated or robust evidence." Medium, "Suggestive evidence with meaningful uncertainty." Low, "Plausible hypothesis or emerging evidence."
Page 242, The Scientific Heartbeat: "Each chapter has three parts here: a short note on where the thinking came from, a list of the work the chapter is built on, and a list of the work that limits it, complicates it, or explains the same evidence differently." "Leaving them out would make the argument look tidier than it is."
Research page: "Evidence for a related mechanism does not automatically prove that a particular exercise works."
Dr Herber, foreword, as quoted on the site: "A book that cautions us against confusing a persuasive interpretation with certainty also applies scrutiny to its own interpretations."

### 7. Is the feeling always wrong, then?

## Three questions to decline or redirect

### A. "So this is a method for anxiety, or a treatment?"

Decline the frame. The book is a psychology trade book, not a treatment. Redirect to the Research page: "This framework offers questions, not a diagnosis or a substitute for professional support." The invite page says of the workshop: "It is not group therapy." and of the seminar: "The framework and its exercises are not presented as a validated treatment." Do not use "tool for anxiety", "mental health guide", "evidence-based method" or "proven".

### B. "How many studies is it based on?" or any request for a number

Decline the number. The book states no count of references, no DOI verification and no "years of research", and none may be invented in conversation. Redirect to what the book does state: the evidence markers on page 7 and your three descriptions of them, and page 242 on the work "that limits it, complicates it, or explains the same evidence differently."

### C. "When is the French or German edition out?" or "Will there be an audiobook?"

Do not give a date, a publisher or a translator. The site says "French and German editions to follow" and nothing else is fixed. No audiobook exists and none is announced. Redirect to the English edition, the extract at https://statenotsituation.com/en/read, and the newsletter form "Book updates" on the site for anyone who wants to hear when something changes.
