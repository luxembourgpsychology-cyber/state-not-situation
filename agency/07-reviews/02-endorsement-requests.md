# 02 Endorsement requests

Prepared 16 October 2026 by the Reviews, Rights and Endorsements desk. Drafts for Ivana to approve. She signs as "I".

The book carries no endorsements and none exist. Nothing in these letters implies otherwise. The agency does not nominate recipients: every name is [NAME, Ivana to supply]. Under professional ethics, no current or former client is to be asked, and no one in a dependent relationship to Ivana (supervisee, employee, student she assesses).

What is honestly asked: a reading of the book; a sentence or two in the recipient's own words, if they wish; no obligation to write anything; nothing quoted anywhere without their written sign off on the exact wording and the exact placement.

---

## Item 2.1 Request to a fellow psychologist or academic

Channel: email, one recipient at a time, never a group send
To whom: [NAME, Ivana to supply], [role and institution, Ivana to supply], [address, Ivana to supply]
Suggested send date: from 20 October 2026, as Ivana chooses
What the recipient is asked to do: accept a copy, read it if they wish, and consider writing a short comment in their own words for the website or a later printing, with no obligation
Attachments or links: https://statenotsituation.com/en/read ; offer of print or digital copy
Approval status: awaiting Ivana

Subject: Would you read my first book?

Dear [NAME, Ivana to supply],

My first book, State. Not Situation., was published on 15 October 2026. I would like to send you a copy, print or digital as you prefer, and I want to be plain about why.

The book has no endorsements. I would rather it carried a few honest sentences from people whose reading I respect than many from people I do not know. If, after reading it, you wanted to write a line or two about it in your own words, I would be glad to consider using it on the website, and perhaps in a later printing. If you would rather not, or if you read it and do not think well of it, that is entirely fine and I would still be glad you read it. There is no obligation of any kind, and I will not ask twice.

Nothing you write would be quoted anywhere without your written agreement to the exact wording and to where it appears. You could also withdraw it later.

So you know what you would be reading: the subtitle is Why your body decides what a moment means before you do. The book examines the gap between what happened and what it felt like it meant. It is less interested in teaching you to trust your instincts than in showing you what, exactly, you are trusting. A section at the back of the book, The Scientific Heartbeat, page 242, says: "Each chapter has three parts here: a short note on where the thinking came from, a list of the work the chapter is built on, and a list of the work that limits it, complicates it, or explains the same evidence differently." The opening pages are at statenotsituation.com/en/read.

If you would like a copy, tell me which format and, for print, a postal address.

With warm regards,
Ivana Budišin
Clinical psychologist, Luxembourg
ivana@luxembourgpsychology.com

Notes for Ivana: send only to people you would be comfortable receiving a critical reply from. Keep a simple record of who received a copy and what, if anything, they agreed to, with the date of their written sign off. Strongest reason not to send: a request of this kind trades on goodwill, and a recipient who feels obliged may write something warmer than they mean, which is the opposite of what the book stands for.

## Item 2.2 Request to a reader who has already written to Ivana about the book

Channel: reply to their email
To whom: [NAME, Ivana to supply], a reader who wrote unprompted; [address, Ivana to supply]. Not a client, not a former client.
Suggested send date: within a week of their message
What the recipient is asked to do: agree, or decline, to have a sentence they already wrote quoted on the website, with their name or without it
Attachments or links: none
Approval status: awaiting Ivana

Subject: Re: [their subject line]

Dear [NAME, Ivana to supply],

Thank you for writing. I read your message more than once.

May I ask something you are free to refuse? The book has no reader comments on its website yet. You wrote: "[THE SENTENCE OR TWO, copied exactly from their message, Ivana to paste]". I would like to quote that, exactly as you wrote it, on statenotsituation.com, either with your first name and city or with no name at all, whichever you prefer. If you would rather it stayed between us, say so and I will not mention it again.

Nothing goes up without your written yes to the exact words and the exact placement, and you can ask me to take it down at any time.

If you also wanted to leave an honest review on Amazon or Goodreads, that would help other readers find the book. Please only do so if you bought or borrowed it in the ordinary way and if we do not know each other personally, since both sites remove reviews from friends and family. I am not asking for a particular rating, only for your honest view.

With thanks,
Ivana Budišin

Notes for Ivana: do not edit the reader's words, not even punctuation, unless they agree the edit in writing. If the reader is, or ever was, a client, do not send this; thank them and stop there. Strongest reason not to send: a reader who wrote privately may feel their letter was a transaction, and some would rather not be asked.

## Record keeping

For every comment used, keep: the sender's name, the date of their message, the exact words, the date and text of their written sign off, and where the words appear. Add each used sentence to CONTENT_SOURCES.md as the author's own supplied material, with the sign off date, before it goes on the site. Until a sentence is signed off it is not to appear anywhere, including in drafts shared outside the agency.
