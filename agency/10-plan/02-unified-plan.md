## Why these film captions

Film 06, Sometimes the alarm is right. The film says that knowing your state does not cancel a real problem. The book's printed form of that thought is the Safety question on page 9 and the title of chapter 13. The research page's own line, "A person’s state can influence an interpretation while a real concern remains", says the same thing and is approved site text; it can replace the chapter 13 sentence if you prefer, but the caption would then be built from the site rather than the page.

Film 05, An estimate. The film is about a sleep score. The fact sheet records nothing printed about sleep trackers, so the caption makes no claim about them: it quotes the Time question on page 9, which puts sleep first, and names chapter 06. What the film says about scores and worry is said in your own voice in the film and stays there.

Film 01, The argument in your head. Rumination is a printed name on page 292, and the 17:45 reading on page 4 is the printed scene closest to a conversation rehearsed in the head. The caption quotes both and adds nothing.

None of the three could be captioned with its own title as the hook, because none of the titles is a printed line. All three could be captioned from printed lines. Film 07 is the only hold, because it does not yet exist as a file.

## The rule for your week

Monday morning, before 12:30, you receive one email from the agency with the week's three posts in full: the words to paste, the file to attach, the time. You reply approve, change or strike, in a line. Then you post them yourself from your phone at the three times, Instagram first and LinkedIn second where a row names both. Nothing else: no boosts, no Facebook, no Stories unless you want one, no results spreadsheet. If a week is too full, strike the Wednesday row; the Monday and Friday rows stand on their own.
