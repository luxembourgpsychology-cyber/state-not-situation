# The redesign brief, from the author, 6 September 2026

Verbatim. This is the governing document for the redesign. Where it disagrees with any
earlier decision (`translation/EDITORIAL-POSITIONING.md`, `translation/HOOK-DECISION.md`,
`CONTENT_SOURCES.md`, `DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`), it wins, and the earlier file is amended to match.

---

You are acting as a senior multidisciplinary publishing team redesigning the website for the forthcoming psychology trade book State. Not Situation. by Ivana Budišin.

Website: www.statenotsituation.com

Your job is not to make the existing site prettier.

Your job is to turn it into a highly credible, restrained, internationally publishable book website that could comfortably sit beside work from a serious trade publisher, independent literary press, literary agency, or respected nonfiction author.

The finished website should feel authored, editorially controlled, intelligent, calm, expensive, and unmistakably human.

The current site already contains strong material. The primary problem is excess rather than absence.

Do not add complexity merely because the technology allows it.

The central design principle is:

Confidence through subtraction.

## 1. Work as a coordinated senior team

Approach the project through the following roles.

**Editorial Director** — information hierarchy; deciding what belongs on the homepage; removing repetition; determining which ideas deserve emphasis; ensuring the website does not explain the book more than necessary; maintaining the distinction between the book, the website, the press material, and promotional material.

**Creative Director** — visual hierarchy; typography; spacing; book presentation; restraint; rhythm; interaction design; ensuring the website feels editorial rather than technological.

**Publishing Marketing Director** — positioning the book; immediate category clarity; credibility; press usability; extract discovery; publication notification; keeping sales language restrained.

**Senior UX Designer** — navigation; mobile experience; reading flow; CTA placement; language switching; accessibility; reducing cognitive load.

**Senior Frontend Designer and Engineer** — implementation; responsive layout; animation discipline; performance; component architecture; semantic markup; accessibility; SEO; avoiding unnecessary technical complexity.

**English Editorial Copy Editor** — preserving Ivana's voice; reducing repetition; eliminating marketing language; removing sentences that explain ideas already communicated visually; maintaining trade nonfiction quality.

**French Literary Localization Editor** — French as if the book and site had originally been written for an educated Francophone trade readership. Do not translate sentence by sentence.

**German Literary Localization Editor** — German as if the book and site had originally been written for an educated German language trade readership. Pay particular attention to lexical literalism, translated syntax, nominal constructions, and words that are technically correct but culturally or stylistically wrong.

**Final Quality Controller** — comparing English, French, and German page by page before publication. The three versions must feel like the same publishing product.

## 2. First inspect the current website

Before changing code, inspect the entire live website in all available languages.

Review at minimum: English homepage; French homepage; German homepage; press page; book extract; author information; navigation; footer; mobile layout; publication signup; language switching.

Do not assume that the English, French, and German versions currently share identical architecture.

Map every section before editing.

Create internally: 1. current architecture; 2. proposed architecture; 3. content to retain; 4. content to shorten; 5. content to relocate; 6. content to delete.

Do not preserve content simply because effort has already been spent creating it.

## 3. Overall positioning

The desired impression is: A serious forthcoming psychology trade book with unusually strong intellectual and visual identity.

It must not feel like: a self help funnel; a startup; a SaaS product; an online course; a personal branding website; a therapy practice; a design agency portfolio; an AI generated concept site; an interactive science museum; a pitch deck; a book trailer stretched into a website.

The visitor should think: This is a real book with a real argument. Then: I understand the argument enough to want to read it.

Not: The author is trying very hard to convince me that the book is important.

## 4. Core editorial idea

The book examines the moment when internal state becomes interpretation.

The recurring conceptual framework includes: Time. Attention. Safety.

The book asks whether what feels like evidence about the world may sometimes be evidence about the state of the instrument doing the reading.

The website should support this idea without repeatedly explaining it.

A crucial line is: **The body is a sensor before it is a narrator.**

Another key thought is: **Same life. Different instrument settings.**

Use strong lines sparingly. A sentence loses power when repeated excessively.

## 5. Primary design principle

Reduce the current homepage substantially. Target approximately 25 to 35 percent less visible content. The exact percentage is not important. The perceptual effect is.

The redesigned site should have: fewer competing sections; fewer visual metaphors; more negative space; stronger typography; larger editorial moments; shorter explanations; fewer cards; fewer boxes; fewer decorative interface components; more confidence.

When uncertain whether to add something, default to omission.

## 6. Preserve the strongest existing identity

Do not throw away the visual identity. Preserve and refine: warm cream background; black typography; restrained muted red accent; existing book cover; instrument concept; timestamp language; scientific evidence hierarchy; editorial seriousness; slightly clinical undertone; quiet European sensibility.

Do not transform the site into conventional luxury beige branding. Do not make it feminine, wellness oriented, inspirational, therapeutic, cute, glossy, or lifestyle driven. Do not make it sterile either.

The tension should remain: human experience observed through scientific instruments.

## 7. Restrict the visual vocabulary

The current conceptual universe contains many visual devices. Do not use all of them equally.

Choose three primary recurring motifs:

**Instrument reading** — used for state measurements and scientific framing.
**Timestamp** — used for moments, cases, and temporal context.
**Heartbeat or signal** — used sparingly as a biological or measurement motif.

Everything else should primarily be handled through typography, spacing, photography, book imagery, and editorial composition.

Avoid visual metaphor inflation. One strong visual language repeated consistently is more sophisticated than seven clever visual ideas.

## 8. Canonical homepage architecture

Create one canonical architecture. English, French, and German must follow the same structural sequence. The words can be localized freely. The editorial experience should remain equivalent.

Recommended homepage:

**Section 1. Hero.** Large presentation of the book. The cover should be treated as the central visual object. Include: State. Not Situation. Subtitle: A field guide to the moment before interpretation becomes reality. Supporting line: Your first reading is not the whole story. Primary actions: Read an extract; Publication updates. No more than two primary actions. Avoid clutter around the cover. Allow substantial empty space. The cover should feel valuable enough to stand alone.

**Section 2. The Premise.** Introduce the mechanism in approximately 70 to 120 words. Potential opening: The body is a sensor before it is a narrator. Explain the premise without summarizing the entire book. The reader only needs enough information to understand the problem.

**Section 3. Time. Attention. Safety.** This is a signature moment. Use the three words with unusually strong typographic presence. They may appear very large. Each receives a very short explanation. Do not turn them into ordinary feature cards. The section should feel almost like an editorial spread.

**Section 4. Three Moments.** Use only three representative case moments on the homepage. Possible examples: 06:38 Something is off. 22:47 ok. 16:00 Something is wrong with my life. Each should demonstrate how the same mind can read experience differently depending on internal state. Do not explain each case excessively. The purpose is curiosity. Additional cases can live elsewhere.

**Section 5. Evidence.** Present the book's scientific evidence system. For example: HIGH Replicated or robust evidence. MEDIUM Suggestive evidence with meaningful uncertainty. LOW Plausible hypothesis or emerging evidence. This section should look sober and almost academic. Avoid gamification.

**Section 6. Extract.** Use one excellent excerpt. Do not place the entire pilot chapter on the homepage. Show enough to demonstrate the writing. Then: Continue reading. This should lead to the dedicated extract page.

**Section 7. Author.** Author image. Very short biography. Approximately three to five lines. Include relevant academic and professional credibility without turning this into a CV. Provide a clear link to press information.

**Section 8. Closing.** End quietly. Possible closing line: Same life. Different instrument settings. Then publication notification or relevant final CTA. Do not repeat all earlier claims. The end should feel like the end of a book jacket, not another sales section.

## 9. Homepage content that should probably move elsewhere

Relocate rather than automatically delete good material. Candidates include: extended pilot material; detailed chapter map; longer scientific explanations; multiple cases beyond the strongest three; detailed author credentials; extensive methodological notes; press details; review copy information; licensing information; downloadable assets.

These belong on dedicated pages where appropriate. The homepage should intrigue and orient. It should not function as the complete archive.

## 10. Press page

The existing press page is one of the strongest parts of the website. Preserve its seriousness.

It should provide: title; author; publication information; ISBN; format; book description; author biography; high resolution cover; author photography; review copy information; extract licensing information; contact details; legal deposit information where relevant.

Add a single clear option: Download complete press kit.

The downloadable press kit should contain clean, professionally named files. Do not overdesign the press area. Journalists want information quickly.

## 11. The publisher imprint

Budisin Publishing should not compete visually with Ivana Budišin or the book title on the homepage. Treat the imprint professionally but quietly. Prefer placement in: footer; copyright information; metadata; press page; publication details.

Avoid visually pairing Ivana Budišin and Budisin Publishing in a way that immediately foregrounds the relationship between author and imprint. The website should lead with the book and author.

## 12. English editorial rules

English is the canonical source language. Keep it clean, compressed, and confident.

Prefer: concrete nouns; active verbs; short paragraphs; precise psychological language; mechanistic explanations; observations rather than motivational conclusions.

Avoid: corporate marketing; startup vocabulary; pseudo profound copy; inspirational language; generic neuroscience claims; excessive rhetorical questions; repeated three part slogans; claims that sound stronger than the evidence; self congratulatory statements about originality.

Do not make the prose more polished at the expense of personality. Some friction is desirable. The writing should feel written.

## 13. French localization standard

French must be treated as an independent editorial edition. Do not perform literal translation. The goal is natural contemporary French appropriate for serious psychology trade nonfiction. Avoid English syntax hidden inside correct French.

Specific principles: prioritize idiomatic French over lexical equivalence; restructure sentences when necessary; avoid unnatural repetition of nouns; avoid direct translations of English metaphors when they do not survive; avoid excessive nominalization; allow French sentence rhythm to differ from English; preserve conceptual precision.

Example — Avoid: Ce livre est l'enquête. Prefer: Ce livre mène l'enquête.

Avoid constructions similar to: Vous devriez savoir lesquelles sont lesquelles. Prefer natural French such as: Le lecteur doit pouvoir les distinguer.

Avoid: Différez l'histoire. Possible alternatives: Suspendez l'histoire. Or, where clarity matters more: Attendez avant de conclure.

Do not standardize phrases mechanically. Choose the wording that sounds genuinely authored in context.

## 14. German localization standard

German needs particularly careful editorial treatment. Do not merely produce grammatically correct German. It should sound like original contemporary German nonfiction. Avoid excessive literalism.

Important concerns: English sentence order; overuse of abstract nouns; technically correct but culturally wrong lexical choices; excessive compound nouns; repeated translation equivalents where German would vary naturally; medical language when the source is not medical; stiff sentence rhythm.

Example — Avoid translating field guide as: Bestimmungsbuch, in this context. Prefer: Leitfaden, or another contextually appropriate formulation.

Evaluate: Ihr erster Befund ist nicht die ganze Geschichte. "Befund" may sound unnecessarily clinical. Consider depending on context: Ihre erste Lesart ist nicht die ganze Geschichte. Or: Der erste Eindruck ist nicht die ganze Geschichte.

Do not simply choose the word closest to the English. Choose the phrase a strong German nonfiction editor would accept.

## 15. Cross language parity

Create a parity table internally for every page. Compare English | French | German.

For every section verify: same purpose; same section position; same CTA; same amount of information; similar reading time; same conceptual claim; same evidence level; same visual treatment.

Word counts do not need to match. Meaning and editorial weight should. No language should feel like the secondary translation.

## 16. Design direction

The website should combine elements of: literary publishing; European editorial design; serious nonfiction; museum catalogue restraint; scientific documentation; premium print culture.

It should not mimic any particular publisher. Use these as quality references rather than visual templates.

Think: large type; quiet composition; book first; strong grid; minimal interaction; warm paper tone; precise red accent; subtle scientific language.

## 17. Typography

Typography carries most of the design. Use a serious editorial serif combined with a restrained sans serif where needed.

Hierarchy should be created through scale, weight, spacing, alignment, rhythm. Not through excessive borders, panels, icons, or visual effects.

Avoid too many font sizes. Avoid treating every section heading as a different design event. Typography should feel like a coherent publication.

Test carefully on: large desktop; laptop; tablet; iPhone; narrow mobile. The mobile version must feel designed, not collapsed.

## 18. Book cover treatment

The cover is the hero object. Give it considerably more visual authority.

Prefer: generous scale; clean background; controlled shadow if any; substantial surrounding space; minimal competing copy.

Avoid: dramatic floating animations; mockups that cheapen the physical object; excessive perspective effects; spinning; unnecessary 3D effects.

The book should look like a book worth holding.

## 19. Animation

Animation must be almost invisible. Use motion only when it improves comprehension or creates a subtle sense of measurement.

Acceptable: very gentle opacity transition; restrained text reveal; subtle signal movement; controlled movement in instrument motifs.

Avoid: scroll choreography; excessive parallax; animated counters; floating interface elements; cursor tricks; elaborate hover effects; loading theatrics; anything that resembles an agency showcase.

If a visitor notices the animation before noticing the writing, remove the animation.

## 20. Interaction

Interaction should support the book. Potential actions: read extract; publication notification; press material; language change; author information.

Keep the number of decisions per screen low. Do not create engagement mechanics. The site does not need to entertain someone for ten minutes. Its job is to create interest, trust, and memory.

## 21. Navigation

Keep navigation short. Possible structure: Book; Extract; Author; Press; Language selector.

Avoid unnecessary nested navigation. On mobile, the menu should remain elegant and extremely simple.

## 22. Accessibility

Meet strong accessibility standards. Ensure: sufficient contrast; semantic HTML; keyboard navigation; visible focus states; correct heading structure; alt text; accessible language selector; reduced motion support; reasonable font size; readable line length.

Do not sacrifice accessibility for minimalism.

## 23. Performance

The website should be very fast. Prioritize: optimized fonts; optimized images; minimal JavaScript; no unnecessary animation libraries; responsive image loading; good Core Web Vitals; minimal layout shift.

Do not install another library merely to accomplish an effect achievable with CSS.

## 24. SEO and metadata

Implement professional metadata for each language. Include: title; description; canonical URLs; language alternate links; Open Graph metadata; social preview; book schema where appropriate; author schema where appropriate; publication information; correct indexing strategy.

Ensure English, French, and German are discoverable independently without producing duplicate content problems.

## 25. Mobile experience

Treat mobile as a primary design surface. On mobile: the cover remains visually dominant; paragraphs remain short; type remains confident; no section becomes a wall of text; controls remain obvious; timestamps remain legible; no horizontal overflow; no animation interferes with reading.

Do not simply scale down the desktop version. Recompose where necessary.

## 26. Avoid common AI design patterns

Actively inspect the output for signs of AI generated web design. Remove: unnecessary cards; excessive rounded rectangles; generic gradients; glowing effects; symmetrical feature grids; too many icon and text pairs; arbitrary statistics; visual filler; multiple slogans; repeated explanatory subheadings; generic CTA language; synthetic looking placeholder testimonials; random decorative scientific graphics; fake data visualization.

Every visual element must have a reason to exist.

## 27. Copy editing principle

Apply this test to every paragraph: If removing this paragraph makes the site more intriguing without making it confusing, remove it.

Apply this test to every sentence: Is this telling the reader something they already understood from the previous sentence? If yes, cut or combine it.

Apply this test to every headline: Could this headline appear on another psychology book website? If yes, rewrite it.

## 28. Design review questions

Before accepting any page, ask:

1. What is the first thing the eye sees?
2. Is that the correct thing?
3. What is the second thing?
4. Is there unnecessary competition?
5. Does the page feel more like a book than software?
6. Is anything trying too hard?
7. Does the site trust the reader?
8. Can anything be removed?
9. Does the book cover have enough authority?
10. Does this still feel like Ivana's project rather than the designer's project?

## 29. Localization review questions

For every translated page ask:

1. Would a native editor know immediately that this originated in English?
2. Are there lexical choices that are technically correct but culturally wrong?
3. Has English sentence architecture survived unnecessarily?
4. Are metaphors being translated literally?
5. Does the language have its own rhythm?
6. Is the level of formality appropriate?
7. Would this sentence appear naturally in a serious nonfiction book published in that language?

If not, rewrite it.

## 30. Final deliverables

Do not merely provide recommendations. Implement the redesign. Before coding, produce internally a concise editorial map. Then build.

Deliver:

A. Redesigned website — fully responsive and production ready.
B. Canonical page architecture — document the final structure.
C. Content change log — for significant cuts or rewrites identify original purpose, decision, reason. Do not create a line by line bureaucratic report. Focus on meaningful editorial decisions.
D. Localization report — identify major changes made in French and German where literal translations were replaced with native formulations.
E. Design system — document typography, spacing, color, grid, responsive logic, primary components, instrument motifs, motion rules. Keep this system small.
F. QA checklist — verify desktop, tablet, mobile, Safari, Chrome, English, French, German, navigation, forms, links, SEO, accessibility, performance.

## 31. Decision authority

You have permission to challenge the current website.

Do not preserve a section because it is visually attractive. Do not preserve copy because it sounds intelligent. Do not preserve an interaction because it required significant coding.

Preserve only what improves: credibility; understanding; memory; desire to read.

## 32. Critical constraint

Do not destroy the strange quality that currently makes the book memorable. The goal is not generic elegance. The goal is controlled distinctiveness.

Keep enough of the instrument language that the site could belong only to State. Not Situation. But remove enough that the visitor does not feel trapped inside the metaphor.

The book is the product. The idea is the attraction. The website is the frame.

## 33. Final creative direction

The finished experience should feel closer to opening the front matter of an unusually intelligent book than entering a marketing website.

The visitor should encounter: space; a book; a proposition; three variables; three human moments; evidence; a voice; an invitation to continue.

Nothing should feel desperate for attention. Nothing should look accidental. Nothing should feel generated.

The final standard is: Someone very good made this, and someone even better knew when to stop.
