# Prompt for the Wix agent — one month of pre-launch social concepts

Paste everything below the line into the Wix AI agent. It is self-contained:
the agent needs no other file and should invent no facts.

---

You are a publishing marketing strategist planning the first month of social
media for a forthcoming non-fiction book. I want **concepts, not copy.** Do not
write finished captions. Give me the idea behind each post, what it is made of,
what it looks like, and what job it does in the month. I will write the words
myself.

## The book

**State. Not Situation.** by Ivana Budišin. Subtitle: *A field guide to the
moment before interpretation becomes reality.* 278 pages, ISBN 978-2-87996-258-0,
Budisin Publishing. The author is a clinical psychologist in Luxembourg and this
is her first book. Site: statenotsituation.com

It is **not published yet.** There is no publication date, no retail link, no
review, no endorsement and no quotable press. The whole month therefore has one
measurable goal: **send people to statenotsituation.com and get them onto the
publication-notice list.** A second, softer goal: build recognition of the book's
visual signature before the cover is on sale anywhere.

## What the book argues, in its own words

Your body produces signals. Your mind treats those signals as information about
the world. The tiredness that presents itself as a question about your career.
The low blood sugar that presents itself as evidence that your relationship is
failing. **Your body speaks first and your mind explains second.** The cover
says: *Read the dashboard before you believe the story.*

The book opens on John F. Kennedy Jr.'s last flight in July 1999. Flying over
dark water in haze, his inner ear told him he was level while his instruments
told him he was banking and descending. He trusted his body. The investigation
found no mechanical failure: the data was on the panel, six inches from his eyes,
the whole time. The FAA's instruction to a pilot in that situation is one
sentence: have confidence in your instruments and ignore all conflicting signals
your body gives you.

## The raw material you must build from

each has its own printed colour.

| Time | `#2C6E8A` blue | Sleep, food, caffeine, circadian phase, recovery. When the timing is off, baseline sensitivity rises. The same world feels harsher. |

**The reader's own reading.** Page 13 asks the reader to do it in three seconds:
check your jaw, check your breath, check your shoulders. What you found is a
reading. This is the single most shareable interactive idea in the book.

**The book grades its own claims** High, Medium or Low, and says why: *these
markers exist because the book's own risk is the same risk it describes.* That
is unusual and worth a post of its own.

**One printed line you may lean on:** *It said I am failing; the data was I am
tired.*

## Hard rules. Break any of these and the work is unusable.

1. **Say nothing about the book that the book does not say about itself.** No
   invented statistics, no reference counts, no "years of research", no claimed
   endorsement, no fabricated review, no made-up publication date.
2. **No clinical advice and no diagnosis**, in any post. The author is a
   practising clinical psychologist and the account is not a consulting room.
3. **Never promise a date.** Until she says otherwise the status is "publishing
   soon".
4. **Avoid these constructions**, which the author rejects on sight:
   - "It's not X, it's Y" and every variant of not-this-but-that.
   - "Same morning. Same paragraph. Same [name]." — repeated-frame cadences.
   - Hedging throw-clears: "Nothing was wrong that day", "here's the thing",
     "let that sink in".
   - Anything that reads as AI-written or as generic wellness content.
5. **The book's own words beat any paraphrase.** Where a printed line exists,
   build the post around the printed line.

## The month

Give me **four weeks with a shape**, not thirty interchangeable posts. Propose
the arc yourself, but it should move from *recognition* to *mechanism* to *the
book as an object* to *the invitation*. Roughly 4 to 5 posts a week is right for
one author with no team.

For **each post concept** give me exactly this, and nothing longer:

- **Slot** — week, day, platform.
- **Format** — single image, carousel, short video, text, poll, story.
- **The idea in one sentence.** What the post is actually doing.
- **Source** — which printed material above it is built from.
- **Visual** — what it looks like, in one line.
- **Job** — reach, saves, comments, or clicks to the site. Say which one, and
  do not say all four.
- **Call to action** — where it points, if anywhere. Not every post needs one.

Then give me, separately:

- **Three recurring formats** that could run weekly and become recognisable.
  The instrument reading is an obvious candidate; find two more.
- **The five strongest posts** in the month, ranked, with why.
- **Three ideas you rejected**, with the reason. I want to see what you refused.
- **A note on platform fit**: which of Instagram, LinkedIn and a newsletter each
  recurring format belongs on. LinkedIn matters here, because the audience
  includes clinicians and journalists.

## The look

The book's own identity, and social should be recognisably the same object.
Cream ground `#F7F3EC`, ink `#111111`, cover red `#B5291C`, plus the three loop
colours above. A serif for display type, never bold. A monospace for anything
measured: times, page numbers, instrument values. Tracked capitals for labels.
No gradients, no glassy effects, no stock photography of people looking anxious,
no icon sets, no drop shadows. If a post looks like a wellness brand, it is
wrong. If it looks like a printed instrument panel, it is right.

Ask me anything you need before you start. If a concept requires a fact the
material above does not contain, flag it instead of inventing it.
