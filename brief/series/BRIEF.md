# The series: a working brief

A discussion series by Ivana Budišin. Recorded for YouTube, linked from the book's site.
Consolidated 17 October 2026 from the conversation of that day, in two passes. The
first pass framed the series on bifurcation and dynamical systems; the author set it
aside the same day on the assessment that a clinician is believed on what only a
clinician can see, and that a borrowed mathematical frame spends credibility where this
series should build it. The first pass is kept below under *Superseded*, and its
material survives as a tool. On the branch `series/bifurcation-brief`; a working draft
until the author has read it. Title to be decided by the author.

## What it is

Each episode takes one claim about the body and the mind that circulates widely, that
patients arrive with, and that has a real literature behind it, and asks a single
question: what is actually known. The episode separates the kernel of good physiology
from the overextension and from the commercial edge, with the sources on screen, gives a
one-sentence verdict, and then shows what the clinician does with a person who arrived
believing the popular version.

The series is not a takedown. It is a practitioner being exact in public about what she
can and cannot promise, and saying what she does instead. An episode ends on the better
clinical answer, not on the error.

## How the book sits in it

The book is the scientific background and the application, not the story. Its page 7
prints the hinge: the three systems "are not brain regions or neural pathways. They are
a sorting tool." Every popular claim the series examines fails by presenting a sorting
idea as a physiological fact. The book claims less, and claiming less is what survives a
verdict.

So each episode has four movements:

1. The claim, as patients bring it.
2. The evidence, with the sources named on screen.
3. The verdict, in one sentence, written before anything else.
4. The application: what is done instead, which is to sort the moment with the three
   questions page 9 prints. What came before this moment. What keeps pulling me back.
   What seems at stake. The application needs the examined theory to be neither true nor
   false, which is why it can follow every verdict without looking rigged.

The back cover's own list indexes the episodes: "Sleep pressure, hunger, timing,
attention, threat detection, memory, and prediction quietly shape what feels true."
Cortisol and adrenal fatigue belong to Time. The attention economy and "dopamine detox"
belong to Attention. The vagus nerve, polyvagal theory and trauma stored in the body
belong to Safety.

## Three structural defences against the series becoming an advertisement

The application must stand alone: a listener who has never heard of the book can use the
three questions from the episode itself.

At least one episode ends with the book having nothing to add, said plainly.

One episode turns the method on the book. "Threat detection" and "prediction" on the
back cover lean on predictive accounts of interoception that are themselves contested;
examined as hard as the vagus nerve, they show the standard applies to the author.

## The discipline

Write the verdict first. If it reads "the evidence is weaker than the claim and here is
what is solid underneath", the episode works. If it reads only "this is wrong", it is a
takedown and does not. If it cannot be written because the evidence is balanced, that is
also an episode, and a rarer one.

Cut the application and show the rest to someone. If they ask "so what do I do", the
application belongs. If they do not, it was promotion.

Every factual sentence rests on a source a journalist could check, verified in
`CITATIONS.md` before it reaches a script. Nothing is cited from memory alone.

## Candidate claims, first list

Cortisol as "the stress hormone". Adrenal fatigue. The vagus nerve and what is sold under
its name, including polyvagal theory. Trauma stored in the body. The gut as a second
brain. Heart rate variability as a readiness score on a wrist. Dopamine detox. Each
needs its verdict sentence and its citation check before a script is written. The first
recorded should be the one with the strongest evidence and the least controversy, so the
method is established on safe ground before it reaches contested ground; cortisol is the
candidate.

## The tool kept from the first pass

Dynamical systems ideas come in where they earn their place inside an episode, as a
precise idea from a neighbouring field reached for to explain something observed, never
as the frame. Hysteresis is the honest answer to why reassurance does not work at the
level the alarm came in, and belongs in the episode on trauma in the body. Critical
slowing down belongs nowhere until the human evidence improves; see `CITATIONS.md`,
source 10. The verified sources for these ideas are in `CITATIONS.md` and the strict
statements of them in `EPISODES.md`, which is superseded as an episode plan and kept as
the record of the ideas.
