# Bifurcation: Dynamical Systems
## The Biology of a State

A discussion series by Ivana Budišin. Recorded for YouTube, linked from the book's site.
Brief consolidated 17 October 2026 from the conversation of that day. Not committed; a
working draft until the author has read it.

## What it is

A series of conversations that use dynamical systems, and bifurcation in particular, as a
lens for how a body moves between states. It does not set out to prove anything. It sets
out to let a listener notice something in their own week that they could not name before.

The book *State. Not Situation.* is the room the series is recorded in, not its subject.
The book's loops, its three variables and its devices stay in the background. They may be
referred to; they do not structure an episode.

## The claims the series stands on

Three are demonstrated and can be stated plainly, with the case that shows each:

1. Nature reuses a small catalogue of transition shapes because stability forces them, not
   the material. The neuron crossing from silence into firing as current rises; the lake
   flipping from clear to turbid as nutrient load rises; two fingers switching from
   anti-phase to in-phase as the metronome speeds up.
2. A biomarker is a trajectory, not a level. The body answers the shape of the change.
   Two clocks: autonomic in seconds, endocrine (cortisol) in tens of minutes to hours.
3. Experience is not an outside sensor. Attending to a bodily signal changes that
   signal's trajectory, with a measurable lag and a direction set by appraisal. The
   observer is inside the loop.

Everything beyond these three is marked, on camera, as proposed rather than shown.

## The discipline

Each episode carries one idea, one demonstrated biological case, one ordinary moment a
listener can feel, and one negative case: a change that looks like the idea and is not.
Without the negative case the lens cannot be wrong and teaches nothing.

Every factual sentence rests on a source a journalist could check. The failure to avoid
is the one catastrophe theory committed in the 1970s: a diagram drawn over data nobody
measured (Zahler & Sussmann, 1977).

## Episode plan, first cut

0. What a state is, as distinct from a feeling and a situation.
1. Sudden change from gradual cause. Case: the neuron. Moment: "I was fine and then I wasn't."
2. Hysteresis. Case: the lake that will not clear where it clouded. Moment: needing more
   reassurance to come down than it took to go up.
3. Critical slowing down. Case: ecological early warning. Moment: small things taking
   longer to shake off in the week before you tip.

Twenty to thirty minutes each. One idea each.
