## The short version

You are right about the case studies, and it is worse than taste.

**Two of the nine home-page sections quote devices the book no longer prints.**

| Section | What it shows | Status in v50 |
|---|---|---|
| `Moments` | Three CASE EVIDENCE panels: INPUT, VERIFIED EVENT / VERIFIED TONE / VERIFIED CRISIS | **Gone.** "CASE EVIDENCE" appears 0 times in v50. "VERIFIED" appears 0 times. |
| `Readings` | Fifteen chapter openers as `TIME: NOT CLAIMED · ATTENTION: DEGRADED · SAFETY: DOMINANT` | **Gone.** Replaced by *Before you read* boxes in plain sentences, each led by a question. |

So the site breaks its own rule — *the site may not say anything about the book that the book does not say about itself* — in the two places you already felt were vague. They read as vague because the book stopped being vague and the site did not follow.

Both have printed replacements, and both are better.

## 1. `Moments` → the same day, five readings

**Page 4 has been rebuilt.** It now prints, whole:

> **THE FIRST MISREADING**
>
> Nothing went wrong on this day.
> The instruments were working. The data was there the whole time.
>
> **THE SAME DAY, FIVE READINGS**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | 06:38 | A heaviness arrives before the day does. |
> | 09:12 | A two-line email reads like a judgement. |
> | 14:23 | Five neutral words tighten a jaw. |
> | 17:45 | An unanswered message starts charging cognitive rent. |
> | 22:47 | Two letters and a full stop feel hostile. |
>
> **THE BODY SPEAKS FIRST. THE MIND EXPLAINS SECOND.**

Put that in the slot the three panels occupy now, in the same place in the page order.

**Why it is the fix.** The three panels each ended on a negation — *Nothing has happened. / None. / None.* Three refusals in a row, and a reader skimming takes away that the book is about nothing. The five readings are five positives, each one a thing the reader has felt, and they are **one day**, so they cohere instead of being three unrelated specimens. "Cognitive rent" and "Five neutral words tighten a jaw" are the kind of line someone screenshots.

**What it costs.** Nothing. It uses the devices the section already has — the timestamp in mono at DISPLAY, one line beside it — and drops the INPUT / VERIFIED apparatus, which is the part that read as bureaucratic. No new type step, no new colour.

**One thing the site is missing entirely.** *"The body speaks first. The mind explains second."* is on page 4 and is nowhere on the website. Dr Herber's foreword singles it out as the line that captures the book. It should be on the first page a stranger reads.

## 2. `Readings` → the sixteen names

This is the largest single gain available, and it is new since the site was last touched.

**Every chapter now ends with *A name for it*:** a word for the mechanism, a plain explanation outside Katrin's story, three *How it shows up* examples, where the book explains it, and a **TRY IT**. All sixteen are listed on page 292.

> Narrating mode · Misattribution · The smoke detector principle · Variable reward · Wanting and liking · Wake Maintenance Zone · Default mode network · Evaluation threat · Rumination · Demand–withdraw · Metacognition · Stacking · Prediction error · Calibration · Warm panel · Forecast

Sixteen nouns. That is the answer to *what do I actually get from this book?*, and the site currently answers it with fifteen rows of `NOT CLAIMED / DEGRADED / DOMINANT`, which mean nothing to someone who has not read it.

**On the page:** keep the dashboard shape that already exists — number, name, page — so it costs no new design. Then open **one** entry in full, so a reader sees what an entry is. Use **Misattribution**:

> **A NAME FOR IT / 01 — Misattribution**
>
> Giving a feeling the wrong cause. The body produces the feeling. The mind looks for a reason and takes the nearest one. The feeling is real. The reason is a guess.
>
> **How it shows up**
> – Tiredness that arrives as a question about your career.
> – Coffee on an empty stomach that arrives as worry about an email.
> – Low blood sugar that arrives as doubt about a relationship.
>
> **Try it** — Before acting on a confident judgement, name one fact that could change your mind.

**Why that one.** Those three examples are already on the site. They are in the pilot extract, four sections above: *"The tiredness that presents itself as a question about your career. The caffeine spike that presents itself as anxiety about an email. The low blood sugar that presents itself as evidence that your relationship is failing."* A reader meets them as prose in the extract, then meets them again with a name on them. The page closes its own loop, and the reader learns the book's method by doing it once.

**Recommendation: the names replace the readings, not join them.** Nine sections is already the ceiling and your brief is confidence through subtraction. If you would rather keep the opener dashboard as well, it has to be rebuilt from the new boxes anyway — see §4.

## 3. `Variables` — three questions instead of three definitions

The site sets the three systems as three paragraphs from pages 7–8. They read as lectures on a screen.

**Page 9 now prints them as questions:**

> **THREE WAYS TO READ A MOMENT**
>
> **TIME** — What came before this moment? Sleep, meals, caffeine, recovery, the hour.
> **ATTENTION** — What keeps pulling me back? A message, a thought, a cue, an unfinished task.
> **SAFETY** — What seems at stake? What have I observed, what am I assuming, and does anything need action?

Three questions, roughly fifteen words each, against three paragraphs of fifty. A question invites the reader to answer it; a definition asks them to study. This is also what the book now leads with, so it is the current text, not a shortening of it.

Keep the longer definitions on `/press`, where a journalist wants the full version.
