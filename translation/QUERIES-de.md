# Queries to the author, German edition

Date: 5 September 2026, extended 6 September 2026 after the review of content/de.ts (the last three queries, and the second half of the page 20 query, are from that pass). Raised by the lead German translator while rebuilding content/de.ts under STANDARD.md. Each TRANSLATION QUERY is a place where German could not keep both the scientific meaning and the idiom without a choice only the author should make; the recommendation is what the file currently says. Each SOURCE QUERY is a place where the English itself seemed unclear. Nothing here is blocking: the file typechecks and reads as German with the recommendations in place.

---

TRANSLATION QUERY
Source: A field guide to the moment before interpretation becomes reality (subtitle, cover, meta description, press facts)
Issue: German has no single word that is both "guide" and "used in the field". Feldführer exists (birding) but is rare and reads as the English word in German letters; Bestimmungsbuch is the established German name for a field guide and carries bestimmen, to identify or determine, which is what the book asks the reader to do with a state. Leitfaden and Handbuch lose the field.
Option A: Ein Bestimmungsbuch für den Moment, bevor aus Deutung Wirklichkeit wird
Option B: Ein Feldführer für den Moment, bevor aus Deutung Wirklichkeit wird
Recommendation: A. It is the word a German publisher would print, and the double sense is a gift the English does not have. B if you want the reader to see the English subtitle through the German.

TRANSLATION QUERY
Source: The Clean Panel (chapter 12 title, page 167; on the site in the chapter list)
Issue: "clean" for a panel that shows no fault is an English collocation. "Das saubere Instrumentenbrett" is understandable and keeps the panel, which is the book's central image (standard, metaphor type D), but a German reader may hear physical cleanliness until the chapter explains itself. German medicine's phrase for a check that finds nothing is "ohne Befund", which is exactly the chapter's situation (the check came back green and something was there) and ties to the glossary word for reading.
Option A: Das saubere Instrumentenbrett
Option B: Ohne Befund
Recommendation: A, because the panel is type D and "clean panel" recurs inside the chapter as a pair with "warm panel" and "loaded panel", which "ohne Befund" cannot pair with. B is the wittier German title and I would take it if you are willing to let the chapter's running text say "ohne Befund" for "clean" and keep Instrumentenbrett for "panel".

TRANSLATION QUERY
Source: It works in the gap between fine and clinical, the space where most people live on most days. (page 14)
Issue: "fine" is a self-report ("I'm fine"); German has no one-word pole that is both colloquial and pairs with "klinisch". "gesund" is the natural German pole and makes the sentence read as German, but it is a health status, a small semantic shift from "fine". "unauffällig" is the clinician's word for nothing found and keeps the register of the author, but it is jargon where the English is plain. "in Ordnung" needs quotation marks to work as a pole.
Option A: Es arbeitet in der Lücke zwischen gesund und klinisch, in dem Raum, in dem die meisten Menschen an den meisten Tagen leben.
Option B: Es arbeitet in der Lücke zwischen „in Ordnung“ und „klinisch“, in dem Raum, in dem die meisten Menschen an den meisten Tagen leben.
Recommendation: A. The gap the sentence names is the subclinical one, and "zwischen gesund und klinisch" says that at once; the shift from feeling fine to being well is small and the sentence is about territory, not diagnosis. Flagged because the standard asks for any semantic adjustment to be flagged.

TRANSLATION QUERY
Source: from attention and prediction to memory, threat, uncertainty and social inference (your description of the book)
Issue: "social inference" is a term of art in social cognition. The German term of art, "soziale Inferenz", is rare outside journals and would be jargon in a list where the other four words are plain; "soziales Schlussfolgern" is heavier still. The plain German says the mechanism instead of naming it.
Option A: bis hin zu den Schlüssen, die wir über andere Menschen ziehen
Option B: bis hin zur sozialen Inferenz
Recommendation: A. It keeps the level of the list. B only if you want the term visible to an academic reader.

TRANSLATION QUERY
Source: An unanswered message starts charging rent. (page 10)
Issue: There is no German idiom for a thought that charges rent; German says something "nistet sich ein" (nests) or "geht einem nicht aus dem Kopf", neither of which carries the cost. The literal image reads as the author's own oddness in German as it does in English, and page 10 is a display page of compressed images.
Option A: Eine unbeantwortete Nachricht fängt an, Miete zu verlangen.
Option B: Eine unbeantwortete Nachricht nistet sich ein.
Recommendation: A. It keeps the cost, which is the point (the open file, the phantom load), and a German reader gets it on the first pass. B is smoother and loses the mechanism.

TRANSLATION QUERY
Source: It said I am failing; the data was I am tired. (page 20, standing alone under the case cards)
Issue: Two things at once. First, on the site the line has no antecedent for "It" in either language. English tolerates a bare "It said"; a bare "Es sagte" in German reads as a grammatical accident. "Es hieß" (the word was) is the German impersonal that stands alone. Second, the English withholds a speech verb from the data on purpose: the story said something, the data simply was something. German wants to answer "sagte" with "sagten", and that gives the data a voice which in this book belongs to the body and the mind. German takes the copula with a colon in this position (Die Antwort war: ...), so the asymmetry survives as asymmetry.
Option A: Es hieß: Ich versage. Die Daten waren: Ich bin müde.
Option B: Es hieß: Ich versage. Die Daten sagten: Ich bin müde.
Recommendation: A, which is what the file now says. It keeps the said / was contrast the English draws inside one line. B is smoother German and loses it. A third form, the verbless "Die Daten: Ich bin müde.", is the book's own fragment habit but reads as a label under the case cards.

TRANSLATION QUERY
Source: It is about that error as a daily, ambient, invisible feature of being alive in a body (page 12)
Issue: German has no one-word "ambient". The English triad names three different things: how often (daily), how it is present (in the background, unattended, the "background headache" of the same page), and whether it can be seen (invisible). "Allgegenwärtig" was in the file and is withdrawn: it means omnipresent, which overlaps "täglich" and claims more than the English. "Beiläufig" is the plain German for something that is there without being attended to.
Option A: als etwas Tägliches, Beiläufiges, Unsichtbares
Option B: als etwas Tägliches, das ständig im Hintergrund mitläuft und unsichtbar bleibt
Recommendation: A, which is what the file says. It keeps the three-part list and adds nothing. B says the mechanism instead of naming it and turns a list into a clause, which lengthens a sentence the English keeps tight.

TRANSLATION QUERY
Source: how we notice, interpret and revise the world around us (your "Who it is for" line)
Issue: In English one object serves all three verbs. German "revidieren" does not take "die Welt": it takes ein Urteil, eine Meinung, eine Einschätzung, eine Annahme. "Die Welt revidieren" reads as revising the world itself. The German therefore has to name what is revised, which is the interpretation the previous verb just made.
Option A: wie wir die Welt um uns herum wahrnehmen, deuten und unsere Deutung revidieren
Option B: wie wir die Welt um uns herum wahrnehmen, deuten und neu deuten
Recommendation: A, which is what the file says. It says what the English means and uses the book's own word for the thing that gets revised. B avoids repeating a noun but makes revision sound like a second interpretation rather than a correction of the first. Flagged because the standard asks for any semantic adjustment to be named, and this one supplies an object the English leaves implicit.

TRANSLATION QUERY
Source: Extract licensing, interviews and events by arrangement. (press page)
Issue: "Abdruckrechte für Auszüge, Interviews und Veranstaltungen" was in the file and is withdrawn: a German reader takes it as reprint rights for extracts, for interviews and for events, which offers something the English does not. Two further points. "Auszug" is on the rejected list for extract, where the site's word is Leseprobe everywhere else; and "Leseproben" in this slot does not remove the ambiguity, because "für Leseproben sowie Interviews" can still be read as one list.
Option A: Abdruckrechte, Interviews und Veranstaltungen nach Absprache.
Option B: Abdruckrechte für Leseproben sowie Interviews und Veranstaltungen nach Absprache.
Recommendation: A, which is what the file says. On a German press page "Abdruckrechte" already means the right to print an extract, so the three offers stand as three, the glossary stays whole and nothing is claimed that the English does not offer. Take B if you want the word Leseprobe visible in the rights line.
