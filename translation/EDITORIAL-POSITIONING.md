# Editorial positioning of the launch site

Date: 5 September 2026
Decided by: the editor-in-chief, after reading the almost-final text of the book in full (pages 1 to 278), the four colleagues' proposals (the book's editor, the publicist, the reader's advocate, the bookseller), `content/en.ts`, `CONTENT_SOURCES.md`, `site.config.ts` and the home page section order in `app/[lang]/page.tsx`.

The rule of the site is unchanged and governs everything below: the site may not say anything about the book that the book does not say about itself. Every passage added here is quoted word for word from the almost-final text, with its page number checked against that text. Where a line is needed that the book does not contain, it is a question for the author, not a sentence written on her behalf.

## The author's complaint

"I find some things too important to be at the bottom and others too far the top." She is right in both directions, and the four colleagues, working independently, reached the same diagnosis. What follows is the decision on their proposals.

## Diagnosis

The book has a deliberate opening sequence. A story (the pilot, pages 7 to 9). A one-page account of itself with five timestamped readings (page 10: "Nothing went wrong on this day. The instruments were working. The data was there the whole time. This book is the investigation."). The map (page 11). A recognition passage in the second person (page 12, "You Know The Day", ending "This book is about that mistake"). Only then the instruction to check your jaw (page 13), the note on evidence (page 13), the three sorting systems (pages 13 to 14) and Katrin (page 17). Problem, recognition, reading, tool, character, in that order.

The site runs that sequence backwards. Its second section is the page 13 exercise, detached from the page 12 sentences it answers. Its third is the three systems, the book's most abstract passage, headed by a sentence about a woman the visitor has not met. Its fourth is six case panels. The pilot comes fifth, then an audio player with no audio, and only seventh, on a phone eight to ten screens down, the author's own description of what the book is, who it is for, and the page 12 line "This book is about that mistake", quoted with the antecedent of "that mistake" missing. The author sits eighth.

Meanwhile the book's own pitch page, page 10, which answers what the book is, how it is told and what it claims in about seventy words, is not on the site at all.

Four smaller faults compound this.

1. The case evidence introduction, "Every chapter opens on one of these pages.", is not the book's sentence and is not true. CASE EVIDENCE pages precede chapters 00 (page 16), 02 (page 28), 05 (page 66), 11 (page 150), 12 (page 166), 13 (page 182) and 15 (page 208) only. Nine chapters have none.
2. The heading of "About the book", "The body is a sensor before it is a narrator." (page 181), is Chapter Twelve's sentence, from the part of the book that argues against itself. As a headline over the whole book it promises the exception rather than the rule, and the Scientific Heartbeat explicitly parts company with the book that takes that line further (page 269, on de Becker). An academic who had read the first eleven chapters would notice.
3. The "Who it is for" line ("For anyone who has ever been certain about what a situation meant…") has no recorded source. `CONTENT_SOURCES.md` still lists `about.readers` as a placeholder, and unlike `about.paragraphs` it carries no note that the author supplied it. Under the site's own rule it needs a source or it comes down.
4. The fourth paragraph of the press page description is a paraphrase of the reference apparatus, and part of it ("the places where the chapters say more than their sources") echoes a heading that has been cut from the almost-final text. The press page is the page a journalist checks hardest.

What is right, and stays: the hero, which is the cover and nothing else; the page 13 reading, which moves but is not cut; the case panels, trimmed; the pilot; the author's two paragraphs and her closing sentence; the map, the three systems and the evidence markers, which move together into one reference block; one sentence for the author and no more; and the restraint of the whole.

Nothing here requires new copy. The book contains its own website in pages 10 to 14, page 20, page 26, page 225 and page 278. The site should follow that arc in miniature and move the author's description up under it.

1. Hero. The cover as it is: eyebrow "Time · Attention · Safety", title, subtitle, strap, "by Ivana Budišin", the status line, two buttons. Read an extract becomes the primary button and Be notified the secondary; before publication the extract is what sells, and the notify form is repeated at the foot of the page. Unchanged otherwise.

This book is the investigation." Then "The same day, five readings" and the five timestamped lines, 06:38 to 22:47, in the mono face. Closing on "The body speaks first. The mind explains second." Eyebrow: "The first misreading", the page's printed heading.

3. You know the day (new). Page 12, verbatim, under the page's printed heading. Three runs: "The day I mean is the other kind. The day where nothing went wrong and everything felt off…" through "…like rainy weather behind glass."; "You have had this day. Everyone has had this day…" through "…make the story dissolve."; and "The body spoke first. (pages 12 to 13). The sentence "The story that is build on a day like this…" is skipped because it carries a typo in the almost-final text.

4. The reading. Page 13, exactly as the site has it: "Whatever you are feeling as you read this sentence. Check your jaw. Check your breath. Check your shoulders. 5. The book. Heading: "Same morning. Same paragraph. Same Katrin. Different instrument settings." (page 20). 6. Read. The pilot, pages 7 to 9, with the teaser extended from two paragraphs to three so that it ends on "You feel level, but you are not." Then Continue reading. 7. Case evidence. Four panels, 00, 02, 11 and 13 (pages 16, 28, 150, 182), in chapter order, with the untrue introduction removed and the section title dropped (the count belongs to the map, where the book prints it). The section closes on page 20: "It said I am failing; the data was I am tired."

8. A map of the book. One reference block, in the order the book presents these things. First the three systems, introduced by the full page 20 paragraph ("Three systems ran through Katrin's morning. Time started at 06:38 and never stopped. Attention took the phone at 06:52. Safety wrote the email's meaning at 07:25. She can name none of them. The only thing she can name is David, and David is the smallest part of it."), then the front-matter sentence "They are not brain regions or neural pathways. They are a sorting tool, a way to ask three questions when everything feels wrong at once." and the three definitions (pages 13 to 14). Second, the evidence note (page 13, "Before We Begin"), extended by the three sentences that follow it in the book: "These markers exist because the book's own risk is the same risk it describes. It does not want to treat a plausible story as settled truth. These markers are a brake on that.", with the High / Medium / Low legend. Third, "The Investigation", "16 cases. Three readings. One question: state or situation?", the sixteen chapters with their page numbers (page 11), and the map footer "The Scientific Heartbeat follows the chapters.", now followed by page 226: "Each chapter has three parts here: a short note on where the thinking came from, a list of the work the chapter is built on, and a list of the work that limits it, complicates it, or explains the same evidence differently." and "Leaving them out would make the argument look tidier than it is."

9. The author. Photograph, the one supplied sentence, Contact, Press. Unchanged in size. If the author writes a line on why she wrote the book, it sits here.

10. The closing question and Be notified. Page 225, the book's last sentence, directly above the form: "Is this the situation? Or is this their state?" Then "Be notified when the book is released." and the form.

11. Footer. "Before you believe the story." and "Same life. Different instrument settings." as now, joined by the back page's three lines: "Read the dashboard. Delay the story. Take the reading again." (page 278).

Listen is hidden until a recording exists. The companion section stays off.

## Each change, with its reason

Each change is marked "Now" (can be done by the team today) or "Author" (needs a line only she can write, or her confirmation).

1. Add page 10, "The first misreading", directly under the hero. Now. It is the book's own one-page pitch, already written and already designed, and it answers what the book is, how it is told and what it claims in one phone screen. All four colleagues proposed it independently.

2. Add the page 12 passage, "You know the day", as section 3, ending on the existing "This book is about that mistake" quotation. Now. It is the only passage in the manuscript that says in plain second-person prose what the book is about and why it concerns the person reading, and it restores the antecedent that "that mistake" has been missing on the site. `CONTENT_SOURCES.md` itself admits the quotation needs its context.

3. Move the page 13 reading from position 2 to position 4, after page 12, and relabel its eyebrow "Try something right now". Now. In the book the instruction is the answer to "try something right now"; on the site it currently precedes the problem it is testing, so the question lands as a puzzle rather than a hinge.

4. Move the author's description and "Who it is for" from section 7 to section 5, immediately after the reading, under the page 20 heading "Same morning. Same paragraph. Same Katrin. Different instrument settings." Now. It is the only prose on the page that says in plain words what the book contains, and it introduces Katrin, whose name the site currently uses two sections before it explains her. The page 20 heading names her in the book's words one screen before the author's paragraph does, and echoes the cover.

5. Replace the unsourced "Who it is for" line with page 14, "This book does not replace professional support… the space where most people live on most days." Now, with the old line restored beneath only on the author's confirmation. The page 14 sentence names the reader, names the territory and states a limit, which is what a psychologist, an academic or a careful editor wants to see from a clinical psychologist's trade book. The old line has no recorded source.

6. Retire "The body is a sensor before it is a narrator." as the heading of the book section. Now. It is Chapter Twelve's concession, not the book's thesis; over the whole book it sells trust-your-body, which chapters 0 to 11 argue against. If the design can carry one line on card 13 (The Time the Body Was Right), this is that line, with its page, 181; otherwise it stays in the book.

7. Extend the extract teaser from two paragraphs to three, ending "You feel level, but you are not." Now. Two paragraphs stop at haze, before the mechanism. Three end on the sentence that explains it and leave "He trusted his body." for the reading page, where it lands as the story's turn. Four would put the teaser past three phone screens.

8. Close the extract on page 26, "This book is about the same error at kitchen scale…". Now. The opening is a plane over dark water and a visitor could take the book for an aviation book; this sentence tells them, in the book's words, that the territory is kitchens, inboxes and Tuesdays. The book itself licenses attaching "the same error" to the pilot (page 8: "applies to your life as directly as it applies to a cockpit"). The preceding sentence on page 26, "The warships are the extreme case.", is omitted because a visitor who has not read Chapter One would meet an unexplained word.

9. Move the case panels below the extract, trim them to 00, 02, 11 and 13, cut the introduction, drop the section title, and close on page 20, "It said I am failing; the data was I am tired." Now. Page 10 now does the "five readings" job at the top of the page, so the cards are texture and read best once the frame and the opening voice are in place. Four cards trace the book's arc: nothing happened, two letters, six ordinary things become a crisis, and the one time the alarm was real. 05 ("Oddly capable.") and 15 ("She reads tomorrow's.") need their chapters to be legible. The introduction is not the book's sentence and is not true. The page 20 line is the verdict on the first card and the sentence a journalist will quote.

10. Fold the three systems into the map section, introduced by the full page 20 paragraph. Now. The taxonomy is the book's sorting tool, not its hook, and it names Katrin. Next to the chapter list that carries TIME / ATTENTION / SAFETY it explains the map; on its own at position 3 it explains nothing yet. The page 20 paragraph makes three abstract nouns concrete in six sentences.

11. Extend the evidence note by its next three sentences (page 13) and add the two page 226 sentences under the map footer. Now. The markers and the Built on / Pushed against apparatus are the single feature that separates this book from the shelf it will sit on. The added sentences say why the markers exist and how the reference section is built, in the book's own words, replacing nothing and paraphrasing nothing.

12. Add page 225, "Is this the situation? Or is this their state?", directly above the notify form. Now. The page then opens (section 4) and closes on the book's question with one word changed, which is the whole arc of Chapters 14 and 15 and the afterword in a single turn. A reader who notices the changed word has understood the book; a journalist who notices it has a lead.

13. Add the back page's three lines, "Read the dashboard. Delay the story. Take the reading again." (page 278), to the footer band. Now. Printed, currently unused, and the method in nine words.

14. Hide Listen, with its nav and footer links, until `editions.en.audioUrl` points at a file. Now. A player reading "The recording will be added here." under "Read by the author" is the one thing on the page that reads as marketing, and it costs the phone visitor a full screen between the extract and the description.

15. Make Read an extract the primary hero button and Be notified the secondary. Now. Before publication the extract is what sells; the form is repeated at the foot.

16. Replace the fourth paragraph of the press page description with the page 226 sentences quoted in change 11. Now. The current paragraph is a paraphrase and part of it echoes a heading cut from the almost-final text. The book's own words survive a check; the paraphrase does not.

17. The author section keeps its size and moves to position 9, after the map and before the closing question. Now. Book first, author second is satisfied because sections 1 to 8 are all book. With Listen gone and the page shortened, she is no longer eighth of eleven under an empty player.

18. A hero byline for the credential. Author. "Clinical psychologist" is the one word that tells an editor what kind of book this is before they scroll, and the publicist is right that it belongs on the first screen. The supplied biography cannot be cut down without editing her sentence, so the site should ask her for a short byline in her own words rather than write one.

19. A line on why she wrote the book. Author. It is the one question in the twenty-second test that nothing in the manuscript answers in her voice. It belongs in the author section, beside the photograph, and only she can write it.

20. A publication month or season in place of "Publishing soon". Author. A journalist cannot diary "soon". Nothing is to be invented; if no date exists, "Publishing soon" stays.

Notes for the developer, not copy changes: on phones the first words about the book (section 2) should be reachable within one or two thumb swipes of the cover, so the hero's scroll track should be shortened on narrow screens; and on phones the words, status line and buttons should precede the 3D book in reading order.

## What was rejected, and why

- The publicist's hero eyebrow, "The body speaks first. The mind explains second." in place of "Time · Attention · Safety". The eyebrow is the cover, and the line is the closing line of section 2 immediately below it; saying it twice within one screen weakens it.
- The publicist's author section at position 4, ahead of the extract, the cards and the map. Book first, author second means the book, all of it, first. The credential need is met by a hero byline in her own words, which is a question for her.
- The publicist's supplied biography in the hero under "by Ivana Budišin". It repeats her name and cannot be trimmed without editing her sentence. Replaced by the byline question.
- The bookseller's "The body speaks first. The mind explains second." as the heading of the book section. Same reason as the eyebrow: it is section 2's last line. Page 20's "Same morning. Same paragraph. Same Katrin. Different instrument settings." does the job and introduces Katrin.
- The publicist's six case panels, and the reader's advocate's five. Four carry the arc; 05 and 15 are cryptic without their chapters, and six panels are three phone screens.
- The publicist's four-paragraph teaser ending "He trusted his body.". Three paragraphs end on the mechanism and keep the teaser under three screens; the sentence that carries the book is better met on the reading page.
- Pull lines on individual cards: page 29 "Certainty on a tired brain…" beside 22:47; page 181 "State. Not situation. And then, once in a while…" and page 224 "a tool, not a leash" beside 13. The cards are the book's own device and should carry no commentary; one closing line for the section (page 20) is enough. All three are good lines for the press page or an interview.
- Page 21, "The reading creates a small space…" and "This is reading mode…", as a close to the reading section. The section must end on the question that is the title; an explanation after it dilutes it.
- Page 14, the stop rule ("Each test comes with a stop rule… Put the tool down."), on the home page. A strong anti-self-help passage, but the page is not an anthology; one page 14 passage, the one that says who the book is for, is enough. Keep it in reserve for press.
- The bookseller's separate "Scientific Heartbeat" section ahead of the map. One reference block, not two; the page 226 lines are added, inside the map section.
- Page 205, "Same life. Different instrument settings. Yours, and theirs." in the map footer. Page 225 makes the same turn outward at the foot of the page, and once is enough.
- The publicist's cut of the companion placeholder. It is a permitted placeholder and the section is off.
- Any publication date, byline, or "why I wrote it" line written by the team. The rule does not allow it.

## The twenty-second read

A phone opens on the cover: STATE. NOT SITUATION, a field guide to the moment before interpretation becomes reality, your first reading is not the whole story, by Ivana Budišin, publishing soon, with Read an extract under the thumb and Be notified beside it. One swipe: nothing went wrong on this day, the instruments were working, the data was there the whole time, this book is the investigation; then five times of day and five small things, a heaviness before the alarm, a two-line email that reads like a verdict, five neutral words that tighten a jaw, two letters and a full stop that feel hostile; and the thesis in eight words, the body speaks first, the mind explains second. Second swipe: the day where nothing went wrong and everything felt off, a drag in the limbs, a heaviness behind your thoughts like rainy weather behind glass; you have had this day, everyone has; the body spoke first, the mind explained second, and the explanation felt like the event; this book is about that mistake. By now, at roughly twenty seconds, the visitor knows it is a serious psychology book about the ordinary day on which nothing happened and everything felt off, that it proceeds as an investigation in timestamped readings, that it feels precise, dry and on their side, and that there is an extract to read and a way to be told when it is out. If they keep going, the page asks them to check their own jaw and ends on the book's question; introduces Katrin in the book's words and then the author's, sixteen days of ordinary situations followed into the psychology underneath them, less interested in teaching you to trust your instincts than in showing you what, exactly, you are trusting; says the book works in the gap between fine and clinical, the space where most people live on most days; gives them the pilot over dark water who felt level and was not, and tells them the book is that error at kitchen scale, the version that happens every Tuesday; shows four case pages and the verdict on the first, it said I am failing, the data was I am tired; lays out the three systems, the evidence markers and the sixteen chapters for the reader who wants the structure, with a reference list that includes the work that limits each chapter because leaving it out would make the argument look tidier than it is; gives them a clinical psychologist in Luxembourg and one sentence; and closes on the book's last line, the same question with one word changed, is this the situation, or is this their state, with the email field beneath it and, on the way out, read the dashboard, delay the story, take the reading again.
