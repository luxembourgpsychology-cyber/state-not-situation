# German translation method for State. Not Situation.

Date: 2026-09-05. Written by the lead German translator after reading the almost-final text (278 pages) in full. This is the method for the whole book. It was applied first to the website strings in content/de.ts, and a second translator should be able to work from this document without me.

The author's brief, in her words: "I want the context translated, not word by word." On German: "much more straightforward vocabulary, and simpler words, and it is not a technical guide but more natural and conventional German." The job is to translate sense and effect. For every sentence, ask what it does to the reader in English, then write the German sentence a German writer would write to do that. Recast freely. Never add an idea, claim, image or qualification the English does not have. Never drop one it has.

## The book's voice in German

The book is a clinical psychologist writing for adults, in the second person, about a mistake everyone makes. It has three registers and moves between them inside a chapter, sometimes inside a page.

The narrative register. Katrin, Marc, Léo, Mila, David, Antoine, Sophie, in Luxembourg, at timestamps. Present tense, mostly. Concrete nouns: the kettle, the felt tips, the fruit bowl, the coil in the vending machine, the third stair that creaks. The sentences are plain and the detail is exact. "She ate a cereal bar from the drawer at 20:15 and called it dinner." In German this is Präsens, simple main clauses, the same concrete nouns, no literary heightening. Do not reach for Präteritum where the book is in the present. Do not make Katrin's kitchen sound like a novel translated from English; make it sound like a kitchen in Luxembourg described by someone who lives in one.

The mechanism register. Names, years, numbers, one-line verdicts. "Three patients." "One study, one walk." "The magnitude is different. The architecture is identical." Here the German may use the technical term the book uses (Amygdala, präfrontaler Cortex, zirkadian, Interozeption) because the book uses it and explains it in the same breath. But the explanation around the term must be as plain as the English. The book calls its own words "handles where the literature uses constructs". The German handles must be handles too: Lieferkette, offene Akte, Erzähler, Instrumentenbrett, Toast.

The instruction register. Imperatives, short. "Stop." "Read it once." "Put the phone face down. Set a timer." "Jaw. Breath. Shoulders." In German these are Sie-imperatives kept to the same length: "Halten Sie inne." "Lesen Sie es einmal." "Legen Sie das Handy mit dem Display nach unten. Stellen Sie einen Timer." And the three-word check stays three words: "Kiefer. Atem. Schultern."

Across all three, the voice has habits that must survive:

It uses apposition, not dashes. "The pilot's inner ear, the organ that tells the brain which way the body is oriented in space, works by detecting changes in motion." German does this naturally with commas. Keep it. Do not introduce Gedankenstriche to tidy the sentence.

It ends paragraphs on a short sentence that lands, and it is not a summary. "He trusted his body." "The bolognese is burning. Neither of them can smell it." "Nothing is moving." The German paragraph must end on the same short sentence, and it must not be lengthened or softened.

Its humour is dry and made of ordinary nouns: "a comparison that favours the vending machine by a ratio of approximately fifty-seven to one", "the ancient legal principle of having touched it", "litigating breakfast with a procedural limb", "a manager who expresses dissatisfaction through politeness". The German joke is in the precision, never in a jokey word. Do not add a wink. Do not make it jollier than the English.

It refuses to overclaim, on almost every page. "In all likelihood." "Libby's father was wrong about the specific mechanism, but he was not wrong about the principle." "This is a spectrum, not a switch." "The mechanism is the same, but the effect size is not." "Not every late night is a state error. Some are situations." "This book is not going to pretend she can." "The model knows its limits." Every one of these hedges is load-bearing. The Scientific Heartbeat at the back is a list of exactly where the chapters say more than their sources, written in the first person. If a German sentence is stronger than its English, it is wrong.

It talks to the reader as an equal and is sometimes blunt: "Do not use this for gaslighting yourself." "Call it what it is, which is maintenance." German can be as direct. Do not add politeness particles (doch, mal, ruhig) to soften an instruction the English gives straight.

It repeats words on purpose. "His body was telling him something different. His body was telling him he was flying straight and level. His body felt right. His instruments felt wrong." Repetition is the rhythm. German editors are trained to vary; here they must not. Repeat the word.

## Glossary

The book is built on a small set of terms it uses hundreds of times. The German choice for each is fixed below, with the alternatives I rejected and why, and where consistency must hold. Terms in the same family are grouped.

### state, situation

state: Zustand. situation: Situation. The pair "State. Not situation." in running prose (the book uses it lowercase, as a sentence, in several chapters) is "Zustand. Nicht Situation." and the CASE EVIDENCE line "This is state, not situation." is "Das ist Zustand, nicht Situation." Rejected: Verfassung (too formal, and it also means constitution), Lage for situation (Lage is right for "the position of the body in space" and for a military situation, but the book's word is Situation and German has it). Consistency: absolute. Wherever the book contrasts the two, the German uses these two words and no synonym. Where the English uses "situation" in its ordinary sense, as in "pilots who find themselves in this situation", still use Situation; the echo is deliberate in the English and should stay audible.

The book title itself is never translated. It is "State. Not Situation." in every German sentence, including the sentence "State. Not Situation. ist ihr erstes Buch."

### reading, read, misread, first reading, second reading, reading mode

This is the hardest term in the book and the one place where German cannot carry everything in one word. In English "reading" is at once what an instrument shows (a meter reading), the act of reading a text or a person or a room, and an interpretation (a reading of events). The book fuses all three on purpose: "It feels like you are reading the situation. But you are only reading the instrument that is reading the situation."

The noun: Befund. "What you found is a reading." is "Was Sie gefunden haben, ist ein Befund." "Your first reading is not the whole story." is "Ihr erster Befund ist nicht die ganze Geschichte." "Three readings" is "Drei Befunde". "Next reading: 06:38." is "Nächster Befund: 06:38." Reasons. Befund is a word every German adult knows from the doctor and says aloud. It is what was found, not what it means; German lay usage already separates Befund (the finding) from Diagnose (the verdict), which is exactly the distinction the book draws between a reading and a verdict, between reading mode and narrating mode. It echoes "gefunden" in the sentence that introduces it. And it holds across the website wherever the reading is the result of the check or of the panel. It does not hold where the English hangs a genitive on it ("her first reading of what is happening"): there the German never says "Befund von etwas" or "Befund dessen, was"; it either drops the object where the sentence already names the situation ("ihr erster Befund") or goes to the verb (ablesen for the panel, lesen for a text, a room, a person). See the amendments below.

Rejected for the noun: Ablesung (correct for a meter, but a word from the utilities office; it fails on a book cover and on a person), Messung and Messwert (an act and a number; the book's reading is qualitative, "jaw tight, breathing high", and you do not measure a situation), Lesart (interpretation only; it fails on the instrument), Lesung (a literary event), Anzeige (also a police report and an advertisement), Wert (a number).

The verbs stay German verbs. Reading the panel, the dial, the instrument: ablesen. "He did not read it. He read his body instead." is "aber er las sie nicht ab. Er las stattdessen seinen Körper." Reading a message, a room, a face, a person: lesen. "Reading mode" and "narrating mode" are Lesemodus and Erzählmodus. "Take the reading again" (back cover) is "Lesen Sie noch einmal ab."

Where the English "reading" means an interpretation and there is no instrument in the sentence, Lesart is allowed: "resistant to alternative readings" is "unzugänglich für andere Lesarten". Use this sparingly and never for the panel.

misread, misreading: Fehldeutung. The legend "Threat detection, first drafts, misreads" is "Bedrohungserkennung, erste Entwürfe, Fehldeutungen". Rejected: Fehlbefund (exists, but rare and clinical), Verleser (too cute).

Consistency must hold at every place the book names what the panel gives you, at the state-line headings (READING / 01 becomes BEFUND / 01), on the cover strap, in the case panels and in the halfway and closing maps ("Ten Readings" becomes "Zehn Befunde", "Five Readings" becomes "Fünf Befunde").

### instrument, panel, dashboard, cockpit, settings

instrument: Instrument. instrument panel and panel: Instrumentenbrett. dashboard: die Instrumente, or das Instrumentenbrett; never Armaturenbrett (amended under the standard, see below: German gives the car an Armaturenbrett and the aircraft an Instrumentenbrett, and the book's image is the cockpit). "Read the dashboard." is "Lesen Sie die Instrumente ab."; "your dashboard reading" (page 21) is "was Ihre Instrumente anzeigen". cockpit: Cockpit. settings: Einstellungen. "Same life. Different instrument settings." is "Dasselbe Leben. Andere Einstellungen am Instrument." "We just brought the wrong settings." (Marc, page 93) is "Wir hatten nur die falschen Einstellungen dabei."

Rejected: Panel (in German a discussion panel), Armaturenbrett anywhere (a car; the founding image is a plane, and Instrumentenbrett is the pilot's word), Anzeigetafel (a scoreboard), Konfiguration for settings (a technical word; the book's word is the one on a phone). "The Clean Panel" (chapter 12) is "Das saubere Instrumentenbrett"; "a warm panel" is "ein warmes Instrumentenbrett". The idiom "alles im grünen Bereich" is tempting for the clean panel and the book even says "Green across the panel", so use it for that sentence, but keep the chapter title literal so that clean and warm remain a pair.

### signal, story, narrator, first draft, weather

signal: Signal. story: Geschichte. "The body speaks first. The mind explains second." is "Der Körper spricht zuerst. Der Kopf erklärt danach." narrator (the default mode network as narrator): der Erzähler. narrating: erzählen. first draft (the body's first draft): der erste Entwurf. weather (the body's weather, "its own weather and its own stories", "climate rather than weather"): Wetter, Klima. "The body's confident weather report about the world is only a draft." is "Der selbstsichere Wetterbericht, den der Körper über die Welt abgibt, ist nur ein Entwurf."

Rejected: Erzählung for story (a literary word; the book's story is the one you tell yourself, and that is a Geschichte), Narrativ (jargon), Rohfassung for first draft (a manuscript word; Entwurf is what a person says).

### mind, brain, body

mind: Kopf. brain: Gehirn. body: Körper. "your mind often treats those signals as truth" is "Ihr Kopf hält diese Signale oft für die Wahrheit." Kopf is the word Germans use when they oppose it to the body or the gut (Kopf gegen Bauch). Rejected: Verstand (reason, too narrow), Geist (spirit), Psyche (clinical), Bewusstsein (consciousness, a different claim). Where the English says "brain" it means the organ and the German is Gehirn; do not let Kopf and Gehirn swap places, because the book uses them for different things.

### the three systems: Time, Attention, Safety; loop; cue; checking

Time, Attention, Safety: Zeit, Aufmerksamkeit, Sicherheit. Capitalised as names when the book capitalises them ("the Safety loop" becomes "die Sicherheitsschleife" or "die Schleife Sicherheit"; prefer the compound). loop: Schleife. "the checking loop" is "die Nachseh-Schleife" in a heading and "die Schleife des Nachsehens" in prose; in narrative German the verb is "nachsehen" or, colloquially and correctly for a phone, "checken". The legend "Reward loops, checking, task switching" is "Belohnungsschleifen, Nachsehen, Aufgabenwechsel". cue: Auslöser. "The Predictable Cue" is "Der vorhersehbare Auslöser". Rejected: Reiz for cue (a stimulus, which is a different thing from the trigger of a habit), Hinweisreiz (textbook), Kreislauf for loop (a circulation, and Teufelskreis adds a moral the book refuses), Kontrolle for checking (control, a different sense).

### supply chain, open file, tab, the gap, upstream

supply chain: Lieferkette. "Run the supply chain." is "Gehen Sie die Lieferkette durch." open file: offene Akte; "The Open File" is "Die offene Akte". to file, filed under: ablegen, abgelegt unter. "Filed under toast" is "unter Toast abgelegt". tab (the browser tab as image for a running background process): Tab, which German has. the gap (between signal and story, "You have the gap. You have the time."): die Lücke. upstream (the upstream point, the first domino): the German is "weiter oben", "stromaufwärts" only if the sentence is about a river; "the upstream point" is "der Punkt weiter oben in der Kette" or, where the book says "the first domino", "der erste Dominostein".

### case evidence, verified, input, investigation

CASE EVIDENCE: BEWEISMATERIAL. VERIFIED EVENT, VERIFIED TONE, VERIFIED CRISIS, VERIFIED THREAT, VERIFIED MONDAY: Bestätigtes Ereignis, Bestätigter Tonfall, Bestätigte Krise, Bestätigte Bedrohung, Bestätigter Montag. NEXT READING: Nächster Befund. INPUT: Input, which German uses. The Investigation (the map title): Die Untersuchung. Rejected: verifiziert (a software word), gesichert (established, a different claim), Ermittlung for the investigation (police only; the book's frame is the accident investigation, NTSB, Kemeny Commission, and the German word for that is Untersuchung).

### confidence marker, evidence, high, medium, low

The book's confidence marker is a heartbeat graphic with three levels. confidence marker: in prose, "eine Markierung, die zeigt, wie gut die Aussage belegt ist"; as a label, Belegstärke. evidence, in the research sense: Belege. replicated: wiederholt bestätigt. robust: belastbar. High, Medium, Low: Hoch, Mittel, Niedrig. "Replicated, robust evidence." is "Wiederholt bestätigt, belastbar." "Suggestive but incomplete." is "Ein Hinweis, aber unvollständig." "Plausible hypothesis only." is "Nur eine plausible Vermutung." Rejected: Evidenz (jargon outside evidenzbasiert), Konfidenzmarker (statistics), Beweis for evidence (a proof, which is more than the book claims).

### depleted, fatigue, exhaustion, resourced

depleted: erschöpft; a depleted system is "ein erschöpftes System"; depletion: Erschöpfung. fatigue: Müdigkeit. exhaustion: Erschöpfung. "resourced" (as in "when both of you are resourced"): "wenn Sie beide bei Kräften sind". Rejected: ausgelaugt (colloquial and a bit dramatic), entleert (a tank, which is the model the book explicitly dropped in chapter 7), leer (too vague).

### other recurring words

toast and fire (the smoke detector): Toast und Feuer; "Most of the time it is toast." is "Meistens ist es Toast." forecast: Vorhersage. warm, clean (of the panel): warm, sauber. calibrated, miscalibrated: kalibriert, falsch kalibriert (the word is in everyday German through scales and screens). phantom load: Phantomlast, which German electricians also say. soft fascination: sanfte Faszination. rumination: Grübeln; ruminating: grübeln. rest, restoration: Ruhe (or Ausruhen), Erholung. "Rest without restoration" is "Ruhe ohne Erholung". extract (of the book): Leseprobe; audio extract: Hörprobe. before we begin: Bevor wir anfangen. tool (the book's word for its own method, "a tool, not a leash", "Put the tool down", "a sorting tool"): Werkzeug, never Instrument, which is reserved for the body. texture (page 12 and throughout): Textur. heaviness: Schwere. "Low fuel" on the page 182 card is Katrin's fuel (page 185: "Long day, low fuel, residual charge, bad lighting"), so "Wenig Treibstoff", which a German reader can take for the car as an English reader can; "grit" there is the floor of an underground car park, so Splitt, not Kies.

## How to handle the reader address

The book says "you" in two ways, and German has two words for them.

When the book speaks to the reader, use Sie. This is every imperative ("Check your jaw." is "Spüren Sie Ihren Kiefer.", see the amendment on interoceptive instructions), every "as you read this sentence", every "your life", "your career", "your phone", every test and experiment, every "you have the gap". The reader is an adult stranger and the book never uses "du"; the German never does either.

When "you" is generic, when it could be replaced by "one" or "anyone" without changing the sentence, use man. "You do not notice the horizon is gone until you look for it and it is not there" describes any pilot in that cockpit: "man merkt gar nicht, dass der Horizont weg ist, bis man ihn sucht und er nicht da ist." "When you enter a turn, the fluid inside the ear shifts" is "Geht man in eine Kurve, verschiebt sich die Flüssigkeit im Ohr."

The test: would the author, reading the German aloud, be pointing at the reader or describing how bodies work? If pointing, Sie. If describing, man. Within one paragraph the book often moves from one to the other, and the German should follow it, not flatten to one.

Do not mix them inside one sentence. If a sentence starts generic and ends on the reader, recast it so it is one or the other, or split it.

Where the book uses "we" ("what becomes possible when we learn where our first reading came from"), use wir.

"I" in the Scientific Heartbeat is the author and stays ich.

Never use gendered doubling in the book's own prose (Leserinnen und Leser) unless the English itself names readers as a group; on the website "readers interested in" is "Leserinnen und Leser, die sich interessieren", because there it is a group, not the reader.

## Sentence rhythm rules

The book's rhythm is variance on purpose. A forty-word sentence with three appositions sits next to a four-word one. A paragraph opens on a long description and closes on "He trusted his body." German has a tendency, in editors and in translators, to even this out. Do not.

Keep the short sentences short. If the English is three words, the German is three or four words, not seven. "The turn tightened. The nose dropped. The airspeed built." is "Die Kurve wurde enger. Die Nase senkte sich. Die Fahrt nahm zu." If a German verb-final construction makes a short sentence long, choose another verb.

Keep the long sentences long. The book's long sentences are long because they stack detail, and the stacking is the point ("the tram takes twenty-two minutes to Bonnevoie and he will check his email between four and seven times during that ride, which he knows because Katrin once watched him do it and told him"). German nests happily. Where a nested relative clause in German would push the verb so far out that the reader loses the thread, split into two sentences at the point where the English pauses with a comma, and let the second sentence start with the noun the first one ended on. That is what a German writer does.

Fragments are allowed wherever the book uses them. "Not rested, not destroyed, just slightly less than the minimum her system needs to run cleanly." "Jaw. Breath. Shoulders." "Rested. Fed. Calm." "Done." German has no rule against fragments in prose of this kind; keep them as fragments.

Short after long. When a paragraph ends on a short sentence, the German paragraph ends on a short sentence. Do not move the short sentence into the middle to make the ending "flow".

Do not add connectives. The book rarely says "however", "therefore", "moreover". German translators add "jedoch", "daher", "zudem" out of habit. Every added connective is a change in tone toward the essay and away from the book.

Do not add a summary sentence at the end of a paragraph that has none. Some paragraphs stop. "The tab multiplies in the dark." is an ending.

Timestamps stay as the book sets them: 06:38, 22:47, 15:28, with a colon and no "Uhr" in the panels and section heads; in running prose "um 06:38" without "Uhr" as well, because the book uses the timestamp as an object, like a reading.

## Idiom policy

Sense over image. When an English idiom has a German idiom that does the same work, use the German one and let the image change. "Two nervous systems walk into a kitchen" is a joke opener, and the German joke opener is "Kommen zwei Nervensysteme in eine Küche". "Something is off" is "Irgendetwas stimmt nicht". "Charging rent" (an unanswered message starts charging rent) can stay "fängt an, Miete zu verlangen" because German readers get it and it is the book's own oddness; but "the bill for the evening that felt free" is "die Rechnung für den Abend, der sich frei anfühlte", which is already German.

When the English image is one of the book's load-bearing images, keep the image even if a German idiom is nearer to hand. The cockpit, the instrument panel, the settings, the weather, the smoke detector and toast, the open file, the supply chain, the treadmill and the road, the Skinner box, the phantom load, the graveyard spiral: these recur across chapters and the German reader must meet the same image each time. "Friedhofsspirale" is the German aviation term for graveyard spiral and it is as startling in German as in English; use it, not "Todesspirale", which is also a word from economics.

When the English has no idiom and is simply plain, do not supply a German idiom to make it livelier. "The dinner was not ruined by the phone, but it was thinner." is "Das Essen wurde durch das Handy nicht verdorben, aber es wurde dünner." Keep "dünner"; it is the book's word, not a stock phrase.

Units. Where the book quotes a number from a record, keep the number and the unit: "more than 4,700 feet per minute" is "mit mehr als 4.700 Fuß pro Minute". Where the book converts a number to make it vivid for an English reader ("nearly a mile every sixty seconds", "six inches from his eyes"), convert it for a German reader with the same accuracy: "fast anderthalb Kilometer in sechzig Sekunden", "fünfzehn Zentimeter vor seinen Augen". Where a unit is a specification (the book's trim size, 6 × 9 in), keep the specification and add the metric in parentheses.

Names of studies, institutions and people stay as they are. "Federal Aviation Administration" stays, with "die amerikanische Luftfahrtbehörde" placed before it the first time so the German reader knows what it is; that is an explanation the English reader does not need and the German one does, and it adds no claim.

Luxembourg is Luxemburg in German prose. Place names inside Luxembourg stay as they are (Grund, Kirchberg, Bonnevoie, Belair, Pétrusse, Remich, Schengen, Cloche d'Or, Cactus). French words the book leaves in French (le calme absolu, cave coopérative, en bas, tout de suite) stay in French.

## What must never change

Claims. The German sentence may not claim more or less than the English. Watch especially for German verbs that are more definite than the English ("beweisen" for "suggest", "zeigen" for "found that", "immer" for "often", "nie" for "rarely").

Hedges and qualifications. "In all likelihood", "probably", "roughly", "approximately", "at least partly", "not by a lot, but just enough", "most of the time", "once in a while", "I think": every one of them is reproduced. Where German would naturally drop "ungefähr" because the number already reads as approximate, keep it anyway.

The book's refusals. "The model knows its limits." "This book is not going to pretend she can." "This chapter is not equipped for that work." "The sentence stands as written and it says the opposite of its source." These are the author on the record about her own book. They are translated exactly, in her voice, in the first person where she uses it.

The title. "State. Not Situation." untranslated, everywhere, including the subtitle line "State. Not Situation. ist ihr erstes Buch." and the running head "State. Not Situation".

Proper nouns. Ivana Budišin, Budisin Publishing, Katrin, Marc, David, Léo, Mila, Antoine, Sophie, John F. Kennedy Jr, Martha's Vineyard, New Jersey, Bibliothèque nationale du Luxembourg, Zoe Larusson, and every researcher's name and every study title in the Scientific Heartbeat. Reference entries in the Heartbeat are not translated at all; only the author's lines under them are.

Numbers. ISBN 978-2-87996-258-0, every page number, every timestamp, every count in the book (2,617 touches, 290 passengers, forty-four seconds, one hundred and fifteen afternoons), every year. Thousands separators may follow German convention (4.700) because the value is unchanged.

On the website specifically: file paths, the values of the keys key, chapter, page, number, file and time, and any string containing "[COPY NEEDED:" stays byte for byte in English, so nobody mistakes an unwritten line for a translation.

Chapter and section titles are translated once and reused identically wherever they appear (the map on page 11, the halfway and closing maps, the end note under the excerpt, the Scientific Heartbeat headings).

## Typography

German quotation marks: „ “ for quotations, ‚ ‘ for a quotation inside a quotation. The book's inner-voice italics ("I am waiting for..." set in italics in the English) are kept as italics in the typeset German, not turned into quotation marks.

ß where standard German has it (Straße, groß, schließen, außerhalb); ss where the rule gives ss (dass, Fluss, muss). Swiss spelling is not used.

Nouns capitalised. Nominalised adjectives capitalised (etwas Tägliches, das Ganze).

Sie, Ihr, Ihnen capitalised for the reader throughout.

No em dashes and no en dashes as sentence punctuation. The book does not use them and German prose of this register does not need them. Where a range is set in figures (pages 7 to 9), write "7 bis 9" in prose and use the en dash only in reference lists, which are not translated.

Numbers up to twelve in words in prose, figures above, except timestamps, ages the book gives in figures, counts the book gives in figures, and page references. Follow the English on the choice between figures and words where the English is itself deliberate ("one hundred and fifteen afternoons" is in words; keep words).

Decimal comma, thousands point: 4.700 Fuß, 15,2 cm.

Times: 06:38, not 6.38 Uhr, not 6:38.

Section headings in the book are set in title case in English (The Itch After the Scratch). German headings capitalise nouns only and are otherwise sentence case: "Das Jucken nach dem Kratzen".

## Verify against yourself

Run this over any finished passage before it goes to review. Do it against the English, sentence by sentence, then once more reading only the German aloud.

1. Claims. For each sentence, is there any idea, image, example or qualification in the German that is not in the English? Is there any in the English that is missing from the German? Count both. The count must be zero.

2. Hedges. List every hedge in the English passage (probably, roughly, sometimes, in all likelihood, not by a lot, at least, I think). Find each one in the German. Any hedge without a German counterpart is a defect.

3. Strength of verbs. Where the English says "suggests", "found", "showed", "is associated with", check the German verb is no stronger.

4. Glossary. Search the passage for every load-bearing term and confirm the fixed German word was used: Befund for the noun reading, ablesen for the panel, lesen for the message or the room, Zustand, Situation, Instrument, Instrumentenbrett, Einstellungen, Signal, Geschichte, Erzähler, Entwurf, Lieferkette, Auslöser, Schleife, offene Akte, Zeit, Aufmerksamkeit, Sicherheit, Belege, bestätigt, Beweismaterial. Any synonym used for one of these is a defect unless this document explicitly allows it.

5. Address. Mark every Sie and every man. For each, ask whether the English is pointing at the reader or describing how bodies work. Any sentence that mixes the two is recast.

6. Rhythm. Count sentence lengths in the English paragraph and in the German paragraph. The shortest German sentence should be about as short as the shortest English one, and the longest about as long. If the German sentences are all of a similar length, something was evened out; put the variance back. Check that the paragraph ends on the same sentence in both languages, and that no summary was added.

7. Repetition. Where the English repeats a word (body, body, body; reading, reading), the German repeats it. Any rotation of synonyms introduced by the translator is reverted.

8. Connectives. Count "jedoch", "daher", "zudem", "allerdings", "somit", "dabei" in the German and check each one against the English. Remove any that has no English counterpart.

9. Register. Read the German aloud. Does it sound like a psychologist talking to an adult, or like a manual, or like a novel? The book is the first. Any sentence that sounds like a manual gets a plainer verb; any sentence that sounds like a novel gets its adjective removed.

10. Tell recount. Go through the German with the detection catalogue in hand and count. Vocabulary: entscheidend, zentral, komplex, Zusammenspiel, Landschaft used abstractly, Zeugnis, unterstreichen, hervorheben, fördern, robust used of anything but evidence, Bereich as "realm", Reise used abstractly, nutzen as "leverage", verbessern, aufzeigen, betonen, and "zusätzlich" at the start of a sentence. Syntax: "dient als", "fungiert als", "stellt dar" where "ist" or "hat" would do; trailing participle clauses that analyse ("was die Bedeutung von ... unterstreicht"); "nicht nur X, sondern auch Y" and "es ist nicht X, es ist Y" where the English has no such contrast; triads added for rhythm; synonym rotation. Structure: significance inflation ("ein entscheidender Moment"), didactic disclaimers ("es ist wichtig zu beachten"), summary endings, vague attribution ("Experten sagen"). Typography: dashes, title case, boldface. Rhythm: uniform sentence length, every paragraph the same shape. Write the count down. A single instance proves nothing; density does. ## Amendments under the author's standard

Date: 5 September 2026, on receipt of STANDARD.md. The standard governs. Where this method disagreed, the following now applies, and the glossary above is read together with the translation memory at the end of this document.

1. Interoceptive instructions. "Check your jaw." is "Spüren Sie Ihren Kiefer.", not "Prüfen Sie Ihren Kiefer." Prüfen inspects for a fault; the task is noticing. The same for breath and shoulders. "Notice your jaw" (page 21) is "Achten Sie auf Ihren Kiefer."; "Do not fix it. Just read it." keeps ablesen.

2. Cognitive cost. Never billig or teuer for cognitive or metabolic cost, and never günstig or kostspielig either. Translate the effort. "the cheapest available input" is "das, was gerade da ist und am wenigsten Mühe macht"; "the expensive work" is "die Arbeit, die einem viel abverlangt". Where the book makes its cost model explicit ("metabolically expensive", "a cost-benefit computation with dopamine on both sides"), the German is aufwendig, metabolisch aufwendig, kostet Energie, Kosten und Nutzen: native cost words that carry the mechanism without the shop. The monetary pair is dropped everywhere, so the reader never meets it once and misses it later. Standard's metaphor class C: the mechanism is translated, not the image.

3. Compounds. Do not manufacture a compound because German allows it. "baseline sensitivity" is "das Grundniveau der Empfindlichkeit", never Grundempfindlichkeit. "baseline" itself is Grundniveau throughout the book (dopamine falls "unter das Grundniveau"; "your default baseline", page 181, is "Ihr Grundniveau"; "that is a baseline", page 192, is "das ist ein Grundniveau"); "resting baseline" is Ruheniveau; the loan word Baseline is not used. "kitchen scale" is "im Maßstab einer Küche", not Küchenmaßstab. "lifestyle problems" is "Fragen des Lebensstils". "two-line email" is "eine zweizeilige E-Mail", which is an ordinary adjective, not a compound. Before any new compound, ask the standard's three questions: established or naturally interpretable; would a German psychology writer use it; does a simpler phrase exist.

4. English syntax under German words. "Three systems ran through Katrin's morning." is "An Katrins Morgen waren drei Systeme am Werk." The English "ran" is the book's process image (page 18: "This is time, and it is already running in the background"); "am Werk sein" is the German idiom for something operating unseen, and it keeps the force of the paragraph, which is that they, not David, did the work. "Drei Systeme bestimmten Katrins Morgen" was rejected because it makes the systems the cause of the morning rather than processes running inside it; "liefen durch Katrins Morgen" was the English sentence in German clothes. In the same paragraph "Attention took the phone" is "griff nach dem Handy", "Safety wrote the email's meaning" is "schrieb, was die E-Mail bedeutete". The general rule: translate the argument, then choose the German order; never keep the English order because it is available.

5. Dashboard. Armaturenbrett is withdrawn from the glossary. German gives the car an Armaturenbrett and the aircraft an Instrumentenbrett; the book's image is the cockpit. "Read the dashboard." (page 278) is "Lesen Sie die Instrumente ab."

6. Befund, tested slot by slot under the standard's collocation and precision tests. It passes where the reading is the result of the check or of the panel: "Was Sie gefunden haben, ist ein Befund.", "Der Befund kann stimmen. Oder auch nicht.", "Ihr erster Befund ist nicht die ganze Geschichte.", "Drei Befunde", "Derselbe Tag, fünf Befunde", "woher unser erster Befund kam". A Befund is what an examination found, not what it means, and every German adult knows it can be revised; that is the book's distinction between reading and verdict. It fails where the English hangs a genitive on it: "a reading of the instrument that is interpreting everything around you" is "Ein Befund, abgelesen an dem Instrument, das ..."; "her first reading of what is happening" is "ihr erster Befund", the object carried by "Situationen" in the same sentence. Where "reading" is the first read-through of a text (page 19: "The first reading is informational. The second reading is interpretive.") the German is verbal: "Beim ersten Lesen nimmt sie die Information auf. Beim zweiten deutet sie." Alt text describing printed dials uses Anzeigen. Decision: Befund stays, with these recasts recorded in the translation memory.

7. Kopf for mind, tested slot by slot. It passes every slot on the website: "Der Kopf erklärt danach.", "Ihr Kopf hält diese Signale oft für die Wahrheit.", "bevor Ihr Kopf daraus eine Geschichte macht", "Ihr Kopf redigiert diese Geschichte". It is the everyday German word for the faculty a German opposes to the body, which is what the English "mind" is doing in this book; Verstand would narrow it to reason and Geist would raise it. The rule that "brain" is always Gehirn and the two never swap stands. Decision: Kopf stays.

8. Headlines and interface copy. Headlines are written as headlines: two or three candidates generated, one chosen, none shown. Interface copy follows German publishing-site convention and is shorter than prose: Leseprobe in the navigation, "Leseprobe lesen" on the button, Weiterlesen, "Benachrichtigen lassen", "Bibliografische Angaben", Umschlaggestaltung. A button is never derived from its heading.

9. Subtitle. "A field guide" is "Ein Bestimmungsbuch". Bestimmungsbuch is the German name for the thing an English reader calls a field guide, and bestimmen (to identify, to determine) is what the book asks the reader to do with a state. Decided under amendment 25 on 6 September 2026; Feldführer is withdrawn and the query is closed.

10. Workflow. Every section goes through the standard's ten passes first; this document's "Verify against yourself" runs after pass 10 and before the second reader. The tell recount (item 10 there) now also counts the standard's red flags: more formal, more emotional, more therapeutic, more promotional than the English.

11. Metaphor classification for the website passages, under the standard's four types.

Type A, works in both languages, preserved: the instruments, the panel, the settings ("verstellt" for settings that are off); the body speaks first and the mind explains second; the weather report and the draft; the graveyard spiral (Friedhofsspirale); rainy weather behind glass; an email that reads like a verdict (Urteil); a brake (Bremse); the story the body writes and the mind edits (schreibt, redigiert); texture (Textur); the heartbeat markers; the day that "started before you noticed it starting"; "the morning after will make the story dissolve" (auflösen); "tidier than it is" (aufgeräumter); "a sorting tool" (ein Werkzeug zum Sortieren); "the gap between fine and clinical" (die Lücke).

Type C, confusing or comic if kept, mechanism translated: "cheapest available input" and "expensive work" (effort, see 2); "captured" attention is kept as eingefangen, which German psychology also says, but "when it is captured" loses the passive-agent structure: "Ist es eingefangen, verengt sich der Fokus".

Type D, central to the book, preserved with adjustment where needed: state and situation; reading (Befund) against the instrument (ablesen) and the text or room (lesen); signal against story; the instrument panel and its settings; the investigation frame (Untersuchung, Beweismaterial, bestätigt); toast and fire; the open file; the supply chain. Where these recur, the German reader meets the same word each time.

## Amendments after the review of the website, 6 September 2026

Two independent reviews of content/de.ts, one reading only the German and one reading both languages, found one class of error the method had not covered and several places where the method's own memory had not been followed. The following now applies to the whole book, not only to the website. It is read together with the amendments of 5 September and the memory above.

12. Evidence is never Beweis, in any slot. The memory already rejected Beweis for the research sense; it is now rejected for the everyday sense too, so that "presents itself as evidence that your relationship is failing" is "als Beleg dafür, dass ...". The reason is on page 26: "the mind treats the feeling as proof of the situation and not simply evidence about the state". The book keeps proof and evidence apart inside one sentence, and German keeps Beweis and Beleg apart everywhere so that the page 26 sentence still has two words to use. Beweismaterial for the CASE EVIDENCE heading is untouched; that is the investigation frame, not the epistemic pair.

13. The memory's worked sentences are binding. "Die Fahrt nahm zu." was recorded under the rhythm rules and the file had "Die Geschwindigkeit nahm zu.", which lengthened the third beat of a three-beat run, broke the link to Fahrtmesser in the paragraph above and repeated a word the English does not repeat ("the airspeed built" and "at full speed" are different words). Where this document records a sentence verbatim, that sentence is the translation.

14. Pronoun chains and pro-forms. German "er" cannot be handed a new referent after three "er" for a person. "Beim Start war der Himmel klar, also schloss er daraus, dass er keine Instrumente brauchte. Als er die Küste erreichte, war der Himmel es nicht mehr." The noun comes back and the predicate stays elided as "es", which is what the English elides. Count masculine referents in any passage where two of them run together.

15. Object before the prepositional list. English can put the objects of a preposition before the direct object ("writes a story about a message, a silence, a look"); German stacks four accusatives with no boundary and the reader stops. The direct object goes first: "in der Ihr Körper eine Geschichte über eine Nachricht, ein Schweigen, einen Blick schreibt".

16. An explanatory gloss never goes inside a discontinuous frame. The gloss "die amerikanische Luftfahrtbehörde" before "Federal Aviation Administration", required by the idiom section, must not be dropped between a noun and its own preposition ("Die Anweisung der ..., der ..., an Piloten, die ..., ist einen Satz lang"). Give the authority the verb and let the short sentence land after it: "Die amerikanische Luftfahrtbehörde, die Federal Aviation Administration, hat eine Anweisung für Piloten, die in diese Situation geraten. Sie ist einen Satz lang." Splitting is licensed here although the English does not pause, because the weight is created by a gloss the English does not carry.

17. The data does not speak. Where the book gives one side of a contrast a speech verb and withholds it from the other ("It said I am failing; the data was I am tired"), German keeps the asymmetry with a copula and a colon: "Es hieß: Ich versage. Die Daten waren: Ich bin müde." Radar, instruments and panels may say things where the book says they do (page 25, "The radar said the aircraft was ascending"); the rule is per sentence, not per noun.

18. No intensifier the English does not have. "diese Erklärung fühlte sich an wie das Ereignis" takes no "selbst". The German particles that raise a deliberately flat sentence (selbst, geradezu, regelrecht, förmlich) are added nowhere, and the paragraph-final sentence is the place they creep in.

19. Word order in subordinate clauses. With a full noun-phrase subject the reflexive stands directly after the conjunction: "Warum sich der Abend dehnt", not "Warum der Abend sich dehnt". Both are grammatical; the second is marked and puts a stress the English title does not have. In a chapter title, read at a glance in a list of sixteen, the unmarked order is the one that reads.

20. Concessive correlatives are not additions. Where the English has "suggestive but incomplete" inside a conditional, German writes zwar ... aber. The zwar adds no claim; it is the concession the English "but" already carries, in the frame German uses for it.

21. Verbs keep the objects German gives them. revidieren takes Deutung, Urteil, Einschätzung, Annahme, never die Welt. folgen goes after something that moves; verfolgen is what a chapter does to a moment. Where the English hangs one object on three verbs, German names the object each verb can take, and the adjustment is recorded rather than made silently. Amended by amendment 31: it is recorded in the translation memory as a decision, and goes to the author only where the object German must supply would change the argument.

22. In a rights register, "extract licensing" is Abdruckrechte on its own. Leseprobe stays the word everywhere the extract is offered to a reader, and Auszug is not used at all. "Abdruckrechte für Auszüge, Interviews und Veranstaltungen" offers reprint rights for interviews and events, which the English does not.

23. The reader's Sie owns the first position. In a book that addresses the reader as Sie, a sentence may not open with a capitalised "Sie" that means anything else. The capital is unrecoverable, the polite Sie takes the same plural verb as a plural pronoun, and the reader has usually just been addressed in the sentence before. "Sie liefern in diesem Moment Daten, während Sie diesen Satz lesen" makes the instruments into the reader and then confirms the misreading with a second Sie in the same sentence; the English "They are producing data right now, as you read this sentence" cannot be misread. Two repairs, in this order: move another element into first position so the pronoun falls after the verb and stays lowercase ("In diesem Moment liefern sie Daten, während Sie diesen Satz lesen"), or bring the noun back, which amendment 14 already licenses ("Diese Systeme sind ein Werkzeug zum Sortieren"). Never leave it to the verb to sort out, because in these sentences the verb agrees with both readings. Check every sentence-initial Sie in any passage that also addresses the reader.

24. A fronted case-ambiguous constituent is read as the subject. Keines davon, das, nichts, alles, and every neuter noun phrase are the same in the nominative and the accusative, so German takes the first constituent for the subject unless something else forbids it. "Keines davon kann sie benennen" therefore reads as "None of them can name her", and it reads that way harder when the three sentences before it have just made those systems the agents (Zeit fing an, Aufmerksamkeit griff nach dem Handy, Sicherheit schrieb) and the person is carried only by a genitive attribute (Katrins Morgen). The topicalisation is grammatical and German uses it constantly; it is not available where the passage has primed the other parse. Put the subject first: "Sie kann keines davon benennen." Same length, same information, and the singular verb also rules out the polite Sie.

## Queries closed by the translator, 2026-09-06

The author's addendum to STANDARD.md of 6 September 2026, TRANSLATION QUERY FORMAT, sets the threshold: where one option is clearly superior after analysis the translator decides it, and a query goes to the author only where the choice could materially change meaning, metaphor, scientific precision, characterisation or the conceptual architecture of the book. Ten questions stood in QUERIES-de.md. Eight are decided below as amendments 25 to 32, whole-book rules like the ones before them; two remain in QUERIES-de.md, the lower pole of "the gap between fine and clinical" and the source query about "the other kind".

Each of the eight decisions is the wording content/de.ts already carried, so no string was edited in this pass and the file is byte-identical to its state after the third-reader pass. What changed is the status of the wording: it is now a rule with a reason on the record, not a recommendation waiting for an answer.

25. The subtitle is "Ein Bestimmungsbuch für den Moment, bevor aus Deutung Wirklichkeit wird", in all three places it appears (meta description, hero subtitle, press facts). Feldführer is withdrawn. It is a calque that a German reader decodes through the English, which the standard's freedom-from-interference test rejects outright, and Bestimmungsbuch is the word a German publisher prints for the thing an English reader calls a field guide. The metaphor is identical in both options, so nothing conceptual turns on the choice; and the standard's headline rule, which asks for candidates to be generated internally and one chosen, puts display copy of this kind with the translator. The gift, that bestimmen is also what the book asks the reader to do with a state, is a bonus and not the reason.

26. Chapter twelve is "Das saubere Instrumentenbrett". "Ohne Befund" is withdrawn, and not on grounds of taste. Befund is the book's fixed noun for a reading, and a clean panel is a reading: the check ran, it came back green, and the chapter's argument is that a reading which finds nothing is still a reading and still earns no discount (page 181: "A loaded panel earns a heavy discount. A clean panel earns none. But no discount is not the same as confirmation."). A chapter called "Ohne Befund" says the opposite, that there was no reading, and it says it in the book's own load-bearing word. Beyond that, clean is one grade of three and the German must be able to say all three; sauber and warm are a pair the German reader meets repeatedly, in the meeting on page 199 where two panels are warm and in the sentence that gives Sophie a warm one, and ohne Befund pairs with nothing. The risk the query named, that a German reader hears physical cleanliness first, is a risk the English carries identically and is closed by the chapter itself. When chapter twelve is translated, the third grade, "a loaded panel", still needs an adjective that sits with sauber and warm; it does not appear on the website.

27. "Social inference" in the book description is "die Schlüsse, die wir über andere Menschen ziehen". Soziale Inferenz is withdrawn. The list it closes runs attention, prediction, memory, threat, uncertainty: five words that are technical terms in psychology and ordinary words to an English reader at the same time. "Social inference" is transparent to that reader in the same way; "soziale Inferenz" is opaque to a German one and would be the only journal term in a sentence written for a bookshop. The standard's rule against downgrading technical English is satisfied because no precision is lost: the German says exactly what social inference is. The phrase lengthens the last item of the list, which the English list also does.

28. "Fängt an, Miete zu verlangen" stands, and its relation to amendment 2 is settled here so that it is not reopened. Amendment 2 removes the monetary pair from cognitive and metabolic cost, so that billig and teuer never carry effort. The rent is not part of that frame. 29. The page 20 line is "Es hieß: Ich versage. Die Daten waren: Ich bin müde." Two rules, both already implied and now explicit. First, where a sentence lifted out of the book onto the site loses its antecedent (in the book "It" is the conclusion forming between the screen and her body), German cannot use a bare "Es sagte", which reads as a dropped subject; "Es hieß" is the German impersonal that stands without one, and it costs nothing, because "es hieß" is what German says when a verdict has been passed and nobody is named. Second, amendment 17 governs the rest: the data does not speak, so the copula and the colon carry the asymmetry the English draws by withholding a speech verb. "Die Daten sagten" is smoother and destroys the only contrast the line contains. The verbless third form, "Die Daten: Ich bin müde.", is the book's fragment habit but reads as a caption under the case cards, which is where the line sits.

30. "Ambient" is beiläufig, and the triad stays a triad: "als etwas Tägliches, Beiläufiges, Unsichtbares". The alternative, a relative clause saying that the error runs constantly in the background, was withdrawn on a concrete ground rather than a stylistic one: the English sentence already carries "constantly" twice ("constantly producing signals, which your mind is constantly reading or misreading"), and the clause would put a third ständig into it, which is an addition the English does not make and a repetition it does not have. It also turns a list into a subordinate clause in a sentence the English keeps tight. Beiläufig stands where allgegenwärtig was rejected: it says present without being attended to, and in a triad flanked by täglich and unsichtbar the reading "unimportant" is not available.

31. Where German grammar forbids one object to three verbs, the German names the object each verb can take, and the translator records it here rather than referring it. "Wie wir die Welt um uns herum wahrnehmen, deuten und unsere Deutung revidieren" stands. Revidieren takes Deutung, Urteil, Einschätzung, Annahme and not die Welt; the object German has to supply is the one the previous verb has just produced, so the German makes explicit what the English implies and adds no idea. "Neu deuten" was withdrawn because it makes the third act a second interpretation rather than a correction of the first, and the correction of a first reading is the book's architecture, not a synonym for interpreting again. The last clause of amendment 21 is amended accordingly: an adjustment of this class goes into the translation memory as a decision; it goes to the author only where the object German must supply would change the argument.
