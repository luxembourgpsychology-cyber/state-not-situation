# Production: format, length and what is on screen

For the series in `BRIEF.md`, under the visual rules of `IDENTITY.md`, with the values of
`DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`. Written 17 October 2026; a working draft until the author has read
it. Three decisions are taken and this builds on them: 16:9 recorded at 4K and framed so
a 9:16 crop comes from the same take; twelve to eighteen minutes with the verdict inside
the first thirty seconds; on screen only the host, a source band, a claim card, a verdict
card, the three questions, and nothing else moving.

## Format

The episode is delivered at 1920 by 1080, the frame every card in `IDENTITY.md` was drawn
for. It is recorded at 3840 by 2160 so a second picture comes from the same take: a 9:16
column 1215 pixels wide and the full 2160 high, scaled to 1080 by 1920, is the Short.

The framing makes that possible. The host sits in the left or right third of the frame,
never the centre, eyes about a third of the way down, clear headroom above. The crop
column is taped on the monitor so that everything she needs is inside it.

The source band does not survive the crop: it is full width with type at the left gutter,
and a column a third of the frame wide keeps a third of a citation. So the band is never
in the recording. It is composed at delivery resolution for each picture: on the master as
`IDENTITY.md` specifies, and on the Short re-set at the Short's rem (1080 wide is 27px per
rem, so BODY mono is 32px) and lifted above the region where YouTube draws the title,
channel and description. YouTube publishes no figure for that region; third-party
measurements put it near 300px from the bottom of a 1080 by 1920 frame and near 400px
with the description expanded ([adaptlypost](https://adaptlypost.com/en/blog/social-media-safe-zones-2026-complete-guide), [trymypost](https://www.trymypost.com/blog/social-media-safe-zones-templates-2026)).
The band's bottom edge sits 400px up on the Short; the cut is checked on a phone.

## Length and pacing

Fifteen minutes is the working length. A longer or shorter episode changes the evidence
movement and leaves the others alone.

The first thirty seconds carry the title card, under three seconds, the episode's own card,
then the host, who says the claim in one sentence and the verdict in one sentence, with
the verdict card as she says it. A viewer who leaves at thirty seconds has the answer.

The claim runs to 3:00: the claim as patients bring it, said without contempt, with the
claim card. The evidence runs to 9:00, six minutes, about eight hundred spoken words, the
number to write to; every factual sentence in it has a band.
The verdict is restated at 9:00, word for word, with the verdict card again, in forty-five
seconds, because the sentence was written first and needs no defending. The application
runs from 9:45 to about 14:15; the three questions card comes up as it begins and leaves
when she starts applying them. It ends on the better clinical answer.

The last forty-five seconds are the close and the end card. YouTube places end-screen
elements in the final five to twenty seconds of a video at least twenty-five seconds long
([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6388789)), so the end card holds
fifteen seconds and the elements sit in its cream.

## The on-screen grammar

Everything enters with the site's one transition, opacity and an 8px rise over 400ms,
once, then is still, and leaves on a cut. Nothing else animates and nothing is driven by
time or position. The one exception is the title card's mark, which may draw itself once.

The source band is a cream band across the foot, a rule of ink at 14% along its top, type
at the left gutter, 2rem padding above and below and no taller. One line of IBM Plex Mono
at BODY, 57px on the frame, ink, sentence case, in the `CITATIONS.md` form: author, year,
journal, volume. It appears on the first word of a sourced sentence, holds for the whole
claim it supports and never less than four seconds. It never appears under a sentence the
host marks as proposed; its absence is how the series separates shown from proposed, and
no colour replaces it. The host's name band shares the geometry, her name in the serif at
LEAD and CLINICAL PSYCHOLOGIST in mono at LABEL, quiet; the two are never up together.

The claim card is cream in the TEXT layout: THE CLAIM in DIN caps at LABEL, quiet, in the
label column; the claim in a patient's own words in the serif at DISPLAY, ink, at most
three lines at 30ch. No red, because the claim is not the series' word. It holds for the
sentence spoken over it and at least six seconds.

The verdict card is the same layout with THE VERDICT and the one sentence at DISPLAY in
ink, one word in cover red where a word earns it. It appears twice, identically, and holds
at least eight seconds the first time, when the sentence is new to the viewer.

The three questions card carries THE THREE QUESTIONS at LABEL, quiet, and the three
questions page 9 prints, each at LEAD in the serif, ink, 1.1em apart, with the folio in
mono at LABEL, quiet. It holds while she sets them out, twenty to thirty seconds, and may
return once if she names a question again.

The title card and end card are as `IDENTITY.md` fixes; the title card is under three
seconds and the end card fifteen. All cards are cream `#F7F3EC`, type ink `#111111`,
secondary quiet `#6F6A62`, red `#B5291C` once on a frame at most. The three system inks
do not appear.

## Recording set-up

A recent phone records 4K well enough; a mirrorless camera with a 35mm to 50mm equivalent
lens is better for its softer background. Automatic anything is not acceptable. Set 3840 by 2160 at 25 frames per second, the European rate,
which YouTube accepts as recorded ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171)).
Set the shutter to 1/50, the 180-degree shutter at 25 fps, against 50Hz mains banding ([Amateur Photographer](https://amateurphotographer.com/video/how-to/how-shutter-speeds-work-when-shooting-video)).
Fix white balance by hand, near 4500K under the key, because a face that shifts colour
between takes cannot be cut. Fix exposure and focus once.

The camera is at eye height, two metres away, eye line to the lens and not to a monitor.
One light, a large soft source at about forty-five degrees to one side and a little above
her eyes, the far side of the face left to fall off; an overcast window does the same job.
No second light and no coloured backlight.

Sound matters more than picture. A viewer forgives a soft image and leaves at the first
room echo, and a series whose authority is exact sentences cannot have them swallowed. A
wired lavalier at the sternum is the simplest choice; a shotgun just above frame if she
wants no cable on camera. Record at 48kHz. A consulting room is
already treated: books, a rug, upholstered chairs, curtains. Close the curtains, put the
chair on the rug, switch off anything that hums, and record ten seconds of silence for
the editor.

The background is the room she works in, or a plain wall near cream. The room is better
because a clinician is believed on what a clinician sees, and a set, with a ring light, a
plant and a neon word, says presenter. Nothing in frame is arranged to be read: no book
turned to camera, no diploma. The book appears once, on the end card.

Wardrobe is plain and mid-tone: no fine pattern, no white or cream, which vanish into the
cards, and no red, which the series keeps for one word.

## Delivery

No teleprompter; read text sounds read. The script is written in sections of at most
forty seconds, each one idea with its first sentence fixed, and she speaks each from
memory to the lens, as many takes as it needs, before the next. The cuts between sections
are covered by the cards and bands.

A fluffed line is not stopped. She pauses, holds the look, and says the sentence again
from its first word; the pause is the editor's handle and the restart a clean cut.

Every source is said aloud as well as shown: "Dickerson and Kemeny, in 2004, found". The
band is for a viewer who reads; the spoken name is for a listener, for the transcript,
and for the record. The cue sheet checks both.

## The cue sheet

Each episode's `CUES.md` is a table with five columns. TC is the timecode of the cut as
`MM:SS`. LINE is the spoken sentence or enough of it to find in `SCRIPT.md`. SCREEN is
one of `host`, `title`, `episode card`, `claim`, `verdict`, `questions`, `end`, or `host +
band`. BAND is the source band's text in the `CITATIONS.md` form, empty when there is no
band, or `proposed` when the host marks the sentence as proposed, so an empty band is a
decision and not an omission. CIT is the `CITATIONS.md` number behind any BAND entry. The
scriptwriter fills LINE, BAND and CIT; the editor fills TC.

## Captions and accessibility

Captions are not burned in: they would be a second moving typeface on a frame that allows
one entering card at a time, and could not be turned off or translated. The script,
corrected to what was said, is uploaded as the caption file so YouTube's captions are
exact rather than automatic. The same text is published as the transcript, with the
reference list, as a page the site can link to, so a journalist has every sentence in a
form that can be checked.

## Publishing

The thumbnail is as `IDENTITY.md` fixes: cream, the series label, the episode number in
mono, the red word at COVER, no face. The YouTube title is the series title with its
colon, the episode number, and the claim in plain words, and it promises nothing the
verdict does not keep.

The description opens with the verdict sentence, then the chapters, then the full
reference list in the `CITATIONS.md` form with DOIs, then the site's address and the
transcript's. Chapters need a first timestamp at 0:00, at least three in ascending order,
none under ten seconds ([Tella](https://docs.tella.com/help/export-videos/youtube-chapters));
the movements give five: Claim, Evidence, Verdict, Application, Close.

The Short is the first thirty seconds re-framed, or one sourced passage with its band,
never the application alone. Shorts may run to three minutes ([Cord Cutters News](https://cordcuttersnews.com/youtube-shorts-get-longer-creators-can-soon-upload-up-to-3-minutes-of-content/)),
but this one stays under sixty seconds, because it is a door and not a room. Its
related-video link, set in YouTube Studio, points at the episode ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/youtube-enables-linking-shorts-long-form-videos-430655)),
and its last frame is the thumbnail composition, so what a viewer sees is what they click.
