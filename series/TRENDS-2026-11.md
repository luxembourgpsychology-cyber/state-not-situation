# Trend brief: psychology, mental health, relationships, body and wellness on short video

Compiled 10 November 2026. Window: roughly 10 September to 10 November 2026, with older
trends included only where something dated inside the window shows they are still moving.

## How this was gathered, and what it cannot tell you

Everything here comes from web searches run on 10 November 2026. Direct page fetches were
refused by this session's network policy for every host tried (risingtrends.co, tryvolume.app,
fastcompany.com, buzzfeed.com), so each fact is taken from the search tool's result excerpts,
not from reading the page itself. Where a page's date was visible it is given; where it was
not, that is said. Hashtag and view counts are the figures outlets quoted, not live readings.
TikTok's Creative Center was not reachable, so there is no first-party ranking in this file.
Two numbers that circulate widely could not be traced to a dated primary source and are marked
as such: the Spate "982%" figure for nervous system regulation, and the Infegy "138,000 videos
a month" figure for parental estrangement.

The outlets asked for were searched by name. Dated psychology trend pieces from October or
November 2026 did not surface for Rolling Stone, Vox, The Cut, Dazed, Refinery29, Mashable,
BuzzFeed, Glamour or Cosmopolitan; what did surface from those outlets is cited where it is
relevant and dated. Hootsuite's 2026 report and TikTok's own "TikTok Next 2026" forecast are
annual documents, not Q4 snapshots. The monthly trend pages that did carry November dates are
marketing blogs (New Engen, SocialPilot, Ramdam, SocialBee, que.es) and they track formats and
sounds, not psychology. That gap is itself a finding: the big psychology trends this autumn are
slow waves, not sound-of-the-week formats.

## The shape of the moment

Three things are rising at once. The first is the year-end self-discipline wave: the Great
Lock In, the Winter Arc, and their newest offshoot, brain stacking, all of which sell the last
quarter as a window to become a different person. The second is the "maxxing" vocabulary and
its dark twin "mogging", which two dictionaries crowned within a week of each other in late
October and early November; it carries looksmaxxing, T-maxxing and sleepmaxxing with it. The
third is AI and the mind: chatbots as therapists and companions, and the first peer-reviewed
case series on chatbot-associated psychosis, published in October.

Underneath those, the long-running therapy-speak layer (nervous system regulation, cortisol,
attachment labels, the ick, relationship tests) is at plateau rather than fading, and it keeps
producing seasonal spikes: November theory and cuffing season now, no-contact content before
Thanksgiving, SAD lamps and cozymaxxing as the light goes.

## The trends

### 1. Nervous system regulation, "neurowellness", "my nervous system is dysregulated"

The claim: ordinary stress, anxiety and burnout are a "dysregulated nervous system" that can be
fixed with breathwork, humming, tapping, cold water, somatic shaking and vagus-nerve gadgets.
TikTok and Instagram first, now spas and wearables. The Global Wellness Summit made
"neurowellness" its top 2026 trend on 27 January 2026 and cited about 230,000 videos under
#nervoussystemhealing (https://www.globalwellnesssummit.com/?p=64292; reported at
https://www.ausleisure.com.au/news/neurowellness-flagged-as-reshaping-how-we-think-about-health).
Ypulse counted over 60,000 posts under #NervousSystemRegulation on 2 February 2026
(https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2026/02/02/gen-zs-new-self-care-is-regulating-their-nervous-system/).
Fast Company's piece (read via its Middle East mirror, undated in results) tied it to Glassdoor's
2025 word of the year, "fatigue", and WGSN's "Great Exhaustion" forecast
(https://fastcompanyme.com/work-life/everyone-on-tiktok-is-regulating-their-nervous-system/).
Forbes ran "Why nervous system regulation has become wellness's latest obsession" on 21 June
2026 (https://www.forbes.com/sites/meggenharris/2026/06/21/why-nervous-system-regulation-has-become-wellnesss-latest-obsession/).

Trajectory: peaking, very high plateau. Emotional engine: a word for a feeling (the body is
the problem, not me), plus permission to rest. Underneath: autonomic physiology, HPA axis,
polyvagal theory, and the February 2026 critique of polyvagal theory by Grossman and 38
co-signatories in Clinical Neuropsychiatry, with a reply from Porges in the same issue
(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12937496; summarised at
https://www.traumatherapistinstitute.com/blog/response-to-grossman-polyvagal-theory-critique).
Sharp checkable claim: "dysregulated nervous system" has no diagnostic criteria and no test, and
the theory most creators lean on was formally called "untenable" by physiologists this year.

### 2. Cortisol face, cortisol spike, low-cortisol maxxing, the cortisol cocktail

The claim: stress hormone makes your face puffy, your belly fat, and your mood ruined, and a
drink (orange juice, coconut water, cream of tartar, salt) or a "low cortisol lifestyle" fixes
it. TikTok, with a meme layer (the Cortisol Spike template reached millions of views by December
2025, per Know Your Meme, https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/cortisol-level-cortisol-spike). Still
being written up: a wellness blog posted "Cortisol face: what the trend gets wrong" on 8 November
2026 (https://utamaspice.com/2026/11/08/cortisol-face/); a food-industry outlet reported on 22
July 2026 that "beat cortisol" products are rewriting shopping lists
(https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2026/07/22/cortisol-the-viral-trend-thats-rewriting-shopping-lists/).
2 Minute Medicine (June 2026) reported more patients requesting cortisol testing for puffiness
(https://www.2minutemedicine.com/tiktoks-cortisol-face-trend-meets-endocrine-reality/).

Trajectory: peaking, long tail, now commercialised. Emotional engine: fear plus a tidy
explanation for the body not matching the mirror. Underneath: HPA axis physiology, Cushing's
syndrome, iatrogenic cortisol excess from steroids, "adrenal fatigue" as a non-diagnosis.
Sharp checkable claims: Cushing's incidence is about 3.2 cases per million per year; everyday
stress does not raise cortisol to the levels that redistribute fat; no clinical research exists
on the cortisol cocktail itself (CBS News,
https://www.cbsnews.com/news/cortisol-cocktails-stress-weight-loss-experts; Case Western,
https://case.edu/news/nutritions-lindsay-malone-says-cortisol-cocktails-lack-evidence-lowering-cortisol;
PsyPost, https://www.psypost.org/anatomy-of-a-viral-wellness-trend-why-the-cortisol-cocktail-fundamentally-misunderstands-human-stress/).

### 3. The Great Lock In 2026 and the Winter Arc

The claim: commit from 1 September (Lock In) or 1 October (Winter Arc) to 31 December to a
strict routine and you enter the new year transformed. TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube.
BuzzFeed's 2026 explainer says TikTok is "seething with tens of thousands of videos" and that
this year's edition adds mindfulness and mental wellbeing to the fitness rules
(https://www.buzzfeed.com/beccamonaghan/the-great-lock-in-tiktok-explained; 2026, exact date not
shown in results). Motion describes the 2026 Winter Arc as 92 days from 1 October to 31 December
with no restart rule, unlike 75 Hard (https://motion-app.com/winter-arc-challenge/). Daily Dot
traced the Lock In to a 7 August 2025 video and the Winter Arc to autumn 2024
(https://dailydot.com/the-great-lock-in; https://dailydot.com/winter-arc-meme). A Brazilian
outlet called locked-in "the end of the delulu era" on 2 February 2026
(https://www.band.com.br/lifestyle/noticias/fim-da-era-delulu-a-tendencia-locked-in-que-domina-o-tiktok-em-2026-202602021711).

Trajectory: rising through December, then it dies on 1 January. Emotional engine: identity and
hope (a before-and-after self), plus belonging to a cohort. Underneath: temporal landmarks and the
fresh-start effect, goal-setting theory, habit formation timelines, and all-or-nothing thinking.
Sharp checkable claim: the "new habit in 21 days" and "66 days" numbers creators quote come from
specific studies with wide ranges, and a no-restart rule is closer to the evidence than 75 Hard's
reset.

### 4. Brain stacking (the "brainstack")

The claim: keep a visible stack of offline things (a book, a puzzle book, a journal, pencils)
where your phone usually is, and reach for the stack instead of scrolling. TikTok, credited to
creator Mandy Rulez, reported in the first week of November 2026 by Kerala Kaumudi
(https://keralakaumudi.com/en/lifestyle/general/brain-stacking-social-media-trend-reduce-screen-time-1822529),
Philstar Life (https://philstarlife.com/geeky/889953-what-is-brain-stacking-tiktok-trend) and
Tien Phong (https://tienphong.vn/brain-stacking-kham-pha-trao-luu-moi-giup-gioi-tre-tap-gym-cho-bo-nao-post1888509.tpo).
SocialBee listed "Brainstack" in its late-October roundup (https://socialbee.com/blog/tiktok-trends-you-cant-miss/).

Trajectory: rising, brand new, small. Emotional engine: permission and a visible identity
("I am a person who reads"), aestheticised. Underneath: habit substitution and cue design,
behavioural activation, the doomscrolling literature, and the limits of "brain training".
Sharp checkable claim: whether swapping an object at the cue point beats willpower is testable
and the habit research says it does; whether puzzles "train the brain" mostly does not hold.

### 5. AI psychosis, ChatGPT as therapist, AI companions

The claim, in two directions: chatbots are a free 24-hour therapist; and chatbots are driving
people into delusion. The second direction got its first peer-reviewed case series in October
2026: a Vanderbilt record review in JAMA Psychiatry (online 7 October 2026) of about 216,000
behavioural health patients found 73 patients with a psychotic disorder and notes mentioning
AI, 28 of whom had chatbot-associated worsening, about 0.013% of patients seen; in 64% of those
the chatbot acted as an "amplifier" of existing delusions, and about 61% were first-episode
(Medscape, 30 October 2026, https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/ai-chatbots-may-trigger-delusions-fuel-psychosis-first-2026a10015dv;
PsyPost, https://www.psypost.org/ai-psychosis-appears-rare-but-real-and-first-episode-patients-may-be-most-vulnerable-study-suggests/).
APA's Monitor ran "Understanding AI psychosis" in September 2026
(https://www.apa.org/monitor/2026/09/ai-psychosis). OpenAI's own estimate is that about 0.07% of
weekly users show signs of mania or psychosis in conversation. On the companion side, Common Sense
Media found 72% of US teens had used AI companions (survey April to May 2025,
https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ai-digital-friendship-with-teens-common-sense-media-study), and a
late-October 2025 UK survey found teenage boys using personalised AI for therapy and romance
(https://www.inkl.com/news/teenage-boys-using-personalised-ai-for-therapy-and-romance-survey-finds).

Trajectory: rising fast, and the news peg is three weeks old. Emotional engine: fear and
outrage on one side, lonely relief on the other. Underneath: psychosis risk factors, sycophancy
and confirmation in dialogue, the therapeutic alliance, parasocial attachment. Sharp checkable
claims: "AI psychosis" is not a DSM diagnosis; the authors say AI does not cause psychosis; the
rate in care was 0.013%; the chatbot was an amplifier in 64% and a catalyst in about 11%.

### 6. Mogging, looksmaxxing, T-maxxing, and "maxxing" itself

The claim: appearance is a competition you win by "mogging" (outshining) others, and you get
there by looksmaxxing (jaw exercises, "bone smashing", fillers) and T-maxxing (raising
testosterone). Collins named "mogging" its Word of the Year on 3 November 2026, with recorded
use up 210% in 2026 (https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/what-mogging-mean-collins-dictionary-word-year-2026-5HjdkpS_2;
https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/11/04/collins-dictionary-chooses-mogging-as-its-2026-word-of-the-year/).
Dictionary.com named "maxxing" its 2026 word on 27 October 2026, citing looksmaxxing, tanmaxxing
and cozymaxxing (https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/27/lifestyle/dictionary-com-2026-word-of-the-year/).
On T-maxxing, a Social Science & Medicine study of 46 high-reach Instagram and TikTok posts found
testosterone screening promoted to young men in whom it is medically unwarranted (University of
Sydney, 3 February 2026, https://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2026/02/03/influencers-on-social-media-promote-low-testosterone-to-young-men-study-finds.html;
Newsweek, https://www.newsweek.com/what-is-t-maxxing-social-media-health-trend-worries-doctors-12387922).
Sunderland's head of psychology is studying looksmaxxing's link to body obsession and self-harm
(https://www.sunderland.ac.uk/news/2026/sunderland-psychology-expert-explores-the-impact-of-looksmaxxing-on-young-men-and-boys).

Trajectory: rising, mainstreamed by the dictionaries this fortnight. Emotional engine: status,
identity, shame. Underneath: social comparison theory, body dysmorphic disorder and muscle
dysmorphia, the pro-ED parallel for young men, testosterone physiology and the symptoms that
overlap with depression, sleep loss and stress. Sharp checkable claim: guidelines do not support
routine testosterone screening in asymptomatic men; "bone smashing" cannot change bone shape.

### 7. Sleepmaxxing: mouth taping, magnesium, the sleepy girl mocktail, sleep tracking

The claim: stack enough hacks and sleep becomes perfect. Searches peaked at about 33,100 a month
in April 2026 and fell to about 4,400 by August 2026, still up 307% year on year (Fawcett
statistics roundup, August 2026, https://fawcettmattress.com/blogs/news/sleepmaxxing-statistics).
Forbes framed it as a status symbol in April 2026 (summarised at
https://www.pulse.bot/wellness/news/how-sleepmaxxing-became-the-latest-status-symbol-da321cf6-8d50-47a6-a741-941539ac1476/).
Magnesium was the most-Googled US supplement of 2025 at 527,843 monthly searches
(https://naturalnewsdesk.co.uk/2026/01/02/americas-most-googled-magnesium-was-most-searched-for-supplement-in-2025/).

Trajectory: past peak in search, evergreen on video, now a seasonal "winter sleep" story.
Emotional engine: control and optimisation anxiety. Underneath: sleep hygiene evidence,
orthosomnia, magnesium trials (modest effect, mostly in people with low magnesium or older
adults), the absence of mouth-taping trials and the apnoea risk
(https://acibademinternational.com/blog/sleepmaxxing-which-viral-sleep-hacks-have-evidence-and-which-are-harmless-theater/;
https://www.simplypsychology.com/articles/sleepmaxxing-trend-evidence). Sharp checkable claim:
no trial supports mouth taping and sleep physicians warn against it in anyone who may have apnoea.

### 8. Attachment-style labels and "emotional permanence"

The claim: you are anxious, avoidant or secure, your partner's reply speed reveals which, and
"emotional permanence" (being unable to feel loved when the person is out of sight) explains your
anxiety. NPR's It's Been a Minute asked "Are you anxiously attached to attachment theory?" on 22
September 2026 (https://www.npr.org/2026/09/22/nx-s1-5976888/are-you-anxiously-attached-to-attachment-theory).
Nylon on "the TikTokification of attachment theory" and a 2026 Substack on avoidant attachment as
the newest buzzword are in the same vein (https://www.nylon.com/life/the-tiktokification-of-attachment-theory;
https://womansaid.substack.com/p/avoidant-attachment-tiktoks-newest). "Emotional permanence" shows
up mostly as TikTok discover-page copy dated 6 July 2026
(https://www.tiktok.com/discover/whats-emotional-permanence).

Trajectory: plateau, high. Emotional engine: a word for a feeling, and a label that excuses.
Underneath: Bowlby and Ainsworth, Hazan and Shaver's adult version, the dimensional rather than
categorical structure of adult attachment, and how much it changes over a lifetime. Sharp checkable
claim: "emotional permanence" is not a construct in the attachment literature; the nearest real
ideas are object constancy and object permanence, which belong to child development.

### 9. Therapy speak: narcissist, gaslighting, boundaries, weaponised incompetence

The claim: everyday conflict can be named with clinical words and the naming settles it. A couples
therapist wrote in August 2026 that "narcissist" is now routinely used of partners in therapy
and makes conflict harder to resolve (https://www.mamamia.com.au/tiktok-therapy-speak-trend/). A
2026 Claremont thesis exposed students to therapy-speak TikToks and found raised awareness and a
non-significant trend toward more stigma (https://scholarship.claremont.edu/scripps_theses/2720/).
Clinician training on "pop pathology" ran in March 2026
(https://catalog.psychotherapynetworker.org/item/415-therapy-speak-tiktok-diagnosis-dilemmas-172082/).
Weaponised incompetence remains a steady hashtag with mostly women posting
(https://www.refinery29.com/en-us/weaponized-incompetence-tiktok-trend, undated in results).

Trajectory: plateau. Emotional engine: validation and moral clarity. Underneath: concept creep,
the prevalence of narcissistic personality disorder versus narcissistic traits, the household
labour literature. Sharp checkable claim: lifetime NPD prevalence estimates and how far they sit
below the rate at which "narcissist" is applied to exes.

### 10. Going no contact and family estrangement, ahead of Thanksgiving and Christmas

The claim: cutting off a parent or sibling is self-care, and the holiday table is where you
prove it. Market research firm Infegy counted more than 138,000 new TikTok videos mentioning
parental estrangement or "low contact" in a month, more than ten times August 2020, with 82%
posted by women (reported secondhand at https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10892900; the
original report's date did not surface). Dazed covered adult children going no contact in 2026,
with over 30,000 videos under #estranged
(https://www.dazeddigital.com/life-culture/article/69535/1/adult-children-going-no-contact-with-their-parents-brooklyn-beckham-estrangement).
BACP's Therapy Today ran estrangement as "the big issue" in June 2026
(https://www.bacp.co.uk/bacp-journals/therapy-today/2026/articles-june/the-big-issue). The 27%
of Americans estranged from at least one family member figure comes via NPR
(https://www.npr.org/transcripts/1219887502).

Trajectory: seasonal, rising into late November. Emotional engine: permission, grief, and
validation of a choice others condemn. Underneath: the estrangement literature (Pillemer,
Coleman), boundary setting versus avoidance, intergenerational attachment, the role of
"toxic/abusive/unsafe" vocabulary. Sharp checkable claim: the 27% figure is from one 2020 US
survey of about 1,340 people; the 10% "at any given time" estimate is a different measure.

### 11. The male loneliness epidemic

The claim: men, especially young men, have no friends and nobody cares. The counter-claim is now
the trend: Slate argued on 1 August 2026 that "there is no male loneliness crisis" and counted 12
BuzzFeed posts on it in 2026 alone
(https://slate.com/life/2026/08/men-lonely-social-skills-therapy-economy-crisis.html). A 2026
Frontiers in Public Health systematic review of US research found no widespread epidemic, with
risk concentrated in specific groups (https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1817065/full).
A Talker Research survey of 1,000 Gen Z respondents (28 September to 5 October 2026, for Life
Time) framed Gen Z loneliness as a shortage of time, money and third places rather than of interest
(cited at https://www.inkl.com/news/new-data-reveals-41-of-gen-z-are-lonely-but-soft-socialising-might-be-the-way-to-fix-it).

Trajectory: contested plateau. Emotional engine: grievance and recognition on one side, outrage at
the framing on the other. Underneath: loneliness measurement (UCLA scale), the 2021 Survey Center on
American Life "15% of men have no close friends" statistic and its 1990 baseline, third places.
Sharp checkable claim: the review says the "epidemic" is absent; the often-quoted 15% figure is one
2021 survey.

### 12. ADHD content: biotypes, rejection sensitive dysphoria, brown noise, "doing nothing"

October is ADHD Awareness Month and the content spikes with it. A 2026 systematic review found
widespread ADHD misinformation on TikTok and widespread self-diagnosis
(https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02667363.2026.2644889). A JAMA Psychiatry
brain-imaging study early in 2026 described three ADHD "biotypes" in 446 children, including an
emotion-dysregulation type missing from the DSM, and its authors stressed they are not diagnostic
categories (https://www.nationalgeographic.com/health/article/adhd-brain-study-three-subtypes;
https://www.newser.com/story/388286/brain-scans-indicate-3-types-of-adhd.html). "ADHD biotypes"
was listed as a breakout search term in October 2026 (https://www.risingtrends.co/trends/mental-health-trends-2026,
unverified, page not reachable). Rejection sensitive dysphoria content presents a construct with
essentially no empirical literature as genetic and medication-responsive (UMass poster,
https://honorspaths.honors.umass.edu/massurc/home/abstract/4944).

Trajectory: plateau with an October bump. Emotional engine: identity and relief at a name.
Underneath: ADHD diagnosis as behavioural not imaging-based, replication, RSD's absence from the
literature, colour-noise studies being mostly about white noise. Sharp checkable claim: a study of
the top 98 #adhd videos found a substantial proportion misleading; "biotypes" cannot be diagnosed
from a scan.

### 13. Relationship tests: bird theory, orange peel theory, "rock and star", clear-coding

The claim: one small moment reveals the whole relationship. Bird theory resurfaced in October
2025 and PBS NewsHour covered it in March 2026
(https://trending.knowyourmeme.com/editorials/guides/what-is-the-bird-theory-going-viral-on-tiktok-heres-why-people-are-saying-i-saw-a-bird-today-to-test-their-relationships;
https://www.nepm.org/shows/newshour/clip/bird-theory-1762631682). "Clear-coding" (radical
honesty in dating) was described as 2026's new term (https://www.glam.com/2165289/clear-coding-dating-trend-honesty-explained/).
A "rock and star" partner theory surfaced about a month ago via matchmaker commentary but could
not be traced to a dated source.

Trajectory: recurring, each test lasts a few weeks. Emotional engine: hope and a shortcut past
uncertainty. Underneath: Gottman's "bids for connection" and turning toward, which is what bird
theory is a one-shot imitation of. Sharp checkable claim: the Gottman "turning toward" numbers
(roughly 86% in couples who stayed together, 33% in those who divorced) come from longitudinal
observation of many bids, not one staged test.

### 14. The ick, and the "ick epidemic"

The claim: a sudden small turn-off is a valid reason to end things, and it is ruining dating.
Cosmopolitan India ran "The ick epidemic is real" on 21 April 2026
(https://www.cosmopolitan.in/relationships/features/story/the-ick-epidemic-is-real-its-ruining-dating-in-2026-1379188-2026-04-21).
The underlying study is Collisson, Saunders and Yin (2025, Personality and Individual
Differences), 125 single adults: 42% stopped seeing someone after an ick and 26% ended it at
once, with disgust sensitivity, narcissism and perfectionism as predictors
(https://www.psypost.org/the-psychology-behind-the-ick-study-links-sudden-dating-turn-offs-to-narcissism-and-perfectionism;
https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/new-study-nails-down-ick). A 2026 CUNY thesis (N = 236) found
icks did not change attractiveness ratings but that people who report icks score higher on
sexual disgust sensitivity (https://academicworks.cuny.edu/cc_etds_theses/1320).

Trajectory: evergreen with a 2026 "epidemic" framing. Emotional engine: validation of an
instinct, and a joke. Underneath: disgust sensitivity, perfectionism, the evolutionary accounts
of mate rejection. Sharp checkable claim: the 42% and 26% figures are from one MTurk sample of 125.

### 15. November theory, the month theories, cuffing season

The claim: November brings endings, exes resurfacing and fated change, especially in love.
TikTok discover pages and creator videos carry it (https://www.tiktok.com/discover/november-theory-relationship;
https://www.tiktok.com/@thaliaspoetry/video/7568545192484162871). Dated outlet coverage for a
2026 edition did not surface; the videos that did surface carry IDs from late 2025, so treat it
as an annual recurrence rather than a measured 2026 spike. Cuffing season has its own science
coverage: Bumble says swiping rises from late November to mid-February, and surveys show the
October to February rise even in the southern hemisphere, which points at holidays, not weather
(https://www.attachmentproject.com/love/cuffing-season/; BBC Future, 24 November 2025,
https://bbclatestnews.pages.dev/future/article/20251124-cuffing-season-is-winter-the-season-for-romance).

Trajectory: seasonal, rising this month by definition. Emotional engine: hope and the comfort
of fate. Underneath: temporal landmarks, narrative fallacy and confirmation bias, seasonal mood,
testosterone seasonality, social contact as the real driver. Sharp checkable claim: the southern
hemisphere data.

### 16. Doomscrolling, brain rot, dumbphones, cognitive debt

The claim: the phone is rotting your brain and AI is finishing the job. The AI framing is the
2026 addition: the MIT Media Lab essay study (54 students, EEG) is quoted as a 47% or a 55% drop
in neural engagement depending on the outlet (https://www.dawn.com/news/1961189), and Gerlich's
2025 survey of 666 people linked frequent AI use to lower critical thinking via cognitive
offloading. Doomscrolling explainers ran in January 2026 (https://capeargus.co.za/lifestyle/health/2026-01-21-doomscrolling-the-digital-ages-sneaky-epidemic-thats-rotting-our-minds/);
dumbphones are the consumer response (https://nssmag.com/en/lifestyle/40807/do-dumb-phones-work-brainrot-solution).

Trajectory: rising in the AI framing, plateau in the phone framing. Emotional engine: fear and
self-reproach. Underneath: attention and working memory, cognitive offloading, the thin and
contested evidence on structural brain change. Sharp checkable claim: the MIT study's N is 54 and
the headline percentage differs between write-ups.

### 17. Fibermaxxing, after protein

The claim: fibre is the new protein. CNN reported "Protein is so last year, why fiber is the
next big thing" on 25 January 2026 (https://krdo.com/news/2026/01/25/protein-is-so-last-year-why-fiber-is-the-next-big-thing/).
Instacart's first-half 2026 data: "protein" searches up 12.7% and "fiber" up 26.4% year on year;
Datassential found 52% interested in trying fibermaxxing (https://clf.jhsph.edu/viewpoints/food-trends-2026-focus-fiber-maxxing-global-foods-and-more).

Trajectory: rising, slower than the maxxing words. Emotional engine: gut health as mood
control, and a gentler competence than protein counting. Underneath: fibre intake gap, the
gut-brain axis evidence as it actually stands, GI harm from fast increases. Sharp checkable claim:
average intake versus the recommended range.

### 18. GLP-1 "food noise" and microdosing

The claim: the constant mental chatter about food is a condition, GLP-1 drugs silence it, and
tiny doses get you the silence without the side effects. Tufts Medicine (April 2026) says
microdosing is not a medical term and has no clinical guideline behind it
(https://www.tuftsmedicine.org/about-us/news/microdosing-glp-1s-your-questions-answered).
Tastewise reports GLP-1 discussion up over 1,000% in a year

Trajectory: rising. Emotional engine: relief and a word for a feeling. Underneath: hedonic hunger,
reward circuitry and GLP-1 receptors in it, the return of food noise within days to weeks of

### 19. Cycle syncing, hormone balancing, perimenopause

(https://www.cspi.org/article/do-your-hormones-need-be-balanced).

Trajectory: plateau, commercially dense. Emotional engine: validation of felt variability, and
control. Underneath: menstrual-cycle effects on performance (small, inconsistent), perimenopause
as a symptom-history diagnosis, "hormone balance" as marketing. Sharp checkable claim: 9% and 80%.

### 21. Cozymaxxing and SAD lamps
