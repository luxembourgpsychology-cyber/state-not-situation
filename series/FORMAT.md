# The format, from episode 02

Decided 3 November 2026, after episode 01 was recorded in the evidence-first form. From
episode 02 every episode is built in six beats. The architecture is felt as rhythm and
never named on camera. Only three beats are fixed in wording: the card, the verdict and
the return. The three stories are different every time.

The discipline from `BRIEF.md` is unchanged: every fact sourced and the source on screen
and spoken; the author's proposals announced and unbanded; the composite declared; the
real conditions named and referred; the book quoted only in its printed words; never a
takedown; the episode ends on the better clinical answer.

## The six beats

**1. The sentence.** Cold open. One sentence a patient has said, alone on a card, in the
patient's words, for about five seconds. No title, no music, no host. It is the sentence
the viewer has heard a real person say. Then the title card, under three seconds.

**2. The verdict.** The host, within the first twenty seconds. The verdict as a headline,
at or under twelve words on the card and at or under thirty-five spoken, in the form "the
evidence is weaker than the claim, and here is what is solid underneath". The viewer who
came for the answer has it. The host introduces herself in one sentence here, no more.

**3. The commitment.** "Before I show you why, finish this sentence. I would stop
believing this if..." Ten seconds of silence on camera, a mono clock counting them. Phone
or paper. The viewer now owns the next twelve minutes. This is the device that is the
series' own.

**4. The chain of custody.** Three stories, each drawn on the recurring figure, each
carrying two or three sources and no more. In order: where the sentence was born, the real
finding it started as; where it broke, the hand-off that stretched it, with who gained;
and what is true underneath, the study that shows the shape the claim missed. Each story
has a character, a date and a place, and pays off as one mechanism the viewer can repeat
at dinner. A story that is merely interesting is cut. Six to nine sources in all, down
from twenty. The sources stay on screen under every factual sentence.

**5. The room.** The composite, declared as a composite, sorted with the book's three
questions in their printed words. Inside this beat, the thing the viewer does with the
host, in real time, on their own body or their own day: the jaw check, counting the hours
of sleep they are short this week, timing their own switches for one minute. It is placed
where the composite does it too, so the viewer and the composite act together. The real
conditions are named and referred here or in beat four, wherever the story reaches them.
The beat ends on the better clinical answer.

**6. The return.** "Look at what you wrote." Did the episode show them that, some of it,
or is something missing. Then the retraction line: what evidence would make the host take
this episode down, said plainly. Then the close: references in the description, transcript
on the site, see a doctor for the symptoms. End card.

## Time budget, fifteen minutes

| Beat | Approx. |
|---|---|
| 1. The sentence, and title | 0:00 to 0:10 |
| 2. The verdict, and the name | 0:10 to 0:40 |
| 3. The commitment | 0:40 to 1:05 |
| 4. Three stories | 1:05 to 9:30 |
| 5. The room | 9:30 to 13:30 |
| 6. The return and close | 13:30 to 15:00 |

## The hook

The hook is now the first sentence of the first story, after the verdict and the
commitment. The standard from `SEASON.md` holds: a specific, true thing, a scene or a fact
that reverses expectation, never a question, never "everything you know is wrong", never
a number the sources do not carry.

## The stories

What makes a story qualify. It has a person, a year and a place. It contains one
measurement or one decision that the viewer could not have guessed. It pays off as one
mechanism, stated in a sentence the viewer could repeat. It is sourced to a primary paper
or a documented account, and the source is on screen. Two to three sources per story. A
story may be told in sixty to one hundred and fifty seconds, never longer.

Where the sentence was born is almost always a real study that found something narrower
than the claim. Where it broke is almost always a hand-off: a book, a magazine, a product.
Name who gained, without contempt. What is true underneath is the study that shows the
shape the claim missed, and it is where the figure's drawing does its best work.

## The thing you do

One per episode. It takes under a minute. It happens on the viewer's own body or their
own day, not on a screen. It is tied to the claim so tightly that the result means
something without further explanation. It is never a party trick and never a test the
viewer can fail. The host does it too, on camera, at the same time.

## The retraction line

One sentence, in the first person, naming the kind of evidence that would overturn the
episode's verdict. It is specific enough that a researcher could bring it. It is said at
the same pace as everything else, not as a flourish.

## Continuity

The drawn figure is the same person across the season. Her days connect: the sample in
the cortisol episode was taken on the morning after the argument in the memory episode;
the week in the sleep episode is the week the hair in the hair-and-saliva drawing grew.
These links are drawn, never spoken. The figure never becomes cute, never has a face,
never has a name.

## What this replaces

In `PRODUCTION.md`, the four movements and their time budget are superseded by the six
beats and the table above. The on-screen grammar, the source band rules, the recording
set-up, captions and publishing stand. In the `/episode` command, the scriptwriter and
the story finder build to this file. Episode 01 stays as recorded, in the earlier form,
and is the control against which the retention of this form is read after a month.
