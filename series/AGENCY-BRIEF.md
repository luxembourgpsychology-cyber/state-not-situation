# Agency brief: the series, episode 1

For Codex, from Ivana Budišin. 19 October 2026.

This brief is everything your editors, animators, sound and art direction need to finish
episode 1. Read sections 1 and 2 before anything else. They hold the rules that cannot
bend. Everything after them is craft, and there your judgment is welcome.

The first recording is in this folder, as the author made it. Nothing has been cut.

---

## 1. What this is

A serious YouTube series by a clinical psychologist in Luxembourg. Each episode takes one
popular claim about the body and the mind that patients bring into her room. It sets out
what the evidence actually shows, with the sources on screen and spoken aloud. It gives a
verdict in one sentence. Then it shows what she does instead with a patient who believed
the claim.

Episode 1 examines "cortisol is the stress hormone", with adrenal fatigue, cortisol belly,
home kits and supplements. The verdict:

> The evidence is weaker than the claim. A cortisol level says little about a person's
> stress. What is solid underneath is the shape of the day, and the slow second wave after
> alarm.

The audience is intelligent adults, including clinicians and researchers. The register is
a practitioner being exact in public. It is not an explainer channel, not wellness content
and not a takedown. If a cut would sit comfortably next to a supplement advert, it is wrong.

The author has written a book, *State. Not Situation.* It supplies the method in the last
third of the episode and the visual identity of the series. The episode does not sell it.

## 2. The rules that cannot bend

**Every factual statement on screen or in the voice comes from the sources.** The verified
sources are in `CITATIONS.md`, and the episode's facts are in
`episodes/01-cortisol/RESEARCH.md`. No number, anatomical claim, label or caption may be
added that those files do not support. If an animator wants to show something the files do
not cover, ask first.

**Finding and opinion are kept visibly apart.** Every sourced sentence carries a source
band on screen. Two sentences in section 29 are the author's proposal, not a finding. She
says so aloud. Nothing appears on screen under them, by design. Do not add a band there.

**The book is quoted only in its printed words.** Quotations from pages 7 and 9 appear in
the script in double quotes. If they appear on screen, they appear exactly as printed.

**The cut must not change what was said.** You may cut fluffs, pauses and repeated takes.
You may not join two halves of different sentences so that a claim changes. If the author
misspoke a fact, flag the timecode; do not repair it with a cut that invents a sentence.

**Accuracy is checked before lock.** Before the picture is locked, the transcript of the
cut is checked line by line against the script for every number and source. Section 4.6
says how.

## 4. The edit

### 4.1 Structure and length

Four movements and a close, about fifteen minutes. The script's section numbers are the
spine of the edit. Every file keys to them.

| Movement | Sections | Approx. time |
|---|---|---|
| The claim | 1 to 5 | 0:00 to 2:20 |
| The evidence | 6 to 20 | 2:20 to 9:45 |
| The verdict, restated | 21 | 9:45 to 10:05 |
| The application | 22 to 29 | 10:05 to 14:15 |
| Close | 30 | 14:15 to 15:00 |

The verdict must be heard inside the first thirty seconds. It is said twice, word for
word, in sections 2 and 21. Both must survive the cut identical.

The timings come from a reading speed, not from the recording. Her actual pace sets them.
If the evidence movement runs long, the author has named the candidates for trimming:
section 12, on the daily slope, or section 17, on cortisol belly. Ask before cutting
either.

### 4.2 Picture

Recorded horizontal, 3840 by 2160, 25 fps, shutter 1/50, white balance locked near 4500 K,
on a phone, in her consulting room. Deliver at 1920 by 1080 for YouTube, from the 4K master.

She sits in the left or right third so a 9:16 column can be cut from the same take for
Shorts. Keep that in mind when you reframe.

Colour: natural, warm-neutral, faithful to the room. No look, no teal and orange, no heavy
contrast. She should look like a person in daylight.

### 4.3 What is on screen

Most of the time, the author. The other elements:

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Source band | One line in IBM Plex Mono at the foot of frame, ink on cream, sentence case, in the `CITATIONS.md` form. Enters on the first spoken word of the sentence it supports. Holds at least four seconds. Never under a sentence marked proposed. |
| Name band | Same geometry as the source band. Section 3, first two sentences. Never at the same time as a source band. |
| Claim card | Section 4. The patient's words, large, ink, no red. At least six seconds. |
| Verdict | Sections 2 and 21. Motion piece M8. Identical both times. |
| Three questions | Section 23. Motion piece M7. In the book's printed words. |
| Drawn pieces | M1 to M6, at the sections in 5.1. |
| Title card | Under three seconds at the open. See `IDENTITY.md`. |
| End card | Fifteen seconds. The book's cover, flat, no shadow. See `IDENTITY.md`. |

The band text and its source for every sentence are in `CUES.md`. Use that file, not the
script brackets, as the source of truth for band wording.

### 4.4 Captions

No burned-in captions. Upload a caption file made from the corrected transcript of the
final cut, not from the script. Where she departed from the script, the caption follows
her words. The transcript is also published on the book's website, so it must be exact.

### 4.5 Shorts

From the same take, one or two Shorts under sixty seconds: the opening thirty seconds
reframed, or one sourced evidence passage. Each keeps its source band, reset for vertical
and clear of YouTube's overlay at the foot of the frame. `PRODUCTION.md` has the measured
safe area. Check it on a phone before delivery.

### 4.6 Accuracy check before lock

Send a transcript of the locked-candidate cut with timecodes. It is checked against
`SCRIPT.md` and `RESEARCH.md` for every number, every source name and year, every
attribution, and every sentence near a proposal. Picture locks only after that check
comes back clean or with fixes.

Known point to watch: in section 18 the script reads "I looked for a peer-reviewed
validation". The search was run by her team. She may have said "we looked". Either is
fine; the caption must match what she said.

## 5. Animation

### 5.1 The pieces

Eight pieces exist as working animatics, rendered at 1920 by 1080, 25 fps. They show the
intended content, order and timing. Your animators may rebuild them at higher craft. They
must keep the figure, the grammar and every sourced value.

| Piece | Section | Shows | Sourced value | Length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | 6 | The chain: hypothalamus, pituitary, adrenal glands at the mid-back, the signal by blood, the feedback line | None; the chain is Ulrich-Lai & Herman, 2009 | 13 s |
| M2 | 7 | The figure wakes, the day's curve with hourly pulses, a swab at the mouth, one red sample on the morning rise | Hours on the axis only | 13 s |
| M3 | 7, second half | The half hour after waking: the curve rises out of the body | 50 to 75 percent; Pruessner et al., 1997 | 12 s |
| M4 | 9 | Two clocks: the heart jumps and settles; the adrenal line rises late and peaks in a band | 21 to 40 min; Dickerson & Kemeny, 2004 | 14 s |
| M5 | 10 | Chronic stress: the figure near onset with a high curve, then persisting with a lower, flatter one | No values; Miller, Chen & Zhou, 2007 | 14 s |
| M6 | 14 | Hair from the crown with months along it; one red drop from the mouth, minutes | No values; Stalder et al., 2017 | 10 s |
| M7 | 23 | The three questions, entering one at a time | The book's printed words | 10 s |
| M8 | 2 and 21 | The verdict, "level" turning red last | None | 6 s |

`motion/README.md` gives each piece in detail. It also lists what is a drawing device
rather than a claim. Examples: the slump of the second figure in M5 says nothing about
posture. The three marks on the hair in M6 are not centimetres per month. In M4 only the
shaded band sits on a measured scale. A rebuild must not turn a device into a claim, for
example by putting a scale on the M5 curves.

### 5.2 The figure and the line

One recurring figure, the same in every piece: faceless, a round head, a plain neck and
shoulder line, drawn as a hand would draw it. Organs are drawn where they are. The adrenal
glands sit on the kidneys at the mid-back, never shown through the belly. The figure
should feel like a clinician's sketch, not a character. No faces, no expressions, no
clothing, no hair styling.

Line: ink `#111111` on cream `#F7F3EC`, roughly 3 px at 1920 wide, with a slight
hand-drawn irregularity. Drawing order follows a hand: body, then organ, then signal, with
small pauses.

### 5.3 Motion grammar

One thing moves at a time. Each piece holds its last frame so she can keep talking over
it. No camera moves on the drawings, no parallax, no 3D, no bounce or overshoot, no
particles, no kinetic type. One red element per frame at most, cover red `#B5291C`, for the
thing the sentence is about. Nothing decorative.

The pieces must land on her voice. Each drawing should complete a beat before she
finishes the sentence it illustrates. Time them against the recording, not against the
animatic.

### 5.4 Where the drawings sit in frame

Your call. Options: full-frame cutaways, or the drawing on cream beside her in a split.
Full-frame is cleaner and matches the animatics. Whatever you choose, keep it the same
across the episode.

## 6. Art direction

Everything is in `IDENTITY.md`. The essentials:

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Ground | Cream `#F7F3EC` |
| Ink | `#111111` |
| Accent, one job per frame | Cover red `#B5291C` |
| Quiet labels | `#6F6A62` |
| Words, display | IvyPresto Display (Adobe Fonts); substitute Instrument Serif. Never bold. |
| Body | Source Sans 3 |
| Small labels, caps | DIN Alternate 700; substitute Barlow Semi Condensed 700 |
| Numbers, sources | IBM Plex Mono |

Forbidden, from the author's brief: gradients, glass, glow, blur, drop shadows, 3D,
mockups, icon sets, stock footage, stock imagery, emoji, kinetic typography, bold serif,
any new colour or size, any statistic or claim the sources do not make. The three coloured
"system inks" that appear in the book are not used in the series.

Thumbnail, title card, end card, banner and avatar are specified in `IDENTITY.md`, with a
rendered preview in `identity-preview.png`. The series title is not yet decided. Leave the
title slot open on the cards until the author confirms it.

## 7. Sound

Her voice is the content. Treat it as a spoken-word programme.

Clean the dialogue: gentle noise reduction from the room tone recorded at the end of the
take, de-ess, light compression, no heavy processing. She should sound like herself in a
quiet room, not like a radio voice.

Loudness: deliver around minus 14 LUFS integrated, true peak no higher than minus 1 dBTP,
which suits YouTube's normalisation.

Music: not decided. The author's lean is restraint. If you propose music, propose one
sparse, unobtrusive bed for the title, the end card and perhaps transitions between
movements, and none under the evidence. No stings, no swells, no sound effects on the
drawings. Silence under the drawings is acceptable and may be best.

## 8. Publishing materials

with identifiers, the website and transcript links. Chapters follow the five rows of the
table in 4.1. `PRODUCTION.md` has the template.

The episode title and the series title are the author's to set.

1. Rough cut with animatics in place, for structure and pace.
2. The accuracy check in 4.6, run on the transcript of the fine cut.
3. Fine cut with final animation, graphics and sound.
4. Lock and deliver.
