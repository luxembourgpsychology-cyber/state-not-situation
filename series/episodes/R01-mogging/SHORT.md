*0:35 to 0:45. Host. Source band again: Germine et al., 2015. Current Biology 25.*

**5. In a twin study from 2015, about half the variation in how people rated faces came from shared taste.** The rest was the rater's own. [Germine et al., 2015, 58]

*0:45 to 0:53. Host, to the lens, same pace. No band.*

**6. If worrying about your looks is taking over your days, see a doctor.** Every source is in the long version.

*0:53 to 0:59. She says it to the lens. On "A number" the same sentence comes up as a

**7. Looks matter.** A number that depends on who's looking isn't a fact about your face.

## What the editor changed, in full

Narrowed, three. Langlois and colleagues "pooled eleven meta-analyses" became "across
eleven meta-analyses": they ran eleven, they did not pool other people's. "Wanting to
look good isn't shallow. It's accurate" became a sentence about noticing. "If your looks
are taking over your days" became "if worrying about your looks is taking over your
days", which is what a clinician asks about.

Cut, three factual sentences. "This scale is supposed to be objective", which rests on a
meme site's wording the fact-check could not check as a quotation; it survives only in
opening B. "That's a twin study from 2015, and the source is on your screen", which the
band and section 5 cover. And the sentence about identical and non-identical twins.

The funny lines, tested aloud. Kept: "You're not shallow. You're accurate." Kept: "That
one's a joke. Keep it." Kept: the ruler and the desk, now with the sentence that makes it
land, "Face ratings don't work like that." None is aimed at him.

## For the author

"Half", without "about", in the first sentence. The fact-check's own narrowed hook says
it that way, and section 5 says "about half" forty seconds later. If you want "about" up
front, the sentence ends at 0:04.4 instead of 0:04.

"That one's a joke" can be heard two ways: that the word is used as a joke, which is
Collins's point, or that the word is ridiculous. "Keep it" settles it in his favour, but
it is your mouth and your call.

Section 6 does not name a condition. The long version does, with its source and the
crisis sentence, and `VIRAL.md` advises against labelling the viewer in the short. If you
want the name here, the sentence the fact-check supports is: "The diagnostic manual lists
comparing your appearance among one disorder's features, and one feature isn't a
diagnosis." It costs twenty words and section 5 would have to go.

No face is described, no scale carries a number, nobody is named, and no practice is
shown or explained. If a platform classifier is a worry, the card works with the ticks
removed and the plain rule left.
