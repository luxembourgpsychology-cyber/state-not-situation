# R01. Mogging. The sixty-second vertical. Script

Written 10 November 2026 against `FACTCHECK.md` of the same day, which governs every fact
here, and against the ten-second template in `series/WHY-IT-WORKS.md`, section 6. A
working draft until the author has read it. It is the door to `SCRIPT.md`: the first three
sections below are, word for word, section 1 of the horizontal.

How to read this file. Numbered sections, each opening with a fixed sentence in bold.
Citations in square brackets carry the `CITATIONS.md` number and are not spoken. Stage
notes are in italics and are not spoken. Every frame is in the series' identity: cream
ground, ink line, serif type, a mono source band, and one red element at a time. On the
vertical the band is re-set at the Short's size and sits 400 pixels up from the foot, as
`PRODUCTION.md` requires.

Who it is spoken to. The young man inside the trend, as an equal. It has to survive being
sent to him by his sister.

Count and pace. 164 spoken words with opening A, inside the 140 to 165 asked for, which
is sixty seconds at about 165 words a minute. The template's first three time slots are
nominal: twenty words do not fit in four seconds at any pace she should speak at. The
real clock is given beside each. What is fixed is the order, and that the surprising
sentence is finished before the tenth second.

Three sources, the template's limit: Germine 2015, Langlois 2000, and Collins 2026 on the
card.

## The script, with opening A

*Template slot 0:00 to 0:02. Real clock 0:00 to 0:03. The artefact card fills the frame:
cream, the word "mogging" as a dictionary entry in the serif, "noun, slang" in mono, the
gloss "outshining someone or something", and in small mono "Collins Word of the Year,
3 November 2026". The ink rule under the entry is drawn as a scale: one line, ten ticks,
no numbers. One tick is red. Her voice only.*

**A1. This scale is supposed to be objective.**

*Template slot 0:02 to 0:06. Real clock 0:03 to 0:12. Cut to the host on "About half".
Source band up for the whole section: Germine et al., 2015. Current Biology 25. No red on
this frame.*

**A2. About half the variation in face ratings comes from the rater, not the face.** That's a twin study from 2015, and the source is on your screen. [Germine et al., 2015, 58]

*Template slot 0:06 to 0:10. Real clock 0:12 to 0:19. Host. The source band leaves; the
name band enters: Ivana Budišin, CLINICAL PSYCHOLOGIST. On "Here's the word" the name band
leaves.*

**A3. I'm a clinical psychologist.** The trend is right about one thing, and it's wrong about one word. Here's the word.

*The right thing. Real clock 0:19 to 0:32. Host. Source band from "Langlois": Langlois et
al., 2000. Psychological Bulletin 126. It leaves before "So wanting".*

**A4. The right thing: looks do change how people treat you.** Langlois and colleagues pooled eleven meta-analyses in 2000. Attractive people were judged, and treated, more positively. [Langlois et al., 2000, 82] So wanting to look good isn't shallow. It's accurate.

*The word. Real clock 0:32 to 0:41. A word card: "objective" alone in the serif on cream,
in cover red. It is the one red element. On "Not mogging" the artefact card's entry
returns small above it, in ink. On "Hand a ruler" cut back to the host.*

**A5. The word is "objective".** Not "mogging": that one's a joke, keep it. Hand a ruler to anyone and your desk stays the same width.

*One fact. Real clock 0:41 to 0:48. Host. Source band again: Germine et al., 2015. Current
Biology 25.*

**A6. In that twin study, identical twins weren't significantly more alike in their taste than non-identical twins.** [Germine et al., 2015, 58]

*The line to repeat. Real clock 0:48 to 0:54. She says it once to the lens. Then the same
sentence as a card, serif on cream, with "who's looking" in red, held under section A8.*

**A7. So looks matter, and a number that depends on who's looking isn't a fact about your face.**

*The referral and the door. Real clock 0:54 to 1:00. Her voice over the card. The last
frame is the thumbnail composition, so what he sees is what he clicks.*

**A8. If your looks are taking over your days, see a doctor.** Every source is in the long version.

## Three openings for the first six seconds

Each replaces sections A1 and A2 only. Section A3 follows unchanged in all three.

### Opening A. The scale. Recommended.

As written above. It replies to the artefact, it carries a number, and the number is about
the scale and not about him. It also sets up the word: he hears "objective" in the first
second and gets it back as the answer at 0:32. This is hook 1 as the fact-check narrowed
it, with "variation" in the sentence.

### Opening B. The peg.

*The same artefact card. On "Collins" a source band: Collins, 2026. Word of the Year,
3 November, as reported by CP24.*

**B1. Mogging is supposed to be a joke.**

**B2. Collins made it word of the year this month, and says it's used in a playful and ironic way.** The scale under it isn't a joke. The source is on your screen. [Collins, 2026, 79]

If she takes B: section A5 loses "Not 'mogging': that one's a joke, keep it", and section
A6 becomes "A twin study from 2015: about half the variation in face ratings comes from
the rater, not the face.", with the same band. The count is then 164. B is the gentlest and the most searchable this month. It is second
because its surprising sentence is a judgement and not a number. This is hook 13 as
narrowed: "playful and ironic", never "mostly".

### Opening C. The concession.

*The artefact card for C1. Cut to the host on "Partly". Source band: Langlois et al., 2000.
Psychological Bulletin 126.*

**C1. Looks are supposed to decide how people treat you.**

**C2. Partly, they do: across eleven meta-analyses, attractive people were judged and treated more positively.** That part of looksmaxxing is true. The source is on your screen. [Langlois et al., 2000, 82]

If she takes C: section A4 shrinks to its last two sentences, and section A6 opens with
"A twin study from 2015: about half the variation in face ratings comes from the rater,
not the face." before the sentence about the twins. The count is then 158. C is the sentence he
least expects from a psychologist, and it is hook 2, which survived the fact-check whole.
It is third only because it spends the concession in the first six seconds and leaves
"the trend is right about one thing" with nothing new to give.

## Notes for the editor

No face is described, no scale carries a number, nobody is named, and no practice is
shown or explained. The scale on the artefact card stays empty from first frame to last.
If a platform classifier is a worry, the card works with the ticks removed and the plain
rule left.

The lines written to land: "So wanting to look good isn't shallow. It's accurate." and
"Hand a ruler to anyone and your desk stays the same width." Both are aimed at the
measuring, not at him.

Section A8 does not name a condition. The long version does, with its source and the
crisis sentence, and `VIRAL.md` advises against labelling the viewer in the short. If the
author wants the name here, the sentence that the fact-check supports is: "The diagnostic
manual lists comparing your appearance among one disorder's features, and one feature
isn't a diagnosis." It costs twenty words and something else has to go.

Publish the long version first, then this the same hour, because section A8 points at it.
