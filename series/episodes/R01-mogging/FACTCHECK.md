# R01 Mogging: fact-check, 10 November 2026

Survived: the secret shopper (3), the afternoon fall and the week of short sleep (4), the WHO azoospermia figure (5), the Endocrine Society wording (6), the two population-decline papers (7), the forum study's authors and its count of 8,072 comments (10), Joel, Eastwick and Finkel (12), Langlois and Hamermesh (13), the Mew erasure and the dismissed appeal (8, first half), Veale, Bjornsson, Angelakis, the fluoxetine trial and the CBT meta-analysis (9, most of it), YouTube's 2023 rule (14, first half), and hooks 2, 7, 12 and 14.
Narrowed: Germine (1: the figure is about variation in ratings, not about one rating, and not "48 percent of the time"), the low T study (2: the percentages are the university's, not seen in the paper), the British Orthodontic Society wording (8), the DSM criterion and the NICE detail (9: secondary summaries only), Collins and Dictionary.com (11: no "mostly", no "2016", and 26 October, not 27), TikTok's rules (14: a tracked copy and 2024 coverage only), and hooks 1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13 and 15.
Failed: the 0.22 twin correlation (not found in any record of the paper), "two people agree 48 percent of the time", "nearly every thread held an insult" (hook 5, struck), "around 2016", "mostly in a playful and ironic way" as Collins's words, 27 October for Dictionary.com, "the others did not" in hook 15, and the British Orthodontic Society sentence about changing the shape of the face.

## Method

Every item below was checked by web search run on 10 November 2026. Direct fetches were refused for every host tried (ocf.berkeley.edu, eurekalert.org, sydneyhealthliteracylab.org.au), so no publisher page, no PubMed page and no full text was opened. What is called confirmed was returned by search from an abstract, a repository record (PubMed, PMC, a university repository, RePEc, the National Archives case-law index) or at least two independent reports. Where a figure was seen in one secondary report only, that is said. Nothing was accepted from memory, and no reference was repaired from memory: where a co-author's initials or a page range was not returned, it is left open.

## 1. Germine et al. (2015), Current Biology, and Hönekopp (2006)

Confirmed. Germine, Russell, Bronstad, Blokland, Smoller, Kwok, Anthony, Nakayama, Rhodes and Wilmer, "Individual aesthetic preferences for faces are shaped mostly by environments, not genes", Current Biology 25(20), 2684 to 2689, 19 October 2015, DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2015.08.048. 547 identical twin pairs and 214 same-sex non-identical pairs rated 200 faces; more than 35,000 volunteers took the test on TestMyBrain.org. The PMC record (PMC4629915), as returned by search, says that 48% of the variation in face ratings can be explained by common preferences, with the remaining 52% attributable to individual face preferences. One summary adds that this is after adjusting for a rater's inconsistency with himself. The authors' own rounder statement, in the press material, is that an individual's aesthetic preferences for faces "agree about 50 percent, and disagree about 50 percent, with others". The identical and non-identical twin correlations "did not significantly differ" (p = 0.1), which is the basis for the title: individual preferences come mostly from environments unique to the person.

Narrowed. The finding is about variation across many ratings of many faces. It is not that any single rating is half the rater, and it is not that two people agree "48 percent of the time", which is how RESEARCH-social.md puts it. The figure is a share of variance, and it comes from one set of 200 faces and one population of raters.

Failed. The 0.22 figure. No record returned today attaches 0.22 to this paper, as a twin correlation or as anything else. A later review gives heritability estimates of 22% and 30% for individual face preferences across this literature without mapping either number to Germine. Until the paper's Table 1 is read, 0.22 is not spoken, and the line "even your identical twin does not share it" is cut back to what was seen: identical twins were not significantly more alike in their taste than non-identical twins.

Hönekopp (2006), cross-check. Confirmed: "Once more: Is beauty in the eye of the beholder? Relative contributions of private and shared taste to judgments of facial attractiveness", Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance 32(2), 199 to 209. The abstract, as returned, says three experiments with diverse face and rater samples found that "private taste is about as powerful as shared taste", contrary to the prevalent notion in the literature. The two papers agree. The URL VIRAL.md gives for it (journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1068/p5793) is a different journal's DOI and should be removed.

The sentence a script may say: "In a twin study of 2015, about half of the variation in how people rated faces came from taste people share, and about half from the individual rater's own taste (Germine et al., 2015)."

Is "about half the rating is the rater" fair? As a headline standing alone, no: it must be narrowed, because it reads as a decomposition of one score. It is fair only if the word "variation" (or "difference") is in the sentence, or if the precise sentence follows in the same breath. The corrected hook is in section 15.

## 2. Gram et al. (2026), Social Science & Medicine

Confirmed. Gram, Mintzes, Copp, Moynihan, Brown, Shih and Nickel, "Selling masculinity: A qualitative analysis of gender representations in social media content about 'low T'", Social Science & Medicine 393, 118903, DOI 10.1016/j.socscimed.2025.118903, online 26 December 2025 (the date from one report), University of Sydney release 3 February 2026. A qualitative content analysis of 46 high-reach Instagram and TikTok posts promoting testosterone tests and treatment, with four themes. One secondary report says the 46 were drawn from 200 posts identified in January 2024, 30 from Instagram and 16 from TikTok.

Narrowed. The percentages were not seen in the abstract or in any record of the paper itself. "None of the posts cited scientific evidence", "about 67 per cent provided links to purchase products or schedule consultations" and "31 per cent of individual influencers claimed to be medical doctors" are in the university's release and its syndication. "72% of social media posts analysed had financial interests, such as selling testosterone tests, treatments or supplements, or were sponsored by industry" was returned from secondary reports; it refers to posts, not accounts. "85 percent published by individuals rather than health organizations" was returned by one search summary of press coverage and not found again. The combined audience of 6.8 million followers was returned once, from coverage. So each of these is attributed to the university or "the researchers report", never to the journal page, until the paper is read.

Unconfirmed, not to be spoken: 85 percent as a figure from the paper; 6.8 million on screen.

## 3. Dubin et al. (2022), JAMA Internal Medicine

Confirmed. Research letter, JAMA Internal Medicine 182(12), 1321 to 1323, DOI 10.1001/jamainternmed.2022.4928. Seven United States direct-to-consumer platforms were assessed. The secret shopper followed a script for a 34-year-old man with low energy and low libido who wanted future fertility, and reported normal total and free testosterone. Six of the seven platforms (85.7%) offered him testosterone therapy. Six had no testosterone threshold for starting treatment. He was also offered a range of off-label medications. Only one of the seven asked about recent cardiovascular events or his wish for future fertility.

Not re-verified today: "five of those six did not discuss polycythaemia" (RESEARCH-testosterone.md). Leave it out unless the letter is read.

## 4. Brambilla et al. (2009) and Leproult and Van Cauter (2011)

Confirmed. Brambilla, Matsumoto, Araujo and McKinlay, Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism 94(3), 907 to 913, DOI 10.1210/jc.2008-1902. Sixty-six men aged 30 to 80 from the Boston Area Community Health Survey, sampled on three mornings and three afternoons. In men 30 to 40 years old, testosterone was 20 to 25% lower at 16:00 than at 08:00; the gap fell with age to about 10% at 70. The authors concluded that measurement should be restricted to the morning in young and older men alike. How many of the 66 were aged 30 to 40 was not seen, so the script does not give a number of young men.

Confirmed. Leproult and Van Cauter, JAMA 305(21), 2173 to 2174, DOI 10.1001/jama.2011.710, a research letter. Ten healthy young men; after a week of sleep restricted to five hours a night, daytime testosterone was 10 to 15% lower. The letter itself calls them "this small convenience sample". The university's account gives eight nights and a fall in sleep from 8 hours 55 minutes to 4 hours 48 minutes. The comparison with "ageing 10 to 15 years" is press-release language and is not spoken.

## 5. WHO Task Force (1990), The Lancet

Confirmed from the abstract (PMID 1977002). The Lancet 336, 955 to 959. Ten centres in seven countries, 271 healthy fertile men, 200 mg testosterone enanthate by weekly injection. 157 men became azoospermic, a cumulative rate at six months of 65%. After the injections stopped, the estimated median time from azoospermia to recovery of a sperm concentration of at least 20 million per millilitre was 3.7 months. It was a contraceptive trial in volunteers on a fixed dose; the script says so and does not transfer the percentage to clinic doses.

## 6. Bhasin et al. (2018), the Endocrine Society guideline

Confirmed. Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism 103(5), 1715 to 1744. Recommendation 1.2, verbatim as returned from the society's full-text page: "We recommend against routine screening of men in the general population for hypogonadism." Diagnosis requires symptoms and signs of testosterone deficiency and "unequivocally and consistently low" serum total or free testosterone, measured fasting in the morning with a reliable assay and repeated to confirm. The guideline's stated reason for repeating, as reported: about 30% of men with a first value in the low range have a normal value on repeat.

## 7. Travison et al. (2007) and Andersson et al. (2007)

Confirmed. Travison, Araujo, O'Donnell, Kupelian and McKinlay, JCEM 92(1), 196 to 202, DOI 10.1210/jc.2006-1375. Massachusetts Male Aging Study, three waves between 1987 and 2004, randomly selected men aged 45 to 79. The authors report a substantial age-independent decline in testosterone that does not appear to be attributable to observed changes in explanatory factors such as smoking and obesity. The size, 1.2% lower per calendar year in age-matched men (95% CI 1.0 to 1.4), was returned from trade coverage and not from the abstract; the per-wave counts of 1,374, 906 and 489 were not returned at all (one summary gives 1,532 men). Say "about one percent a year" and credit the paper; leave the wave counts out.

Confirmed. Andersson and colleagues, JCEM 92(12), from page 4696. 5,350 serum samples from four Danish population surveys, 1982 to 2001. A cohort and period decline in testosterone and SHBG was found; adjusting for the concurrent rise in body mass index reduced the testosterone changes, "which no longer were significant", while the SHBG changes stayed significant. So RESEARCH-testosterone.md is right on testosterone and should add the SHBG half.

Both are older or middle-aged or general adult samples. Neither is a study of young men, and the script says so.

## 8. Mike Mew, the regulator, the court, the societies

Confirmed. The General Dental Council's Professional Conduct Committee determined on 6 November 2024 that Michael Mew's fitness to practise was impaired by misconduct and directed that his name be erased from the Dentists' Register; the charges concerned treatment and claims between September 2013 and May 2019. Because he appealed, the register showed him as suspended while the appeal ran. *Mew v General Dental Council* [2026] EWHC 1116 (Admin), Charles Bagot KC sitting as a Deputy High Court Judge, judgment dated 15 May 2026: the appeal was dismissed on every ground, per the National Archives index and the case note of the regulator's counsel. The transcript itself was not read.

Narrowed. Say "the committee ordered his erasure on 6 November 2024 and the High Court dismissed his appeal on 15 May 2026". Do not say he "was struck off on 6 November 2024" as a completed fact of that day.

Confirmed. American Association of Orthodontists, press release of January 2024, "AAO warns against unregulated practice of 'mewing'": the trend "lacks scientific backing"; its president Myron Guymon: "There's no scientific evidence to support its claims of reshaping the jawline, and the potential risks outweigh any unproven benefits." Seen through trade press quoting the release; the AAO also hosts a "Risks of Mewing" sheet (2025), not read.

Narrowed. British Orthodontic Society, statement after the decision, November 2024, as reported by GDPUK and dental trade press: views in orthodontics "must be supported by clinical evidence of the highest quality", and patients are urged to proceed with caution and seek a second opinion. The sentence in RESEARCH-clinical.md, that there is "no scientific evidence that people can change the shape of their face by holding teeth and tongue in a closed position", was not found in any result today and is not to be spoken as the society's words.

## 9. Body dysmorphic disorder

DSM-5-TR Criterion B. Narrowed to secondary sources. No result reproduced the manual's text. Coverage of the criterion as Katharine Phillips proposed it for DSM-5, a study of the DSM-5 criteria (Journal of Psychosomatic Research, 2015), BMJ Best Practice and the BDD Foundation's screening card agree on the substance: repetitive behaviours such as mirror checking, excessive grooming, skin picking or reassurance seeking, or mental acts such as comparing one's appearance with that of others, performed in response to the appearance concerns. The script may paraphrase this and attribute it to DSM-5-TR. It may not put the criterion on screen in quotation marks until the manual is opened. Comparing is an example of a mental act under one criterion; it is not itself "a criterion", and one criterion is not a diagnosis.

Prevalence. Confirmed from the PubMed abstract: Veale, Gledhill, Christodoulou and Hodsoll (2016), Body Image 18, 168 to 186, DOI 10.1016/j.bodyim.2016.07.003. Weighted prevalence 1.9% in community adults, 2.2% in adolescents, 3.3% in students.

Onset. Confirmed: Bjornsson, Didie, Grant, Menard, Stalker and Phillips (2013), Comprehensive Psychiatry 54(7), 893 to 903, DOI 10.1016/j.comppsych.2013.03.019. Mean age at onset 16.7 years in both samples; about two thirds had onset before 18. That early onset went with more suicide attempts was returned from a later book chapter, not the abstract; say "about two thirds began before eighteen" and leave the attempts comparison out.

Suicidality. Confirmed: Angelakis, Gooding and Panagioti (2016), Clinical Psychology Review 49, 55 to 66, DOI 10.1016/j.cpr.2016.08.002. Seventeen studies; OR 3.63 (95% CI 2.62 to 4.63) for suicidality, 3.30 for attempts, 2.57 for ideation; the authors call the studies few and of low quality, with evidence on deaths scarce. Spoken as "about three and a half times the odds" for suicidality overall, with the caution; ideation alone is 2.57.

Treatment. NICE CG31, "Obsessive-compulsive disorder and body dysmorphic disorder: treatment", published 29 November 2005: confirmed as existing and current; the adult stepped care (CBT for mild impairment, CBT or an SSRI for moderate, both for severe; CBT to include exposure and response prevention) was seen through the BDD Foundation's summary and a secondary site, and the wording for under-18s was not returned. Narrowed: "NICE recommends cognitive behavioural therapy, an SSRI, or both, according to severity." Fluoxetine: confirmed from the abstract, Phillips, Albertini and Rasmussen (2002), Archives of General Psychiatry 59(4), 381 to 388; 67 randomised, 12 weeks; 18 of 34 (53%) responded to fluoxetine and 6 of 33 (18%) to placebo. CBT: confirmed from the abstract, Harrison, Fernández de la Cruz, Enander, Radua and Mataix-Cols (2016), Clinical Psychology Review 48, 43 to 51; seven randomised trials, N = 299; CBT superior to waitlist or credible placebo (delta = -1.22), gains held at two to four months in three studies.

## 10. Halpin et al. (2025)

Confirmed. Halpin, M., Gosse, M., Yeo, K., Handlovsky, I., and Maguire, F., "When help is harm: Health, lookism and self-improvement in the manosphere", Sociology of Health & Illness 47(3), e70015, DOI 10.1111/1467-9566.70015, PMID 40069550. The PubMed abstract says the authors "qualitatively analysed 8072 discussion board comments in total" from a looksmaxxing community with 6 million unique visitors a month. The figure 5,327 appears in a PMC snippet as the comments on one looksmaxxing board; press coverage says the sample came from two of the forum's main boards in 2023. So 8,072 is the total and 5,327 is one part of it. Safest spoken form: "more than eight thousand comments".

Failed. No source gives a share of threads containing an insult. "Nearly every" and "every" are both unsupported. Coverage does confirm, in Halpin's words to the CBC, that users regularly encouraged one another toward suicide. Note that the CBC piece misnames the journal.

## 11. Collins and Dictionary.com

Confirmed. Collins announced "mogging" as its Word of the Year 2026 on Tuesday 3 November 2026. Defined as slang meaning to outshine someone or something. Use up 210% in 2026. Collins says the word has been around since the 2010s and that Generations Z and Alpha "have now firmly adopted it in a playful and ironic way". Origin as Collins gives it, via CNN/CP24: believed to come from the acronym AMOG, "alpha male of the group", first a noun in bodybuilding culture for the most physically dominant man in a gym, later made a verb and "briefly adopted by the manosphere". Euronews (3 November) supplies the distinction that looksmaxxing is effort on one's own appearance while mogging "implies more of a comparison", says the word is playful now "even if it has a darker backstory", and lists stylemogging, carmogging and lifemogging. The shortlist included firewave, hydration break and Manchesterism.

Narrowed. "Mostly" is not in any quotation seen: Collins says "in a playful and ironic way", not "mostly in a playful and ironic way". "Around 2016" was not supported by any result: say "the 2010s". The comparison gloss is Euronews's, not Collins's.

Confirmed with a corrected date. Dictionary.com named "maxxing" its 2026 Word of the Year on Monday 26 October 2026 (Ground News, Euronews of that date); the Boston Globe piece is dated 27 October. Described as short for "maximizing", usable alone or as a suffix; shortlist affordability, data center, odyssey, unc. Change "27 October" to "26 October" in VIRAL.md and RESEARCH-social.md, or say "the last week of October".

## 12. Joel, Eastwick and Finkel (2017)

Confirmed from the abstract as held by the UK Data Service. "Is romantic desire predictable? Machine learning applied to initial romantic attraction", Psychological Science, online 30 August 2017, DOI 10.1177/0956797617714580 (volume 28(10), 1478 to 1489 per the research file). Two speed-dating samples; more than a hundred self-reported traits and preferences; four-minute dates. Random forest models predicted 4 to 18% of actor variance (how much a person tends to desire others) and 7 to 27% of partner variance (how much a person tends to be desired), and were unable to predict relationship variance, the desire of one particular person for another, from anything reported beforehand. The sample size of 350 and the dates of the events were not re-verified.

## 13. Langlois et al. (2000) and Hamermesh and Biddle (1994)

Confirmed. Langlois, Kalakanis, Rubenstein, Larson, Hallam and Smoot, Psychological Bulletin 126(3), 390 to 423, DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.126.3.390. Eleven meta-analyses: raters agree about who is attractive within and across cultures; attractive children and adults are judged more positively and treated more positively than unattractive ones, even by people who know them. Effect sizes were not seen and none is spoken. Note the tension the script must hold honestly: Langlois reports high agreement among raters on average, Germine and Hönekopp report that individual raters differ about as much as they agree. Both are true; averages of many raters agree far more than any two raters do.

Confirmed. Hamermesh and Biddle, American Economic Review 84(5), 1174 to 1194. From the abstract: "The plainness penalty is 5 to 10 percent, slightly larger than the beauty premium", with effects for men at least as great as for women. Interviewer ratings of looks, United States and Canadian surveys of the 1970s and 1981.

## 15. The fifteen hooks

2. Survived. "Looks do change how people treat you. That part of looksmaxxing is true." Langlois et al. (2000): attractive people are judged and treated more positively.
3. Narrowed. "The dictionary calls mogging outshining. The diagnostic manual lists comparing your looks among one disorder's criteria." The next sentence must say one criterion is not a diagnosis. DSM wording by paraphrase only.
4. Narrowed. "Forty-six testosterone posts were studied. The researchers report none cited scientific evidence." Source on screen: Gram et al. (2026) for the 46; University of Sydney (2026) for "none".
5. Struck. No source for "nearly every". Replacement, if wanted: "Researchers read eight thousand comments on one forum. They called what they found demoralisation." (Halpin et al., 2025).
6. Narrowed. "Mogging came out of gym slang in the 2010s. Last week it was word of the year." Not "around 2016". "Last week" holds only for publication by 15 November.
7. Survived. "Collins counted mogging up 210 percent this year. Many people still can't define it." The second sentence is LBC's characterisation, shown as such.
8. Narrowed. "PSL is usually read as three forums. Two had hate in their names." PUAHate and SlutHate both do; "one" understates what the source shows.

## Safe to say on camera

1. In a twin study of 2015, about half of the variation in how people rated faces came from taste people share, and about half from the individual rater's own taste (Germine et al., 2015).
2. In that study identical twins were not significantly more alike in their taste in faces than non-identical twins (Germine et al., 2015).
3. Three earlier experiments reached the same conclusion: private taste is about as powerful as shared taste (Hönekopp, 2006).
4. Across eleven meta-analyses, attractive children and adults were judged more positively and treated more positively than unattractive ones (Langlois et al., 2000).
5. In one classic study of earnings, the penalty for plain looks was 5 to 10 percent, slightly larger than the premium for good looks (Hamermesh & Biddle, 1994).
6. From more than a hundred traits measured beforehand, models could predict part of who tends to be desired, and none of who would desire whom in particular (Joel et al., 2017).
7. Collins named "mogging" its word of the year on 3 November 2026, defined it as outshining someone or something, and said younger speakers use it in a playful and ironic way (Collins, as reported by CP24, 2026).
8. Researchers analysed more than eight thousand comments from a looksmaxxing forum with six million visitors a month, and described what the forum does to its users as demoralisation (Halpin et al., 2025).
9. The diagnostic manual lists comparing one's appearance with that of others among the repetitive behaviours and mental acts of body dysmorphic disorder; that one feature alone is not a diagnosis (American Psychiatric Association, 2022).
10. Body dysmorphic disorder affects about 2 percent of adults in the community and about 2 percent of adolescents (Veale et al., 2016).
11. On average it begins at sixteen to seventeen, and in about two thirds of people before eighteen (Bjornsson et al., 2013).
12. It carries about three and a half times the odds of suicidal thoughts or attempts taken together, from studies the authors themselves rate as few and of low quality (Angelakis et al., 2016).
13. It is treatable: across seven randomised trials, cognitive behavioural therapy did better than waiting or a placebo therapy (Harrison et al., 2016).
14. In the one placebo-controlled trial of fluoxetine, 53 percent responded against 18 percent on placebo (Phillips et al., 2002).
15. The General Dental Council's committee ordered Mike Mew's erasure from the register on 6 November 2024, and the High Court dismissed his appeal on 15 May 2026 (*Mew v General Dental Council*, 2026).
16. The American Association of Orthodontists says there is no scientific evidence that mewing reshapes the jawline (American Association of Orthodontists, 2024).
17. In men aged 30 to 40, testosterone measured at four in the afternoon was 20 to 25 percent lower than at eight in the morning (Brambilla et al., 2009).
18. In ten healthy young men, a week of five-hour nights lowered daytime testosterone by 10 to 15 percent (Leproult & Van Cauter, 2011).
19. The Endocrine Society recommends against routine screening of men in the general population for hypogonadism (Bhasin et al., 2018).
20. A diagnosis needs symptoms and signs together with consistently low testosterone, measured fasting in the morning and repeated (Bhasin et al., 2018).
21. A secret shopper presenting as a 34-year-old with normal testosterone who wanted children was offered testosterone by six of seven online platforms (Dubin et al., 2022).
22. In a World Health Organization trial, weekly testosterone injections left 65 percent of 271 healthy men with no sperm in their semen within six months (World Health Organization Task Force, 1990).
23. A study of 46 high-reach "low T" posts on Instagram and TikTok found testosterone sold to young, fit men as a matter of masculinity; the researchers report that none of the posts cited scientific evidence (Gram et al., 2026; University of Sydney, 2026).
24. One Boston-area study found men's testosterone about one percent lower per calendar year at the same age; a Danish study found the fall no longer significant once rising body weight was taken into account (Travison et al., 2007; Andersson et al., 2007).
25. Since November 2023 YouTube has limited repeated recommendations to teenagers of videos that compare physical features and idealise some types over others (YouTube, 2023). This is for the description or a card, not for the spoken script.

## Not to be said

- That identical twins' tastes in faces correlated 0.22. Not found in any record of Germine et al. (2015).
- That two people agree about a face "48 percent of the time". The 48 is a share of variance, not a frequency.
- "About half the rating is the rater", standing alone with no "variation" and no following sentence.
- That nearly every, or every, rating thread on the forum contained an insult. No source.
- 5,327 as the study's comment count. It is one board's share of the 8,072.
- That mogging began "around 2016", or that Collins says it is "mostly" playful. Collins says the 2010s, and "in a playful and ironic way".
- That Dictionary.com announced on 27 October. Reports place it on Monday 26 October.
- 85 percent of low T posts from individuals, 72 percent with financial interests, 67 percent with purchase links, or accounts with 6.8 million followers, as figures from the paper. They are the university's or the press's, and 85 and 6.8 million were each seen once. If 67 or 72 is used, it is "the university reports", and 72 percent is of posts.
- That five of six platforms did not discuss polycythaemia. Not re-verified today.
- That a week of short sleep "ages" testosterone by 10 to 15 years. Press-release language.
- The per-wave sample sizes of Travison et al. (2007), or 1.2 percent to the decimal as if read from the abstract.
- That the British Orthodontic Society said there is no scientific evidence people can change the shape of their face by tongue posture. Wording not found; only its call for high-quality clinical evidence and a second opinion was seen.
- That Mike Mew "was struck off on 6 November 2024" as a finished fact. The erasure was ordered that day and stood after the appeal failed in May 2026.
- The DSM-5-TR criterion in quotation marks, or "comparing is a criterion". Paraphrase only until the manual is opened.
- That early onset of body dysmorphic disorder means more suicide attempts, as a finding of Bjornsson et al. (2013). Seen only in a later chapter.
- NICE's recommendations for under-18s in any detail. Not returned.
- "The others did not" (hook 15), and 29 March as the Flinders date.
- "One had hate in its name" (hook 8). Two did.
