# Episode 2. "You need eight hours, and you can catch up at the weekend." Motion storyboard

Written 3 November 2026 for `SCRIPT.md` of the same day, as edited, and keyed to its section numbers, which are stable while the sentences move. The pieces are D1 to D4, as the script and `CUES.md` name them. Nothing here is rendered; this file is what the pieces must show, in what order, for how long, and what each mark is allowed to mean. The grammar is the second pass of `series/motion/README.md` and is not restated: the recurring faceless line figure of `setup-sketch.svg`, drawn at a hand's pace in the order a hand would draw her, 3px on the frame, the one noise filter, type outside the filter, serif for words, mono for anything measured, condensed caps for labels, cover red on at most one thing per frame, and the figure never given a face or a name. Every number drawn comes from this episode's `RESEARCH.md` or from the source record in `series/CITATIONS.md` that the cue sheet bands under the sentence, and the number is given below for each. The three system inks do not appear on any frame.

Each piece plays under a story and holds its end frame under the host's voice until the next SCREEN change in `CUES.md`. The durations given are the seconds in which something is drawn or moves; the rest of a piece's time on screen is a held frame. One thing moves at a time, and nothing moves while the host is saying a number the piece does not carry.

The continuity rule of `FORMAT.md` is drawn and never spoken. The week this figure is drawn through is the week the hair of episode 1's M6 grew, and the cortisol sample of episode 1's M2 was taken on one of these mornings. D3 carries both links; D1, D2 and D4 carry the figure's identity, path for path, from M1 and M2.

## D1. The cave. Sections 3 to 5, about 100 seconds on screen, 19 seconds of drawing

D1 begins on the first word of section 3 and holds its end frame through section 5. It is M2's lying figure, head to the left, on M2's rule at fourteen percent, and the first thing the viewer of episode 1 notices is what is missing: there is no hour axis above her. The cave has no daylight, so the frame has no 06, 12, 18, 24. The rule is drawn first, full width, in one second, and it is the one line of darkness the stage note asks for; nothing else stands for the cave, no walls, no rock. She is drawn on it in M2's order, head, neck, back, front, arm, legs, in five seconds, under "went a hundred and forty feet into a cave in Kentucky". She does not sit up. There is no six o'clock to sit up at.

Then the thermometer trace. Under "to live a twenty-eight-hour day", a slow wave leaves her mouth and runs the length of her body to her feet, one crest and most of a second, four seconds. The body is the clock's canvas: head to foot is the day they set themselves, and the wave is her temperature rising and falling across it. That the trace leaves the mouth is M2's device, the measurement taken from the body, and it is not a statement about where Kleitman took the temperature; the height and shape of the wave are a drawing of "rise and fall", not data. The frame holds through the rest of section 3.

In section 4, under "Twenty-eight hours, not twenty-four", a bracket is drawn over the full length of her body with 28 h in the mono at its centre, one second. That is the one measured thing on the frame and the one sourced value D1 carries: the twenty-eight-hour day of nineteen hours awake and nine asleep, Kleitman 1939, `CITATIONS.md` 48, which is the band under this sentence. Her wave carries no number, because the script says "roughly twenty-four hours" and "about a day", and a number would narrow what the source does not.

Under "Over the month, Richardson's temperature rhythm stretched to fit the longer day", a second figure is drawn above her, M2's lying path again, in ink at forty percent, on no rule of its own, four seconds; then his wave leaves his mouth and runs his length in exactly one crest, fitting the bracket head to foot, three seconds. Under "Kleitman's never did" nothing is drawn: her wave, already there, is seen to finish its crest before her feet and begin the next, so the two waves are read against the same bracket and one bends and one does not. The fainter figure is the student and she is given the rhythm that did not bend; neither figure is a portrait, she is not Kleitman and he is not Richardson, and the faintness says "the other body in the cave", not age, not sex, not anything about either man. That one wave fits the bracket and one does not is the finding; how much hers falls short, the number of crests and their heights are a drawing of "stretched" and "never did". The fifty-four degrees, the hundred and forty feet and the thirty-two days are spoken and not drawn.

Section 5's end frame: under "So before anyone had a number, there was a rhythm", a small empty gauge is drawn inside her head, a short vertical tube with a needle mark at its foot, two seconds, unlabelled. It is the gauge D3 fills. Nothing on D1 is red. The continuity link is the body itself: the lying figure, the rule and the trace from the mouth are M2's, path for path, and the viewer who watched her sit up at six in episode 1 now watches her lie through a day with no six in it.

## D2. The hand-off. Sections 6 to 8, about 85 seconds on screen, 17 seconds of drawing

D2 begins on the first word of section 6 and holds through section 8. It is M2's figure on M2's rule, drawn lying in five seconds under "The number arrived later, and it got rounder each time it changed hands", and then sat up by the one hip rotation of M2 and M3, held at its end so that she sits up in bed. Under "In 2017 Matthew Walker" a book is drawn in her hands, two boards and a spine, two seconds, with nothing printed on it. The book carries no title and no cover; the band under the sentence names it. She reads.

In section 7, under "for adults from eighteen to sixty-four, seven to nine hours", a ruler is drawn under the bed, along the rule, with a tick at every hour, three seconds; only two ticks are numbered, 7 and 9 in the mono, and they land as the bracket is drawn above them, in cover red, from seven to nine, over her, at the height of the bed, one and a half seconds. The eighth tick is there as a tick and carries no number: that is the stage note's "no mark at eight". The range seven to nine is the one sourced value D2 carries, Hirshkowitz et al. 2015, `CITATIONS.md` 47, the band under this sentence. The bracket is the one red thing on the frame, because the range is what the panel published, and the frame holds through "It doesn't give you the eight."

Section 8 draws the hand-off up her body. Under "the number they were handed was rounder than the evidence under it", a second bracket is drawn in ink above the first, at the height of the book in her hands, narrower, one and a half seconds. Under "A range of seven to nine became eight in the retelling", a third, narrower still, at the height of her mouth, one and a half seconds. Then, under "once the number was eight", her hand leaves the book and comes down to rest on the ruler at the eighth tick, two seconds. That is the end frame: the ruler, the red bracket, two ink brackets closing above it, and her hand on the eight that is not numbered. The paper, the book and the sentence are the three heights, the bed, the hands, the mouth, and nothing labels them.

The red bracket is a measurement. Everything above it is a drawing device: the two narrowing brackets claim no intermediate range and no count of retellings; they say only "rounder each time it changed hands", in the script's words. Her hand on the eight is the sentence, not a mark on the ruler; the ruler never acquires an eight. The figure claims nothing about who reads in bed. The continuity link is the sit-up: the same body, the same rule, the same rotation about the hip that woke her in M2 and M3.

## D3. Philadelphia. Sections 9 to 13, about 170 seconds on screen, 42 seconds of drawing

D3 begins on the first word of section 9 and holds through section 13. The figure is M1's standing figure at M5's 0.72, in profile at the left of the frame, facing right, drawn in M1's order in four seconds under "In Philadelphia, in 2003, healthy adults lived in a laboratory", with D1's gauge in her head, the tube and the needle at its foot. Under "for fourteen nights" a rule leaves the head at the gauge's height and runs right, ticked at each night, and the number 14 in the mono lands at its end as the fourteenth tick is drawn, three seconds. Fourteen nights is the one sourced value D3 carries, Van Dongen, Maislin, Mullington and Dinges 2003, `CITATIONS.md` 43, banded through sections 9 and 10. The hours in bed, the forty-eight, the two nights' equivalent and the 15.84 are spoken or withheld and are not drawn.

A note on the row's length. The stage note in section 9 says a row of seven days and the first sentence says fourteen nights. The row here is fourteen, because the gauge is asked to climb to "day fourteen" in section 10 and the one value on the frame must be true of the thing it is written on. The first seven nights are her week, the week of the hair and of D4's columns, and the weekend the stage notes place on the sixth and seventh mornings falls inside them; the second seven are where the short nights come back. The scriptwriter should confirm this reading or ask for the row to stop at seven, in which case 14 comes off the frame and the piece carries no number.

In section 10, under "The lapses rose night after night", the gauge works: the needle rises through each day and drops partway each night, so that each morning it rests higher than the last, and each morning's rest is carried out onto the row as a step, the line climbing from the head day by day, days one to five, ten seconds. The chart grows out of the body as M2's did. Under "Sleepiness did something else", a second line leaves the body under the first, with how tired I feel in the serif at its start; it steps up once at the first morning and runs flat, three seconds. Between the climbing steps and the flat line a flat fill in cover red opens from the second morning and widens each day; it is the one red thing on the frame and it is the gap, the thing the authors say the subjects did not feel. The frame holds through the quoted sentence.

Section 11 is the catching up. Under "Now the catching up", on the sixth and seventh mornings the needle drops, and the step line with it, but not to the bottom, three seconds; the red fill narrows and does not close. Under "didn't bring them back to baseline" nothing moves, so the sentence lands on a needle visibly off the floor. Then, under section 13's first sentence, "Then the short nights came back, and the body hadn't reset", the needle starts climbing from where it stopped, from the eighth morning, and the step line climbs on to the fourteenth at the pace of section 10, twelve seconds spread under sections 12 and 13. That the weekend drains the gauge partway and not to the bottom is Belenky et al. 2003, `CITATIONS.md` 33, banded in section 11b, carried without a number; that the pattern then resumes is Depner et al. 2019, `CITATIONS.md` 37, banded in sections 12 and 13. Where the weekend falls on the row, mornings six and seven, is the figure's week and a drawing device; it is not Belenky's design, which recovered after seven nights, and it is not quite Depner's, which recovered after five. The depth of the drop and the rate of the climb are drawings of "not to the bottom" and "kept growing".

The small clock. Under "and their body clock had shifted later" in section 13, a small clock face in ink at fourteen percent beside her head, drawn earlier with the figure and still until now, slides to the right by about its own width, two seconds. The stage note places this in section 12 and the cue sheet follows it; it is moved here to the sentence that says it, inside the same band, 37, and the editor should carry the move into `CUES.md` or send it back. The slide is a drawing of "later" and is not hours; the clock has no hands and no numbers.

Two links are drawn in D3 and neither is spoken. From her crown a single hair grows, M6's hair at its root: absent on the first morning, a few pixels longer on each morning after, so that by the fourteenth it is a short arc off the crown, far short of M6's first tick, which is not drawn. The growth per day is a drawing, not centimetres. And on one weekday morning of the first week, the fourth as drawn, M2's swab, the plain stick and tip, path for path, enters her mouth and leaves, one and a half seconds, in ink, with no red dot, because the frame's red is spent on the gap and because the sample belongs to the other episode's frame. The morning is provisional: the sample is the morning after the argument in the memory episode, and when that episode fixes its night, the swab moves to the morning after it.

D3's end frame is the row of fourteen, the step line climbed, the flat line under it, the red gap at its widest, the clock slid right, the hair a short arc. The gauge itself is Process S of `RESEARCH.md` section 2 and is never labelled; it is a drawing of pressure that accumulates, and the frame says nothing about what the gauge measures.

## D4. The count. Sections 18 to 20, about 85 seconds on screen, 15 seconds of drawing

D4 begins on the first word of section 18 and holds through section 20. It is M1's standing figure at M5's 0.72, in profile at the left, facing right, drawn in four seconds under the printed question that opens section 18. Under "Seven nights", seven short empty columns are drawn to her right, standing on M2's rule at fourteen percent, at the spacing of D3's days so that they stand under the same seven as D3's first week and the weekend's two are the two where D3's needle dropped, three seconds. Each column is two upright strokes and no top. Nothing else is drawn before the count. The host takes up her paper.

Section 19 is seven nights at the same even pace, about four seconds of speech and three of silence each. In each night, inside the four seconds of speech, one column fills: a short horizontal tick appears across the column at the wanted height as the host says "Wanted", and the column's got bar rises to its height as she says "Got", and the pair lands beside it in the mono, 6 / 7.5 on the five weeknights, 9 / 7.5 on the two weekend nights, each in about a second. On the weekend nights the bar rises past the tick. Nothing else on the frame moves, and nothing moves in the silences; the silence is the viewer's.

Section 20. After "That's your week, in hours" and its four seconds of silence, and after the host has read her own difference, under "four and a half short for the week" the balance appears beneath the columns in the mono, −4.5, by the site's one transition, 400 milliseconds, and the minus sign is the one red thing on the frame. That is the end frame: seven columns filled, the weekend's two overshooting their ticks, and the week's balance beneath.

D4 carries no sourced value. Its fourteen numbers are the composite's, from script sections 19 and 20, and the composite is declared in section 14; the balance is their arithmetic, which the host does aloud. The columns, the ticks and the bars are a drawing of two spoken numbers per night and claim nothing about anyone's sleep. One thing for the author: seven and a half is the composite's wanted figure and a viewer could read it as the episode's replacement for eight; the piece labels the tick with the word wanted in the serif once, at the first night, and with nothing else, and the floor of seven from section 26 is not drawn, because D4 has left by then. The continuity link is the figure and the days: the same body as M1 and M5, and the seven columns standing where D3's week stood.

## For the scriptwriter and the editor

D3's clock slides under section 13's sentence rather than section 12's, within the same band. D1 carries 28 h, which is in `CITATIONS.md` 48 and in the spoken script but not in the body of `RESEARCH.md`, whose sections 2 to 7 hold no Kleitman value; if the fact-checker wants the drawn values held to `RESEARCH.md` alone, the bracket loses its number and the stretch is shown by the waves alone. D4's wanted tick at seven and a half is the composite's and may read as a rule.
