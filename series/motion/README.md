# Motion pieces for episode 1

Eight short motion graphics for the cortisol episode, made 17 October 2026 after the author asked for the mechanics and parts of the body shown in simple sketches with a few words, instead of static cards. Each is a self-contained HTML file with inline SVG animated by CSS, no library, 1920 by 1080 on the cream ground, that plays once from a still state and holds its end frame. Line weight is 3px at frame width. Words are set in the serif, anything measured in the mono, and the labels in the condensed caps, as `IDENTITY.md` fixes; cover red appears on at most one thing per frame. Every number drawn comes from `episodes/01-cortisol/RESEARCH.md` and the research source is named below for each. A curve's shape between its stated points is a drawing, not a measurement, and the README says so where that applies. The pieces are keyed to section numbers in `episodes/01-cortisol/SCRIPT.md`, which are stable while the sentences are being reworded.

M1, M2, M3, M4, M5 and M6 are in their second pass, made later the same day. The author's verdict on the first was that she had asked for the mechanics and the parts of the body in simple sketches, and had been given graphs, with the one body drawing a head and a gland floating under it, anatomically nowhere; and that the line drawing itself on, used everywhere, read as generic. The second pass puts the mechanism on a body. The body is the line figure of `setup-sketch.svg`, the host as the camera sees her: a faceless head on a plain neck and shoulder line, and no more. Each organ is drawn where it is. The figure is the same across the pieces: M3 reuses M2's lying figure path for path, M5 and M6 reuse M1's standing figure, M5 at 0.72 and M6 its head alone at five times, with the stroke held at 3px on the frame in both. The first-pass files are kept beside them for the record as `M1-v1.html`, `M2-v1.html`, `M3-v1.html`, `M4-v1.html`, `M5-v1.html` and `M6-v1.html`; their renders were overwritten and are not kept. M7 and M8 are type only and have no body to put a mechanism on; they stand as made.

## The pieces

M1, the chain, thirteen seconds, for section 6. A standing figure in profile, facing left, full height in the left half of the frame, is drawn in the order a hand would draw it: head, neck, back, front, arm, legs, with a pause between each. Inside the head, two marks are set at the base of the brain, the hypothalamus and the pituitary hanging under it, each with its label in the mono and a leader in ink at fourteen percent. At the mid-back, at waist height, the two kidneys are drawn one a little behind the other, and each is capped by its adrenal gland, with the label adrenal. A single red dot then travels from the hypothalamus past the pituitary down through the body to the gland; no line is drawn under it, because the message to the adrenal goes by blood, not by nerve. The word cortisol appears beside the gland in the serif, and a thin line is drawn from the gland back up to the hypothalamus, ending in a small chevron, for the feedback. The chain and its feedback on the first two glands are as Ulrich-Lai and Herman (2009) set them out in `RESEARCH.md` section 2. The positions are a sketch of where the organs are, not an anatomical plate: two kidneys in profile cannot both be seen, so they are drawn side by side; the pituitary stalk is a device. Nothing on the frame is a measurement.

M2, a day, thirteen seconds, for section 7. The 24-hour axis in the mono is drawn across the top, marked 06, 12, 18 and 24. Under it, at the foot of the frame, the same figure is drawn lying on its back on a rule at fourteen percent, head to the left, under the night hours. At six it sits up: the torso turns about the hip in one movement, and the legs stay where they are. Then the day is drawn above it, left to right, as one stroke: the daily curve, high in the morning and low at night, with the hourly pulses riding on it. Then a swab, a plain stick with a cotton tip, is drawn in from the right and enters the mouth. The red dot appears at the tip, leaves the mouth and lands on the curve at half past six, on the steep morning rise, and the words one sample appear in the serif. The sample is taken from the body; the chart grows out of what the body did. The shape is the circadian rhythm with an ultradian pulse roughly every hour that Lightman and Conway-Campbell (2010) describe in `RESEARCH.md` section 2. The only measured things on the frame are the hours on the axis; the height of the curve, the size of the pulses and the hour of the sample are a drawing of that description, not data.

M3, the first thirty minutes after waking, twelve seconds, for the second half of section 7. M2's figure again, lying on its rule at the foot of the frame, drawn head, neck, back, front, arm, legs; it sits up, which is the waking. Then beside it, to the right, a short axis in the mono from 0 to 30, labelled minutes after waking, and the waking level carried across the axis as a rule at fourteen percent, at the height of the mouth. The curve leaves the mouth, runs along that level to the axis's zero and rises to its top at thirty minutes, and as it reaches the top the figure 50 to 75 percent lands above it in the mono, in ink. Nothing on the frame is red. The figure is Pruessner et al. (1997) as `RESEARCH.md` section 2 states it: free cortisol rises by roughly fifty to seventy-five percent in the thirty minutes after waking. The minutes on the axis are the only measured thing; the height of the rise and its shape between the two ends are a drawing, and that the curve comes out of the mouth is the device of M2, the sample taken from the body, not a statement about where cortisol is made.

M4, the two clocks, fourteen seconds, for section 9. The figure seated in profile, facing the clock, on a chair drawn as a rule at fourteen percent: the heart is drawn in the chest behind the breastbone, and the two kidneys at the back at waist height, each capped by its adrenal, as M1 places them, with the label adrenal outside the back. Then stressor begins appears in the serif, and at the right, in the mono at HEAD, a clock reading 0:00. The heart jumps, five quick beats, and a pulse line leaves the chest to the right: tall and quick at first, then smaller and slower, settled well before the twentieth minute; it is revealed at the clock's own rate, so where it is on the frame is when it is on the clock. The glands stay still until the clock reads about twenty minutes; then a slow line leaves the back, passes under the seat behind her, and climbs to join the same timeline, where it peaks and comes down. As it passes its peak the band in ink at fourteen percent is laid over the window from 21 to 40 minutes on the clock's scale, labelled 21 to 40 min in the mono, and the clock runs on to 60:00. The two lines come from two organs, not from an axis; there is no axis on the frame. The window and the two timings are Dickerson and Kemeny (2004) as `RESEARCH.md` sections 2 and 3 state them: the autonomic response comes in seconds, cortisol is the slower second wave, and it peaks in saliva twenty-one to forty minutes after the stressor begins. Everything else is a drawing: the wiggle of the pulse line is "faster, then settled" and is not a heart rate; the heart's five beats are a gesture; the cortisol line's height, the moment it leaves the gland, its route around the body, its rate of climb and its fall are not data, and only the band is placed on the clock's scale. M4 was redrawn a third time the same day: the front-on figure with the kidneys shown through the belly was replaced by this profile with the glands at the back, and that front-on version is kept as `M4-v2.html`.

M5, chronic stress, fourteen seconds, for section 10. M1's standing figure twice, side by side, each at 0.72 and drawn in M1's order with its two kidneys and adrenals at the mid-back. On the left, under the word near onset in the serif, a day curve rises high out of the right adrenal, peaks in the morning and comes down to the gland's height at the right. Then a pause, and on the right, under persisting, the same figure is drawn again a little more slumped, the head lower and forward and the upper back rounder, and a lower, flatter day curve leaves the same gland. Nothing on the frame is red and there is no axis. The relation between the two curves is Miller, Chen and Zhou (2007) as `RESEARCH.md` section 3 states it: output is highest near the onset of a stressor and declines as it persists. The curves carry no values: their height, breadth and shape are a drawing of "higher" and "lower, flatter". The slump is a drawing device for "persisting" and claims nothing about posture, mood or the body under chronic stress; the kidneys at the mid-back are a sketch of where they are, as in M1.

M6, hair and saliva, ten and a half seconds, for section 14. M1's head in profile, facing left, at five times its size, filling the frame's height, with the neck and the shoulder line running off the bottom. From the crown a single hair is drawn, long, arching up and to the right and down to the frame's right third; three short ticks are set across it with 1, 2 and 3 in the mono beside them, and the word months in the serif at its end. Then from the mouth a drop is drawn, in red, the only red thing on the frame, because the drop is what a kit measures, with the word minutes beneath. That hair cortisol integrates months is Stalder et al. (2017) as `RESEARCH.md` section 3 states it. The three marks are a drawing device: they say "months", not centimetres per month, and claim nothing about the length of hair that holds a month. The hair's length and curve are a drawing; the ticks are placed on the hair by a short script at three fractions of its length. The head's path is M1's with its last point brought up to its first, because the 2px seam M1 leaves on its head would read as a break at this size.

M7, the three questions, ten seconds, for section 23. The three questions that page 9 of the book prints, in the form the script prints them, each with its name, drawn in one at a time: Time, then Attention, then Safety, the name entering first and its question half a second after. Everything is in the serif at LEAD and in ink. Nothing else is on the frame. The words are the script's exactly; the straight quotation marks of the script's source file are set as typographic quotation marks on screen.

M8, the verdict, six seconds, for sections 2 and 21. The verdict card as motion, in the TEXT layout of `PRODUCTION.md`: THE VERDICT in the condensed caps in the label column, and the nine words A cortisol level says little about a person's stress. in the serif at DISPLAY, entering one word at a time. The word level turns red last, at four seconds and a fifth, and is the only red thing on the frame.

## How the motion is made

Every mark is a path with `pathLength` set to 1, drawn by animating its dash offset from 1 to 0, so a line is seen being made from its start to its end. In the first-pass pieces that drawing runs at a steady speed. In the second-pass pieces, M1 to M6, it runs at a hand's pace, easing in and out of each stroke, with a pause between strokes and in the order a hand would draw them: the body first, then the organ, then the signal; one thing moves at a time. The same pieces give the line its hand-drawn quality with one SVG filter on the drawing group, a slow fractal noise through a displacement map at scale 2.2, which moves every stroke by about a pixel either way and no more, so that at 1080p a line is irregular the way a pen line is and never wobbles. The filter has a fixed seed, so a render is the same every time. Where a stroke is long and smooth, as on M6's large head, the displacement thins the line here and there by a pixel; that is the pen quality, not a fault. Type is kept outside the filtered group and stays crisp. The paths themselves are also written a little off true: no circle is a `circle`. Where a figure is scaled, in M5 and M6, the stroke width inside the scaled group is divided by the scale so that the line is 3px on the frame.

Everything that appears rather than being drawn uses the site's one transition, opacity and an 8px rise over 400ms. The travelling dots in M1 and M2 are HTML elements on CSS motion paths. The figure in M2 and M3 sits up by one CSS rotation of the torso group about the hip. In M4 the pulse line is revealed by a clip rectangle that widens at the clock's rate, so that the line's place on the frame is its time on the clock; the clock's digits are written by a short script that reads the current time of the clock's own CSS animation on every frame, which is why a page the renderer has paused and seeked shows the right digits as well as a page playing on its own. All other timing is CSS; a `motion:duration` meta tag in each file states the piece's length in milliseconds, and the renderer reads it.

## Fonts

Each file declares the design system's faces, Instrument Serif, IBM Plex Mono and Barlow Semi Condensed, from Google Fonts, with real fallbacks behind them: Liberation Serif, DejaVu Sans Mono and Liberation Sans, which are installed on the rendering machine. In the render of 17 October 2026 the Google faces did arrive through the proxy and were used, and the renderer prints which faces loaded for each piece. If a future render cannot reach Google Fonts, the fallbacks draw instead; the fallback serif is wider than Instrument Serif, so M7 and M8 should be checked for line breaks after such a render. The Adobe faces of `IDENTITY.md` are not involved; these pieces are made again from them when the kit is available.

## Rendering

The renderer is `render.mjs` in this folder. From the repository root:

    node series/motion/render.mjs
    node series/motion/render.mjs M2 M5

renders every piece, or only the ones named, to `M1.mp4` and so on beside the HTML, at 1920 by 1080, 25 frames a second, H.264 with yuv420p at CRF 18, and writes a poster PNG of each piece's end frame as `M1.png` and so on. It needs the global Playwright 1.56 with Chromium under `/opt/pw-browsers` and ffmpeg on the path, and installs nothing.

Frames are captured at a fixed timestep. The renderer pauses every animation on the page the moment the document is parsed, then for each frame sets every animation's current time to the frame's time, waits two animation frames so that Chromium has painted the change, and screenshots. Nothing runs on the browser's own clock, so a render is the same on any machine however slow it is, and a page that would stutter if captured in real time does not. The last frame of each file is the end state, held; the MP4 is one frame longer than the stated duration for that reason.

When the script changes, edit the words in the HTML, keep the section numbers in this file and in the files' description tags in step, and re-render.
